\hwhat{H41 asks whether a novel item (a link, number, name or rare word) can reach an agent before that agent's model calls could read it. The test traces 134k novel marker items (45k adoptions) through the DQ1 context ledger in 32 periods. Within a room the logged light cone holds: adoptions outside it are 0--4\% per period (median 0.6\%). The gating jump at the first reading call passes in 10--12/32 periods as pre-registered and in 22--24/32 when matched for time since the item appeared (post hoc). Across rooms the logged cone is a cage (75--100\% of cross-room adoptions outside it in 7/8 two-room periods; the NE42 merge raises cross-group pickup 80--860$\times$). Round 2 time-stamps the leaks with file-level git history and URL fetches. Cross-room adopters read the source's fresh artifacts far more than same-moment non-adopters, but as much after their own use as before: the ordered excess is only $\Lambda=1.45$ [1.09, 2.26], and it rests on two periods. Pooled with period strata, regime-III numbers gate ($J_{mh,D}=3.2$); only \#40 co-generates them.}

\hmodel{Model 02 gives asynchronous kinetic updates, and model 03 gives contagion on a temporal graph. Agents update only at their model calls. A call reads the room messages posted since the previous call (the ledger). An item that source $s$ posts at $t_0$ can reach agent $i$ only along a time-respecting read-out path. $K_i$ is $i$'s entry call into this logged cone. The adoption hazard per talk call is a shared field $\varepsilon$, plus unlogged channels $\eta$, plus $q$ once $i$ has read the item. $J$ compares the hazard at the first entry call with the call in flight at $t_0$.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
T_s=t_0,\quad T_i=\min\{t^{\rm call}_c:\ c\ \text{reads}\ m,\ t_m\ge T_{{\rm src}(m)}\}\\
\lambda_i(c)=\varepsilon(t_c)+\eta_i(c)+q\,\mathbb{1}[c\ge K_i^{\rm item}]\\
\text{acausal: } c_{\rm use}<K_i,\qquad J=\frac{h(c=K_i)}{h(c=K_i-1)}\Big|_{\text{same }t-t_0}\\
v=1/\mathrm{median}\,(\text{calls from exposure to use})
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{Cross-room leaks follow project membership. In \#51 a cross-room adopter is almost always an agent that already reads the source's repos (75\% vs 3\%), before and after the use. To predict where an item leaks, look at who works on the source's artifacts, not at who fetched them just before. Fixed rooms still cage chat: merging two groups multiplies cross-group pickup about a hundredfold, and hopping rooms leak about half the time. Inside a room, names and numbers both gate at the reading call (regime III, $J_{mh}$ 4.8 and 3.2). A dashboard-style common stimulus does not explain the in-flight adoptions. Spread runs at one hop per 1.5--5 talk turns, so call cadence is not a lever.}

\hbottom{Items obey a logged light cone in rooms; cross-room leaks follow shared projects, with only a small, fragile ordered-read excess ($\Lambda=1.45$).}
