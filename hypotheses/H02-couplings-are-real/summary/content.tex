\hwhat{The spins are the 1-min activity states of the agents. A directed kinetic-Ising fit gives couplings in 21 non-holdout 5-day chunks (\#10--\#44), with block fields and surrogate nulls. In synthetic villages the pipeline recovers edges (AUC 0.95) and finds a leader with outgoing $J\ge1.5$ in 90--100\% of runs. On real data the pairwise couplings sit at the null floor in both goal modes. The fit misses \#26's elected leader. \textbf{The locked holdout \#45 fails}: the Fine-Tuned Leader ranks 4/18 at $z=0.76$. \textbf{Round 1b} corrects the minute grid, which dropped half of all events, and trims day edges in the nulls. The pairwise couplings stay null. The round-1 collective coupling in regime III is the operator's day edges: it is significant in 2/8 regime-III chunks, against 7/13 in regime I. The fit also misses \#12's debate teams and \#44's installed leader. Post hoc, talk-spin couplings transfer across days in regime-III shared-objective weeks.}

\hmodel{Model 02 (kinetic Ising) is primary; model 01 (equilibrium Ising) is the rival. The spin $s_i(t)=\pm1$ says whether agent $i$ acts or talks in 1-min bin $t$. $h_i$ is a bias. The field $\delta_i(b)$ per (day, 30-min block) absorbs the schedule and kickoffs. The coupling $J_{ij}$ is the lagged influence of $j$ on $i$; $\sigma$ is the logistic function. $I_k$ is the net outgoing influence. In mean field, $\beta J_0$ is the uniform coupling, $m$ the mean spin and $q$ the mean spin variance. VR is the variance of $\sum_i s_i$ over its independent value.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
P\big(s_i(t{+}1)=+1\mid\mathbf s(t)\big)=\sigma\big(2H_i(t)\big)\\
H_i(t)=h_i+\delta_i(b)+\textstyle\sum_{j}J_{ij}\,s_j(t)\\
I_k=\textstyle\sum_{j\neq k}\big(J_{jk}-J_{kj}\big)\\
N\,\mathrm{Var}(m)=q/(1-\beta J_0q)\;\Rightarrow\;\beta J_0=(1-1/\mathrm{VR})/q
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{An inverse-Ising fit to activity timing does not identify who leads an agent swarm. Per-pair couplings from activity timing are noise. They miss a leader that the operators installed twice (\#45 and \#44), and they miss a debate's team structure. The collective co-activation that looked like coupling is mostly the operator's daily start and stop. Before you compute any synchrony statistic, trim each day to the all-present window. Without the trim almost every regime-III chunk looks coupled; with it, 2/8 do. To find which agent steers a swarm, measure who talks after whom. That pairwise signal survives, consistent with H50's read-out-gated talk coupling.}

\hbottom{Activity-timing Ising couplings do not measure influence: they sit at the null floor, miss installed leaders, and the collective term is the daily schedule.}
