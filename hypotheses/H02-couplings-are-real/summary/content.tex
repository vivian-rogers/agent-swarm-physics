\hwhat{Directed kinetic-Ising couplings were fitted to 1-min activity spins in 21 non-holdout 5-day chunks (\#10--\#44), with block fields and surrogate nulls. Synthetic villages show the pipeline works (edge AUC 0.95; a leader with outgoing $J\ge1.5$ found 90--100\% of the time). On real data, pairwise couplings sit at the null floor in both goal modes, and \#26's elected leader is not found. \textbf{Locked holdout \#45 failed} (Fine-Tuned Leader 4/18, $z=0.76$). \textbf{Round 1b} (corrected minute grid, which had dropped half of all events; day-edge-trimmed nulls): pairwise couplings are still null. The regime-III collective coupling of round 1 was the operator's day edges (significant in 2/8 regime-III chunks against 7/13 in regime I). \#12's debate structure and \#44's installed leader are invisible too. Post hoc, talk-spin couplings transfer across days in regime-III shared-objective weeks.}

\hmodel{Model 02 (kinetic Ising, primary); model 01 (equilibrium Ising) is the rival. Spin $s_i(t)=\pm1$: agent $i$ active (acts or talks) or not in 1-min bin $t$. $h_i$: bias; $\delta_i(b)$: field per (day, 30-min block), absorbing schedule and kickoffs; $J_{ij}$: lagged influence of $j$ on $i$; $\sigma$: logistic; $I_k$: net outgoing influence. Mean field: uniform coupling $\beta J_0$; $m$: mean spin; $q$: mean spin variance; VR: variance of $\sum_i s_i$ over its independent value.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
P\big(s_i(t{+}1)=+1\mid\mathbf s(t)\big)=\sigma\big(2H_i(t)\big)\\
H_i(t)=h_i+\delta_i(b)+\textstyle\sum_{j}J_{ij}\,s_j(t)\\
I_k=\textstyle\sum_{j\neq k}\big(J_{jk}-J_{kj}\big)\\
N\,\mathrm{Var}(m)=q/(1-\beta J_0q)\;\Rightarrow\;\beta J_0=(1-1/\mathrm{VR})/q
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{A useful negative, now on clean data. Fitting an Ising model to who is active when, and reading off who leads, is the first thing a physicist tries on agent logs, and it does not work. Per-pair couplings from activity timing are noise, they missed a leader the operators installed (twice: \#45 and \#44), and they do not see a debate's team structure. Even the collective co-activation that looked like coupling is mostly the operator starting and stopping everyone each day. Anyone looking for which agent steers a swarm should look at messages: the only pairwise signal that survives is in who talks after whom, which fits H50's read-out-gated talk coupling. Mostly this tells you what not to compute.}

\hbottom{Inverse-Ising couplings from minute-level activity do not measure influence in an agent swarm: on corrected data they stay at the null floor, miss installed leaders and debate teams, and the apparent collective coupling is the operator's daily schedule.}

\hcaveats{The corrected null still leaks under start-up bursts (DQ8 size 0.51 for day-edge drives after trimming); H38's agent-state conditioning agrees with it here. Trimming keeps 33--99\% of minutes (\#44 only 33\%). The \#45 run used the buggy table; the thinning check draws day retention from non-holdout regime-III days, not \#45's own. Native tests are small: 295 debate-minutes for \#12, two days and five agents for 44b. The talk-spin result is post hoc. The synthetic harness was not re-run with the corrected calibration (active 0.67, $J_{\rm self}$ 0.49).}
