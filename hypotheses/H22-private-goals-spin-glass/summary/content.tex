\hwhat{Do the private, partly conflicting roles of \#51 make the swarm a spin glass? Units 51a--e (45 non-holdout days) are tested against the shared-objective weeks \#38, \#40, \#44. Content couplings are real but mostly positive, positions persist without collective switching ($W\approx1$), and same-role rivals co-move \emph{more} than unrelated pairs ($T_{\rm SR}$ +0.04 to +0.06 in every round-1b variant, $p\le0.05$). DQ2 stance is ferromagnetic. \textbf{Round 1c} uses the validated v2.1 disagreement flag with the labeller's confusion built into the null. Rivals are not more disagreeing ($+0.49$~pp $[-0.68,+1.67]$), but the flag is too rare to decide (power 0.47 at odds ratio 3). A read vs in-flight contrast shows that reading a rival pulls content \emph{toward} it ($\Gamma=+0.19$ $[+0.07,+0.31]$). The holdout is \textbf{not run}.}

\hmodel{Spin glass (01, SK) of O($n$) spins (11). Spin $\mathbf s_i$: the whitened chat embedding of agent $i$ per 30-min window ($n=32$). Fields: day field $\mathbf m_d$; static field $\mathbf H_i$ (role, lab, style). $J_{ij}$: correlation of the within-day fluctuations $\mathbf x_i,\mathbf x_j$ minus a cross-day surrogate. $A,B,C$: day folds. $\kappa$: SK ratio $J_0/J$. $\tau_3$: gauge-invariant balance (SK $\approx0$, factions $\approx0.5$). $q$: overlap of day-demeaned states $\hat{\boldsymbol\delta}$. $W$: its synchrony beyond per-agent shifts.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\{\mathbf s\})\propto\exp\big(\textstyle\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\,\mathbf s_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf s_j+\sum_i(\mathbf m_d+\mathbf H_i)\!\cdot\!\mathbf s_i\big)\\
J_{ij}=\operatorname{corr}_w(\mathbf x_i,\mathbf x_j)-\operatorname{corr}^{\rm surr}_w(\mathbf x_i,\mathbf x_j)\\
\kappa=\sqrt N\,\bar J/\sigma_J,\qquad \tau_3=\operatorname{tr}(J^AJ^BJ^C)/\operatorname{tr}(J^AJ^B)^{3/2}\\
q(d,d')=\tfrac1N\textstyle\sum_i\hat{\boldsymbol\delta}_{i,d}\!\cdot\!\hat{\boldsymbol\delta}_{i,d'}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{Little changes in practice. Assigned roles act as fixed fields that pin each agent's topic. The shared room adds a weak positive coupling, and same-objective agents drift together. To steer a mixed-motive swarm, edit the role prompts: roles, not inter-agent conflict, set what agents talk about. Expect no hidden collective states to escape ($W\approx1$). Embeddings measure topic, not stance, so strategic opposition could stay invisible in content. The diagnostics port to other logs; the spin-glass vocabulary is not earned.}

\hbottom{\#51 is not a spin glass: roles act as random fields with weak positive coupling, rivals align, and the swarm never switches collectively.}

\hcaveats{$\tau_3^{\rm dc}$ cannot separate SK from a heterogeneous ferromagnet (Amendment 1), so the unbalanced stance residuals are not glass evidence. Per-pair negative-stance counts assume independent replies within a pair, so threads inflate them. They are not enriched in conflict classes. Each unit has few testable rival pairs (3--5; one in most shared splits). The NE38 coupling result uses a threshold chosen after seeing the coverage. DQ2 stance labels were reliable only in aggregate (pair-level ``opposes'' precision 0.08). The v2.1 flag is validated but rare (about 4 expected rival flags per unit), and its synthetic size is 0.11 in 51d. The $\Gamma$ excess over random pair sets is suggestive only (percentile 0.73--0.95).}
