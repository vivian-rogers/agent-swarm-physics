\hwhat{A pre-registered rule on project labels defined herding onsets: a project's share of labeled agents jumps from $\le 0.25$ to $\ge 0.5$ within an hour and stays there. It found 21 onsets in 14 non-holdout weeks at 15-min resolution, mostly in shared-artifact weeks. Early-warning trends (rolling lag-1 autocorrelation and SD over the previous 6\,h) were compared with matched placebo segments; 8 onsets were evaluable. The composite separated them weakly at 15\,min (AUC 0.62) and not at 30\,min; autocorrelation never rose (0.41), so this is not critical slowing down. \textbf{Round 1b} (deterministic labels, work ledger) reproduces all of it: same onsets, same AUC. The frozen alarm now beats a time-shifted copy at $p=0.038$ (was 0.07), but a naive share alarm matches its 5/21 hits with fewer alarms. Work-commit onsets coincide with attention onsets, and \#40's pile-on ran in minutes after a link. Holdout not run.}

\hmodel{Models 10 (kinetic mean-field Potts) and 01 (mean-field susceptibility). $\sigma_i$: agent $i$'s project in a window (0 = rare projects, uncoupled). $k_a^{(-i)}$: other agents on $a$; $h_a(t)$: a slowly drifting popularity field; $\beta J$: uniform herding coupling; $\gamma$: reconsideration rate; $x_a$: share on $a$. Near a fold of $x_A$ the leading eigenvalue $\lambda\to 0$, so the lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho_1$ and the variance rise (critical slowing down). Rivals (field steps, noise-induced flips) give no warning.}

\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\sigma_i\!\to\! a)\propto e^{\,h_a(t)+\beta J k_a^{(-i)}/(N-1)}\ (a\ge 1),\quad \propto 1\ (a=0)\\
\dot x_a=\gamma\big[\operatorname{softmax}_a(h(t)+\beta J x)-x_a\big]\\
\rho_1=e^{-\gamma|\lambda|\Delta t}\to 1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}x_A\propto 1/(N|\lambda|)\quad(\lambda\to 0)\\
\text{alarm: }\tau_K(\hat\rho_1)>\tau^*\ \wedge\ \tau_K(\hat\sigma)>\tau^*,\quad \tau^*=0.54
\end{gather*}\vspace{-10pt}}

\htelos{For an operator this is a useful negative, now checked on cleaner data and in commits. Generic early-warning indicators (rising autocorrelation and variance) do not forecast pile-ons in a 10--15-agent LLM swarm. Pile-ons are fast pushes: a link posted in chat, then half the room within minutes (\#40: 4 min to half the room's attention, 19 min to half its commits). A 6-hour trend window cannot see that, and at $N\approx 12$ a true fold would be barely detectable anyway. So don't build a critical-slowing-down dashboard for swarms this size. Watch announcements and shares instead, and size any response to minutes, not hours. The transferable lesson: test alarm timing against a rate-matched shift; the round-1 vs 1b $p$ (0.07 vs 0.04) shows how thin such timing signals are.}

\hbottom{Critical slowing down does not warn of herding in the AI Village: pile-ons in attention and in commits are fast, usually marked by a link, and too few agents are watched for slowing down to show.}
