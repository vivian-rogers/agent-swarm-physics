\hwhat{A pre-registered rule on H11's project labels defined herding onsets: a project's share of labeled agents jumps from $\le 0.25$ to $\ge 0.5$ within an hour and stays there. It found 21 onsets in 14 non-holdout weeks at 15-min resolution, mostly in shared-artifact weeks (\#18, \#19, \#30, \#31, \#33, \#38). Early-warning trends (Kendall $\tau$ of the rolling lag-1 autocorrelation and SD over the previous 6\,h) were compared with matched placebo segments. Only 8 onsets were evaluable. The composite separated them weakly at 15\,min (AUC 0.62 [0.51, 0.76]) and not at 30\,min (0.44). Autocorrelation never rose (AUC 0.42), so this is not critical slowing down. The frozen alarm warned of 5 of 21 onsets at 5.3 false alarms per active day, no better than a time-shifted alarm ($p=0.07$) or a naive share alarm. Four onsets came out of nowhere. Post hoc, 11 of 21 followed a chat link to the project within 30\,min ($p=0.0005$). Holdout not run.}

\hmodel{Models 10 (kinetic mean-field Potts) and 01 (mean-field susceptibility). $\sigma_i$: agent $i$'s project in a window (0 = rare projects, uncoupled). $k_a^{(-i)}$: other agents on $a$; $h_a(t)$: a slowly drifting popularity field; $\beta J$: uniform herding coupling; $\gamma$: reconsideration rate; $x_a$: share on $a$. Near a fold of $x_A$ the leading eigenvalue $\lambda\to 0$, so the lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho_1$ and the variance rise (critical slowing down). Rivals (field steps, noise-induced flips) give no warning.}

\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\sigma_i\!\to\! a)\propto e^{\,h_a(t)+\beta J k_a^{(-i)}/(N-1)}\ (a\ge 1),\quad \propto 1\ (a=0)\\
\dot x_a=\gamma\big[\operatorname{softmax}_a(h(t)+\beta J x)-x_a\big]\\
\rho_1=e^{-\gamma|\lambda|\Delta t}\to 1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}x_A\propto 1/(N|\lambda|)\quad(\lambda\to 0)\\
\text{alarm: }\tau_K(\hat\rho_1)>\tau^*\ \wedge\ \tau_K(\hat\sigma)>\tau^*,\quad \tau^*=0.54
\end{gather*}\vspace{-10pt}}

\htelos{For an operator this is a useful negative. Generic early-warning indicators (rising autocorrelation and variance, popular in ecology and finance) do not forecast pile-ons in a 10--15-agent LLM swarm logged at 15-min resolution. Most pile-ons are fast pushes: a project nobody had touched, or a link posted in chat, then a majority within the hour. The synthetic runs show it would not help even if the physics were right: at $N\approx 12$ a true fold is barely detectable. So don't build a critical-slowing-down dashboard for swarms this size. If you want warning, watch announcements (links; H28 is testing this) or just watch shares, and accept about two false alarms a day. The transferable lesson: test alarm timing against a rate-matched shift, or every indicator looks predictive.}

\hbottom{Critical slowing down does not warn of herding in the AI Village: pile-ons are fast, often announced by a link, and too few agents are watched for slowing down to show.}

\hcaveats{21 onsets, 8 evaluable: the per-week AUCs rest on 1--2 onsets and the CIs are wide. Many indicators, leads, window lengths and two onset rules were computed; only the 15-min composite at 1\,h was primary. Labels are artifact mentions (attention, not work) and are sparse in regime I. The step rule misses slow rises (synthetic $N=200$); the slow-rise variant gives 0.73 at 15\,min but 0.50 at 30\,min. The link association is post hoc and partly mechanical, since a link also labels its poster. \#51 supplies most of the false alarms. Leads count from the earliest alarm in the horizon, which flatters noisy alarms.}
