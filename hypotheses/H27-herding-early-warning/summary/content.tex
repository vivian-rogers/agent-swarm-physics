\hwhat{A pre-registered rule on project labels defines a herding onset. A project's share of labeled agents jumps from $\le 0.25$ to $\ge 0.5$ within an hour and stays there. It finds 21 onsets in 14 non-holdout weeks at 15-min resolution, mostly in shared-artifact weeks. We compare early-warning trends (rolling lag-1 autocorrelation and SD over the previous 6\,h) with matched placebo segments; 8 onsets are evaluable. The composite separates them weakly at 15\,min (AUC 0.62) and not at 30\,min. Autocorrelation never rises (AUC 0.41), so this is not critical slowing down. \textbf{Round 1b} (deterministic labels, work ledger) reproduces all of it: the same onsets and the same AUC. The frozen alarm now beats a time-shifted copy at $p=0.038$ (was 0.07). A naive share alarm matches its 5/21 hits with fewer alarms. Work-commit onsets coincide with attention onsets. The \#40 pile-on ran in minutes after a link. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Kinetic mean-field Potts (10) with mean-field susceptibility (01). Spin $\sigma_i$: the project of agent $i$ in a window (0 = rare projects, uncoupled). $k_a^{(-i)}$: other agents on $a$. $h_a(t)$: a slowly drifting popularity field. $\beta J$: uniform herding coupling. $\gamma$: reconsideration rate. Order parameter $x_a$: the share on $a$. Near a fold of $x_A$ the leading eigenvalue $\lambda\to 0$, so the lag-1 autocorrelation $\rho_1$ and the variance rise (critical slowing down). Rivals (field steps, noise-induced flips) give no warning.}

\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\sigma_i\!\to\! a)\propto e^{\,h_a(t)+\beta J k_a^{(-i)}/(N-1)}\ (a\ge 1),\quad \propto 1\ (a=0)\\
\dot x_a=\gamma\big[\operatorname{softmax}_a(h(t)+\beta J x)-x_a\big]\\
\rho_1=e^{-\gamma|\lambda|\Delta t}\to 1,\qquad \operatorname{Var}x_A\propto 1/(N|\lambda|)\quad(\lambda\to 0)\\
\text{alarm: }\tau_K(\hat\rho_1)>\tau^*\ \wedge\ \tau_K(\hat\sigma)>\tau^*,\quad \tau^*=0.54
\end{gather*}\vspace{-10pt}}

\htelos{Do not build a critical-slowing-down dashboard for a 10--15-agent LLM swarm: rising autocorrelation and variance do not forecast its pile-ons. A pile-on is a fast push: a link in chat, then half the room within minutes. In \#40, half the room's attention took 4 min and half its commits 19 min. A 6-hour trend window cannot see that, and at $N\approx 12$ a true fold is barely detectable. Watch announcements and project shares instead, and plan responses in minutes, not hours. Test any alarm against a rate-matched time shift: here $p$ moves from 0.07 to 0.038 between data versions.}

\hbottom{Critical slowing down does not warn of village herding: pile-ons in attention and commits often follow a link within minutes, and $N\approx12$ hides slowing.}

\hcaveats{There are 21 onsets, 8 evaluable and 4 in work, so per-week AUCs rest on 1--2 onsets. We tried many indicators, leads, window lengths and two onset rules; only the 15-min composite at 1\,h was primary. The alarm-vs-shift $p$ crosses 0.05 between null draws (0.038--0.07): treat it as borderline. The step rule misses slow rises; the slow-rise variant gives 0.74 at 15\,min. The link association is post hoc, and most linked onsets are bursts of known projects (H28). Work labels are public agent commits (dense from \#30), and \#51 supplies most false alarms.}
