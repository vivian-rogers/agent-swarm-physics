\hwhat{Starting set D3.1.a (rooms as ordered ``ideological'' units) and D3.2 (coupling vs field), tested on 184,713 embedded chat messages and intentions in 16 non-holdout regime II/III units. Rooms had lower meaning-cluster entropy than random same-size groups on 96\% of 54 days, but the median gap ($-0.098$ nats) \emph{narrowly missed} the locked $-0.1$. The biggest gaps came where rooms got different instructions (\#38: $-0.34$ to $-0.38$), i.e.\ a room field. The exposure slope of residual alignment ($+0.013$ per e-fold) was positive in 12/15 units and beat the rotation null ($p=0.005$), but its random-effects $p=0.019$ missed the required 0.01 and it vanished at $n=16$. Merging rooms (05-04) raised new pairs' alignment by $+0.18$ ($p=0.004$, confounded with a goal change). Failed: field dominance (P5 at null level), day-to-day stability (P3), lab order as style (P2), and weak coupling (P9: median $\beta J_0/n=0.74$). The NE15 holdout test is written but \textbf{not run}.}

\htelos{Rooms make agents talk about the same things, which is obvious in hindsight, and the cleanest cases are rooms given different instructions: the operator's field, not an emergent mind. The coupling residual is tiny and depends on the embedding; nobody would design channels around $+0.013$ cosine per e-fold. The one usable fact is where the shared dynamics live: day-to-day co-fluctuation sits inside rooms (correlation 0.06--0.66 within, about 0 across), so to read or damp herding, look at rooms, not labs. ``Superagent'' is, for now, a word for that. Whether room order is coupling or just a shared room drive is the NE15 holdout's job, and it hasn't run.}

\hbottom{Rooms are measurably more like-minded than random groups, but both locked thresholds were narrowly missed and the order looks more like operator-imposed room fields than an emergent superagent.}
