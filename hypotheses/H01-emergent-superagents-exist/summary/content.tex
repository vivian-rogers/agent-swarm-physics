\hwhat{Round 2 asks whether a group of agents plus its shared artifacts forms an \emph{effective superagent}. Such a group uses Kolchinsky--Wolpert (KW) semantic information to stay viable and holds that information at the group level. The data is the work ledger: 58k strict git/deploy writes in 19 non-holdout units (\#30--\#51). The candidates are artifact crews and behavior-synchrony, co-allocation and reply communities. Rooms and labs are baselines. \textbf{No candidate is an individuality maximum}: crews give Stouffer $Z=+0.06$, and 2--8\% are local maxima. No candidate carries KW semantic information about its environment ($\Delta V_{st}>0$ in 2/19 units, $\Delta V_{obs}>0$ in 0/19). No candidate repairs itself beyond aggregation. Allocation persists across nights ($\Delta C=0.47$) and memory loss (16/17), but single agents out-persist crews. A goal change cuts advancement by 0.39. The holdout tests (NE30, NE24) exist and are \textbf{not run}.}

\hmodel{Model 04 (KW semantic information). The system is a coarse-grained group $G$: members, shared artifacts $R_G$ and channel. The state $x_b$ is binary: $G$ advances $R_G$ in 30-min bin $b$, or not. The environment $y_b$ is the rest of the swarm's writes plus human and automated messages. The viability $V$ is advancement over horizon $\tau$. $p^f$ is KW's coarse-graining scramble of $p(x_0,y_0)$. $A$ is Krakauer et al.'s colonial individuality: self-prediction beyond the environment. The share $\iota=A/H(x')$ and its $z$-scores are project choices, not the paper's. $\Delta C$ is the return to $R_G$ after a night, above a permutation null.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
\Delta V_{st}=V[p]-V[p_X\!\otimes p_Y]=\mathbb E_p\!\big[(1-e^{-\mathrm{PMI}})\,v(x_0,y_0)\big],\ \ |\Delta V|\le\sqrt{I/2}\\
S=\min_f\{I(p^f):V[p^f]\approx V[p]\},\qquad \eta=S/I(X_0;Y_0)\\
\Delta V_{obs}:\ p(x_{t+1}|x_t,y_t)\to p(x_{t+1}|x_t)\\
A_G=I(x_{b+1};x_b\,|\,y_b),\qquad \iota_G=A_G/H(x_{b+1})
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{No group-level mind exists to steer. Coordination on artifacts adds no measurable self-maintenance beyond the members. Each agent returns to its own artifacts after every night's context erasure ($\Delta C=0.47$) and after memory loss (16/17). The operator's goal sets the work rate: a goal change cuts advancement by 0.39. To keep work going across resets, write the work state into the repo. Do not rely on memory or on team culture. The KW values are near zero partly because the ledger counts attempts, not outcomes.}

\hbottom{No group is an effective superagent: one agent plus its own artifact persists, the goal prompt drives it, and grouping adds no measurable agency.}

\hcaveats{No replay exists. A goal change bundles a new instruction with the scramble. The only artifact-store scramble (NE24) is in the holdout. The KW values rest on a coarse Markov kernel, and its bootstrap is anti-conservative under hidden state. The binary state cannot see \emph{which} artifact a group works on. The environment $y$ excludes the scheduler, kickoff and prior fields, so colonial $A$ can still contain field-driven persistence. The ledger counts attempts: it has no failures, no value and no non-git work. The memory-loss evidence is 17 events, mostly in \#51, where private goals can carry continuity. \textbf{Round 1b} repeats the round-1 content tests on improved data with two embedding models. Rooms stay ordered content groups after style removal. Lab order is style. The drafted \#12 teams are not groups. The NE42 merge--split shows that room order follows the channel. Part of the round-1 merge DiD is style.}
