\hmodel{Models 02 (linear response, fluctuation--dissipation) and 09 (Hawkes). $n_i(m)\in\{0,1\}$ says whether agent $i$ is active in minute $m$. A kick (a nudge or a human message) at $m_0$ is a field pulse. $G$ is the matched response, and $r$ is the read-out delay to the target's next model call. $C$ is the activity autocorrelation, $\chi$ the integrated $G$ and $X$ the FD ratio. In the Hawkes fit, $\mu_d b(t)$ is the daily baseline and $\phi$ the talk-triggered kernel. $\eta_c$ counts messages triggered per class-$c$ kick, and $n$ is the branching ratio. In mean field, $K$ is the loop gain and $\tau_0$ the agent time.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
G(\tau)=\mathbb E[n_i(m_0{+}\tau)\mid\text{kick}]-\mathbb E[n_i(m_0{+}\tau)\mid\text{control}]-\delta_d(\tau)\\
G(\tau)\approx\textstyle\sum_r p(r)\,\Delta R(\tau-r),\qquad \text{FDT: }\chi(\tau)=X\,[C(0)-C(\tau)]\\
\lambda(t)=\mu_d b(t)+\textstyle\sum_k\phi(t-t_k)+\sum_c\eta_c\sum_j\gamma_c e^{-\gamma_c(t-t_j)}\\
n=\textstyle\int_0^\infty\phi(\tau)\,d\tau,\qquad \tau=\tau_0/(1-K)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{A nudge acts on the agent it names and on nobody else. It acts at that agent's next model call, so the target's pause schedule sets its lag. A nudge to an agent paused for 10 min does nothing until the agent wakes. Send a nudge to an awake agent: a first nudge buys about 1.4 active minutes, a repeat 0.9. Human messages change what agents say, not how much they work. The physics vocabulary (Green's functions, FDT, effective temperature) mostly shows that a field-on-activity model is wrong. The one reversal test fails in the opposite direction: longer days raise self-excitation. With the nudger off, self-excitation stays the same.}
