\hwhat{Exploratory (non-holdout): a nudge adds 1.54 [0.80, 2.29] active minutes to its named target over 30 min, but only after a 4-min delay; bystanders show nothing. Mentioned agents respond at once to human messages. Goal kickoffs leave activity unchanged. No kick type passes as a linear propagator (dose untestable; superposition confounded by the nudger re-firing). FDT fails in shape: responses come after 5 min, while autocorrelation decays within 1. Kicked activations persist longer than spontaneous ones ($T_{\text{eff}}\approx0.65$), and mean-field Glauber underpredicts decay times 5--26$\times$. Hawkes beats Poisson ($n=0.59$ in regime I, 0.72 in III). \textbf{Locked holdout NE21} (hours $4\to8\to4\to8$ h): \textbf{C1 falsified in the opposite direction}, with $n$ \emph{higher} on 8 h days at 3/3 switches (0.31, 0.67, 0.46, 0.89; ABAB $-0.39$ vs noise 0.42). The NE23 nudger-off manipulation check failed; C2 was inconclusive, C3 untestable.}

\hmodel{Models 02 (linear response, fluctuation--dissipation) and 09 (Hawkes). $n_i(m)\in\{0,1\}$: agent $i$ active in minute $m$. A kick (nudge, human message, kickoff) at $m_0$ is a field pulse; $G$: matched response; $C$: activity autocorrelation; $\chi$: integrated $G$; $X$: FD ratio ($X=1$ at equilibrium). Hawkes: $\mu_d b(t)$ daily baseline, $\phi$ talk-triggered kernel, $\eta_c$ messages triggered per class-$c$ kick, $n$ branching ratio. Mean field: loop gain $K$, agent time $\tau_0$, variance ratio VR.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
G(\tau)=\mathbb E[n_i(m_0{+}\tau)\mid\text{kick}]-\mathbb E[n_i(m_0{+}\tau)\mid\text{control}]\\
\text{FDT: }\chi(\tau)=X\,[C(0)-C(\tau)],\qquad T_{\text{eff}}=1/X\\
\lambda(t)=\mu_d b(t)+\textstyle\sum_k\phi(t-t_k)+\sum_c\eta_c\sum_j\gamma_c e^{-\gamma_c(t-t_j)}\\
n=\textstyle\int_0^\infty\phi(\tau)\,d\tau,\qquad \tau=\tau_0/(1-K),\quad K=1-1/\mathrm{VR}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{The useful part is the plainest. A nudge works, on the agent it names and nobody else, after a lag of minutes, because it lands in a context the agent reads on its next turn. So target nudges and expect the lag, as any scaffold operator might guess. The physics vocabulary (Green's functions, FDT, effective temperature) mostly shows that a field-on-activity model is wrong; it does not supply a better one. The one intervention test, longer days lowering self-excitation, failed in the opposite direction at the edge of weekly noise. If real, longer sessions breed more triggered activity: a sizing hint at best. Goal changes alter what agents do, not how much.}

\hbottom{Nudges and mentions have real, delayed, target-only effects that no linear-field or FDT picture captures, and the one reversal test of how working hours reshape self-excitation was falsified in the opposite direction.}
