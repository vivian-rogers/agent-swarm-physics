\hwhat{HH331 claims that nights demagnetize the swarm: the remanent content field (a finished kickoff, the previous goal's state) falls in steps at night boundaries by a fixed factor per night. H103 compares four clocks (nights, active hours, wall time, context resets) on 30-min agent windows in 31 non-holdout periods, plus a two-time night step in 41 period units, in both embedding models. \textbf{Nights do not demagnetize.} Where the kickoff remanence decays (22/31 periods), active hours fit as well as or better than nights in 17/22 (bge) / 16/22 (gte). At fixed active lag an agent's content is as similar across a night as within a day ($\beta_N=-0.001$ [$-0.023$, $+0.021$]). Across the night the kickoff alignment rises a little. Context resets and the end of the operator's bookends change nothing. The holdout script is written, not run.}

\htelos{For a swarm operator: a night does not reset the swarm's content. What the swarm remembers of a kickoff or of the last goal fades with work done, not with nights or wall time. Shutting the village down overnight neither helps nor hurts that memory, and forcing context erasures inside the day does not move content either. Each morning the agents lean slightly back toward the current goal, which suits goals kept in the prompt. To clear a stale theme, change the goal; waiting overnight does nothing. The result mostly rules out a mechanism; its direct operator value is small.}

\hbottom{The content magnetization decays with active work, not per night; nights add no demagnetizing step and slightly re-orient agents toward the current goal.}
