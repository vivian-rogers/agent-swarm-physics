\hwhat{H10's Legendre test failed on continuous alignment. HH130 proposed a fairer test: treat each agent-window as on-goal or off-goal and ask whether a uniform field (tilt) on the free week predicts the assigned week's occupancy variance and who moves. I ran it on three free $\to$ assigned pairs, 16 kickoffs and three natives, in two embedding models. The synthetic came first and showed the tilt test is not identifiable at village sampling: with a true tilt it passes in 30\% of pairs, and in 40\% under a coupling change. The two-state picture holds descriptively. Occupancy rises from the decoy floor (0.02--0.05) to 0.23--0.44, its variance grows as $p(1-p)$ (Spearman 0.86 over 16 kickoffs), and transverse occupancy stays flat. Who moves: inconclusive in 3/3 pairs. Holdout: not run.}

\hmodel{Model 01, forward mean field (Curie--Weiss with agent fields), in 0/1 spins. Spin $\sigma_{i,t}$: agent $i$'s statements in 30-min window $t$ are mostly on-goal (projection on $\hat g$ above the 95th percentile of decoy statements). Field $h_i$: each agent's on-goal tendency. Coupling $J_2$: synchronized switching. Occupancy $p_t$: mean spin. The tilt adds one $\lambda$ to every $h_i$ and keeps $J_2$. $g_2$: two-state loop gain.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\sigma)\propto\exp\Big(\textstyle\sum_i h_i\sigma_i+\frac{J_2}{2N}n^2\Big),\quad n=\textstyle\sum_i\sigma_i\\
\mathrm{Var}(n)=\frac{S}{1-J_2\bar q},\quad S=\textstyle\sum_i p_i(1-p_i),\quad g_2=J_2\bar q\\
\text{tilt: }h_i\to h_i+\lambda\ \Rightarrow\ \rho_V=\ln\frac{V_A^{\rm obs}}{V_A(\lambda,J_2^F)}\approx0
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

The ranks agree ($\rho_s=0.86$), but only 7/16 fall in the band; \#38a sits far below. (b) \#51: loop gain of each agent's spin along its own private goal (filled) and along the shared \#51 direction (open), per unit. Private fields do not synchronize, except the role-onset unit 51a.}

\htelos{Mostly a methods result. The two-state view is the right bookkeeping for a goal: it moves only the on-goal occupancy, from about 0.03 to 0.2--0.45, and leaves other directions alone. But the forecast HH130 wanted, steerability from a free week, cannot be made from village logs with this instrument: free weeks have almost no on-goal windows, so their coupling is unmeasurable, and 2--4 statements per window misclassify the spin. An operator learns nothing new beyond H10 and H54. A monitor builder learns that occupancy measured this way needs a calibrated classifier before any fluctuation--response forecast.}

\hbottom{Goals act on a two-state occupancy whose variance grows as $p(1-p)$, but the free-week tilt forecast is not identifiable at village sampling, so the Legendre question stays open.}
