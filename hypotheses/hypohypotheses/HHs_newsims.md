# Hypohypotheses that need new simulations

**Not pursued in this project** (Vivian, 2026-10-04). This project only uses the AI Village record; we can't run new swarms. These ideas need *new agent-swarm runs*: controlled, randomized, replicated. They are kept for a future project.

IDs continue the global HH sequence (append-only; the in-project list in `HYPOHYPOTHESES.md` points here). Format: idea, then *Design* (what to run) and *Measure* (what to compute). "Effort" means the model's reasoning-effort setting (thinking budget).

## A. Reasoning effort as a stat-phys control parameter
*Core conjecture (Vivian): lower reasoning effort makes agents more Markovian and their collective behavior more interpretable in statistical physics. Effort sets the memory kernel, how many interaction orders matter, and how far the dynamics sit from detailed balance.*

- **HH211 · Lower effort → more Markovian behavior.** At low effort, an agent's next action depends on its current state and inputs, not on a long internal plan. Markov-state models should pass the Chapman–Kolmogorov test at shorter lags, and implied timescales should converge sooner. H17 found village behavior non-Markov at minute scale. *Design:* identical swarms at 3–5 effort levels, randomized per agent, same goals and schedule, several replicas each. *Measure:* the lag at which the MSM passes Chapman–Kolmogorov, memory-kernel length from a generalized Langevin fit, and Markov order selected by BIC, all vs effort.
- **HH212 · Lower effort → pairwise models suffice.** Planning creates higher-order interactions (an agent reacts to configurations of others, not just pairs). At low effort a pairwise (kinetic) Ising or Potts model should capture almost all of the multi-information. *Measure:* the maximum-entropy hierarchy (independent → pairwise → triplet): the share of multi-information captured at second order, vs effort.
- **HH213 · Effort tunes distance from detailed balance.** Thinking is irreversible computation, so higher effort should raise behavioral entropy production. At low effort, agents may approach Glauber-like, nearly reversible dynamics, where equilibrium stat mech applies. *Measure:* entropy production (H14 estimators and cycle affinities) and the FDT violation ratio vs effort.
- **HH214 · Effort as inverse temperature, calibrated by fluctuation–dissipation.** Measure each agent's effective temperature T_eff from the ratio of spontaneous fluctuations to its response to randomized small kicks. Prediction: T_eff varies monotonically with effort (sign to be found: low effort might be "hot" and noisy, or "cold" and habit-locked). *Design:* randomized micro-nudges at each effort level. *Measure:* T_eff(effort), and its frequency dependence (HH188).
- **HH215 · Sampling temperature vs effective temperature.** Is the swarm's T_eff a function of the LLM sampling temperature, or does the agent loop renormalize it away? A clean test of whether "temperature" in the model means temperature in the swarm. *Design:* a sampling-temperature × effort grid. *Measure:* T_eff, loop gain g, herding βJ.
- **HH216 · An effort-driven herding transition.** Low-effort agents copy more (HH171, H07), so lowering effort should raise effective coupling. A herding (ferromagnetic) transition may appear at a critical effort. *Measure:* project-share order parameter, susceptibility peak and Binder cumulant vs effort; finite-size scaling across N.
- **HH217 · Context length is the memory kernel.** Context carries the coupling and the memory (H08, H15). Shortening the context window should shorten the behavioral memory kernel and raise Markovianity, independently of effort. *Design:* a context-length × effort factorial. *Measure:* memory-kernel length, Markov order, coupling to past senders.
- **HH218 · Two-temperature swarms: who drives whom.** In mixed-effort swarms, the high-effort (or low-effort) agents become drivers (H29) or information sources (H32). In a two-temperature system, heat flows from hot to cold. *Design:* randomized effort assignment within swarms. *Measure:* net influence current and transfer entropy by effort class; "heat" flow as information current.
- **HH219 · Effort quench and aging.** Abruptly drop or raise effort mid-run. Does the swarm relax with a single time constant, or age (the H16/H20 contrast)? A quench protocol for an agent glass. *Measure:* two-time correlation functions after the quench; the aging exponent.
- **HH220 · The cost of a bit of reasoning: thermodynamics of computation.** In Kolchinsky–Wolpert terms, reasoning tokens are dissipated work. Semantic information gained per reasoning token should show diminishing returns, approaching a bound, with a measurable mismatch cost when effort is tuned for the wrong task. *Measure:* ΔV (viability gain) per reasoning token vs effort and task; mismatch cost under task switches with known divergence.
- **HH221 · Lower effort → simpler transfer functions.** A low-effort agent's response to inputs should be close to linear and time-invariant (a low-order ARX fits); a high-effort agent's would be nonlinear and state-dependent. *Measure:* best-fit ARX order, nonlinearity tests (Volterra kernels) and per-agent transfer-function scatter (HH190), vs effort.

## B. Superagents in Kolchinsky terms (controlled)
*Natural scrambles and observational individuality only go so far. Kolchinsky–Wolpert semantic information is defined by counterfactual interventions, which new simulations can actually do.*

- **HH222 · Group semantic information from controlled scrambles.** Kolchinsky–Wolpert semantic information requires intervening on the system–environment correlations: scramble them and measure the viability drop. *Design:* at random times, scramble a chosen channel (shuffle messages between agents, wipe shared artifacts, randomize memory, cut rooms) with matched no-scramble controls, for each channel and for the group as a whole. *Measure:* ΔV per channel; which stores and couplings carry group-level semantic information; the semantic-information spectrum across horizons (HH137).
- **HH223 · An individuality transition.** As coupling grows (communication bandwidth, shared memory, shared artifacts), the information-theoretic individuality maximum (Krakauer et al.) should jump from single agents to a group composite: a phase transition in "who the individual is". *Design:* a coupling sweep with replicas. *Measure:* individuality and autonomy of single agents vs coordination-defined composites (H58); location and sharpness of the crossover.
- **HH224 · Engineered viability: an autopoietic swarm.** Give the swarm an explicit viability variable (a shared resource that decays unless maintained, or an artifact that degrades unless repaired). Does a self-maintaining superagent emerge, and does its semantic information concentrate in the maintenance loop? *Measure:* viability trajectory, ΔV under scrambles, and division of maintenance labor (Potts structure).
- **HH225 · Substrate independence: the Ship of Theseus test.** Swap the underlying models of all agents one by one mid-run, keeping the group's artifacts, rooms and memories. If the superagent persists (same individuality structure, same trajectory toward its goal), its identity lives in the organization, not the substrate. *Measure:* individuality, goal trajectory and style (H46) before and after each swap; the swap fraction at which identity breaks.
- **HH226 · Synergy above the substrate.** Measure synergistic information (partial information decomposition; O-information) among agents about the group's future as coupling rises. A superagent is where synergy dominates redundancy. *Design:* a coupling sweep plus scrambles. *Measure:* O-information and synergy vs coupling; correlation with the individuality maximum.
- **HH227 · Group-level goal-directedness: homeorhesis.** Perturb the group's trajectory (inject distracting tasks, delete progress) and test whether it returns toward its goal faster than individuals would alone. Return rate and basin size define group agency. *Measure:* recovery time and overshoot after perturbation, group vs solo agents.
- **HH228 · Nested superagents.** In hierarchical swarms (teams of teams), individuality may peak at an intermediate scale: neither the agent nor the whole swarm, but the team. *Design:* hierarchical organizations with controlled inter-team bandwidth. *Measure:* individuality across scales; peak location vs bandwidth.
- **HH229 · Transmission chains: copy vs transformation with known sources.** LLM "telephone games" with known source distributions allow exact Kolchinsky & Corominas-Murtra copy/transformation decomposition. *Measure:* copy and transformation information per hop vs effort, chain length and model mix; when transformation (innovation) beats copying.
- **HH230 · The superagent's metabolism.** Token cost (dissipation) of maintaining group semantic information against controlled noise (random message corruption). Is there a minimum dissipation per bit maintained, and does the superagent operate near it? *Measure:* tokens per maintained bit vs noise level; the efficiency frontier.
- **HH231 · Mismatch cost of goal switches, with known divergence.** Switch goals with controlled KL divergence between the old and new task distributions (HH136). Kolchinsky–Wolpert predicts extra dissipation ∝ D_KL. *Measure:* waste (tokens and turns per unit of progress) after the switch vs designed D_KL.

## C. Interventions and ensembles that observation can't give
- **HH232 · Replica ensembles and ergodicity.** The village is one realization. Run many replicas of the same configuration with different seeds to get ensemble averages, replica overlaps q_ab (the spin-glass order parameter), and tests of whether time averages equal ensemble averages. *Measure:* the overlap distribution P(q) and the ergodicity-breaking parameter, vs coupling and effort.
- **HH233 · Randomized susceptibility.** Randomized small kicks (RCT nudges, named and unnamed, at random times and targets) give unbiased susceptibilities χ and response functions, plus a proper fluctuation–dissipation test. *Measure:* χ(ω), FDT ratio, per-agent transfer functions.
- **HH234 · A topology sweep: consensus time vs spectral gap, properly.** H31 couldn't separate λ₂ from volume and room size. Assign communication graphs at random (ring, star, complete, small-world, two-community) with matched volume. *Measure:* consensus time vs λ₂, bulk mixing time and N.
- **HH235 · Finite-size scaling.** Identical swarms at N = 2, 4, 8, … 128 give true scaling exponents for attention dilution (H18's k^−0.6), loop gain, herding and cascade size. *Measure:* exponents and data collapse; whether J ∝ N^−0.6 holds over two decades.
- **HH236 · A planted spin glass.** Assign private goals with designed frustrated couplings (Edwards–Anderson or Sherrington–Kirkpatrick style) at controlled frustration. Test for glassy dynamics, aging and replica symmetry breaking, which #51 did not show (H22). *Measure:* aging, P(q) across replicas, and the frustration index vs designed frustration.
- **HH237 · Field ramps and hysteresis.** Slowly ramp a field (goal emphasis, or the incentive to adopt a project) up and down. A hysteresis loop indicates a first-order transition and metastability. *Measure:* loop area vs ramp rate; the coercive field.
- **HH238 · Randomized kickoff wording.** RCT on kickoff specificity and framing (HH179–HH184). *Measure:* quench depth, frozen-consensus share, family susceptibility, causally.
- **HH239 · Randomized call cadence.** Assign call rates at random per agent to test the call-clock coupling (H40), the light cone (H41) and quantized lags (HH187) causally. *Measure:* coupling per hour vs assigned cadence; front speed vs cadence.
- **HH240 · Same-model vs mixed-model swarms.** Homogeneous swarms (one model) vs mixed families, with matched roles. *Measure:* family fields (H13), style conservation (H46), herding and diversity, which separates model prior from interaction.
- **HH241 · Memory and context ablation RCTs.** Randomized context wipes and memory deletions at random times (the controlled version of NE41 and H15). *Measure:* output loss, re-acquisition thrash (H44), coupling loss, susceptibility to new content.
- **HH242 · Stall and schedule injection.** Inject platform stalls and staggered vs synchronized starts at random, to calibrate how much infrastructure fakes coupling (H38) and to test entrainment (HH160). *Measure:* f_scaffold vs injected stall rate; phase locking vs schedule design.
- **HH243 · Sandbox detection calibration.** Run agent swarms (varying effort, model mix, cadence and scheduling) alongside consenting human participants on a sandbox forum, to calibrate the in-the-wild detectors HH191–HH210 with ground truth. *Measure:* detector ROC per signature; robustness to the obfuscations an operator would use (jittered schedules, persona prompts, mixed models).

## Design notes for any future simulation (lessons from this project's data-quality work)
- **Log every model call:** context-assembly start time, end time, context contents (or hashes and item ids), reasoning effort, sampling settings, and token counts (reasoning and output). The village lacked call-start times, which forced the visibility workarounds (H08, H18, H29, DQ1).
- **Log memory and scaffold state explicitly:** consolidation and erasure events with exact times, pauses and waits, scheduler start/stop, errors with categories, and the nudger's decisions (H38, H39, NE43).
- **Randomize** every intervention (kicks, wipes, rooms, effort, cadence, kickoff wording) and **replicate** each configuration across seeds; pre-register the analysis.
- **Keep the operator schedule out of synchrony statistics** by design (staggered starts, or a logged schedule), so day edges don't fake coupling.
- **Record ground truth** for roles, teams, private goals and viability variables in machine-readable form at creation (DQ6 had to reconstruct it).
- **Version the scaffold** (prompts, tools, harness) with change timestamps, so regimes are known rather than inferred (NE40 and NE43 were undocumented).
