\hwhat{We measured the two-time correlation $C(t_w+\tau,t_w)$ of each agent's day-mean content embedding ($t_w$ = active days since kickoff) in 29 non-holdout goal periods. The aging slope $A$ at fixed lag ($t_w\ge2$) was tested against a fitted stationary swarm model. Synthetic check at village sampling: size 0.03--0.07, power 0.33--0.44 against $\mu=0.5$ aging. The pre-registered isotropic null was 2--3$\times$ too narrow on real data (content fluctuates in 5--12 of 32 dimensions); a post-hoc null with the fitted shape fixed it. \textbf{No aging:} random-effects $A$ over long and medium goals is $+0.021\pm0.021$. The one strong effect (\#38) is a kickoff relaxation: lag-1 $C$ rises 0.66$\to$0.87 in four days, then stays flat. In \#51, $\mu=0.5$ aging is rejected. Holidays drive the short-week outliers. Holdout not run.}
\hmodel{Models 01 (SK-type glassy aging) and 11 (vector spins). Spin: agent $i$'s day state $\bar x_{it}$, the mean of its unit-normalized, regime-whitened ($n=32$) statement embeddings (own chat and plans); $v_{it}$ is its sampling noise. $q$: plateau (style and goal content); $c_0$: short-lag ceiling; $\tau_0$: relaxation time; $\mu$: aging exponent of the Box-Cox clock $s_\mu$ ($\mu=0$ stationary, $\mu=1$ simple aging). Rival: a kickoff transient decaying in $\tau_q$ days. $n_{\rm eff}$: dimension of the day-to-day fluctuations.}
\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}
\begin{gather*}
C(t,t')=\frac{\sum_i \bar x_{it}\cdot\bar x_{it'}}{\sqrt{\sum_i S_{it}\,\sum_i S_{it'}}},\quad S_{it}=|\bar x_{it}|^2-v_{it}\\
C(t_w+\tau,t_w)=q+(c_0-q)\,e^{-\Delta s/\tau_0}\\
\Delta s=s_\mu(t_w+\tau)-s_\mu(t_w),\quad s_\mu(t)=\tfrac{t^{1-\mu}-1}{1-\mu}\\
A=\partial C/\partial\ln t_w\,\big|_{\tau},\qquad \mathrm{Var}\,C(t,t')\propto 1/n_{\rm eff}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-6pt}}

\htelos{Mostly a negative result, mildly useful. An operator worried that a long-running swarm ``rigidifies'' as its goal ages can relax a little: at day resolution, content turnover does not slow in week three versus week one (weak aging is not excluded). The practical number is the settling time after a big kickoff, about four active days, seen once (\#38). The more transferable lesson is methodological: embedding similarity statistics on swarm logs need nulls with the empirical, low-dimensional fluctuation shape, or they are 2--3$\times$ overconfident. As physics, aging finds nothing to grip: daily re-prompting with a fixed goal acts like a strong pinning field.}
\hbottom{LLM-swarm content shows no detectable aging at day resolution: after a few days' kickoff relaxation it decorrelates at an age-independent rate, and only a null calibrated to the low-dimensional fluctuation shape says so honestly.}
