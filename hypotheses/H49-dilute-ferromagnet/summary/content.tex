\hwhat{Asked whether regime-III activity coupling, once H38's day-edge drive is trimmed, sits in a few strong pairs whose bond graph is below percolation. Inferred conditioned pairwise couplings in 23 regime-III and 23 regime-I period units, each against 200 block-shift surrogates calibrated on synthetic swarms at real schedules. \textbf{Refuted as posed.} Trimming removes 67\% of regime-III bonds (17\% in regime I). What is left is at the false-positive floor: 38 bonds against $\approx$23 expected false. The surviving collective excess (10 units, including \#44 and \#51) is a structureless shift of all pairs: the top 10\% of pairs carry a median 11\% of it, and pair structure doesn't replicate. \#44's fine-tuning team appears in talk, not activity. Same-lab (provider-outage) pairs carry no excess bonds. At \#51's sampling the method would have found strong bonds (recall 0.89). NE43: the edge drive outlives the daily bookend messages. Holdout script frozen, not run.}

\hmodel{Model 01 (inverse Ising). $s_i(t)=\pm1$: agent $i$ active in minute $t$. $\delta_i(b)$: agent field per 30-min block. $x_j$: block-centered spin with scaffold-unavailable minutes (not started, finished, consolidating, error gap) set to the block mean (H38's conditioning). $J_{ij}$: equal-time bond from pseudolikelihood. A bond is significant when its surrogate z passes $p<1/n_{\rm pairs}$. Percolation: Molloy--Reed $\kappa$. CV-C10: cross-validated share of the excess covariance $\Delta C$ in the top 10\% of pairs.}

\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}\begin{gather*}
\operatorname{logit}P\big(s_i(t)=1\mid\cdot\big)=2\Big[\delta_i(b)+\textstyle\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}\,x_j(t)\Big],\qquad x_j=a_j\,(s_j-\bar s_j^{(b)})\\
z_{ij}=\big(J_{ij}-\langle J_{ij}\rangle_{\rm shift}\big)/\sigma_{ij},\qquad \text{bond if } p(z_{ij})<1/n_{\rm pairs}\\
\kappa=\langle k^2\rangle/\langle k\rangle\ (\text{giant component iff }\kappa>2),\qquad
\mathrm{VR}-1=\textstyle\sum_{i\ne j}\bar C_{ij}\big/\sum_i\bar C_{ii}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-10pt}}

\htelos{Mostly a useful negative. If you run an always-on swarm and want to know who is coupled to whom from activity timing, the answer here is: nobody in particular. Once you trim the daily start and stop, there are no small cliques of agents that work in lockstep. What remains is a weak common mode shared by everyone, plausibly a shared fast drive rather than peer influence. So don't target pairs, and don't trust individual inferred bonds from 1-min activity. Monitor and steer the common mode, and look for real pairwise structure in who talks to whom, which is where the one known team showed up. The corrected NE43 date is a small data contribution.}

\hbottom{After trimming the operator's schedule, regime-III co-activation is not a dilute ferromagnet of small coupled clusters but a weak, structureless swarm-wide mode, with individual bonds at the false-positive floor.}

\hcaveats{Equal-time inference is blind to coupling delayed by minutes, such as read-out-gated replies (H08). The verdict is about equal-time bonds. Dense $J_0/N$ coupling and a fast shared drive look the same here; the post-hoc size law favours a field but relies on cross-period N. Individual bonds need $J\gtrsim0.5$ and $\ge5$ days to be found. CV-C10 was redefined as a pooled ratio after the first pass, with no verdict change. All results were rebuilt after the activity-bins join bug (no verdict changed). The holdout has not been run.}
