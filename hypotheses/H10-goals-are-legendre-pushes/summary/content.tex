\hwhat{The question is whether a swarm's unforced fluctuations predict how it lines up behind an assigned goal. The state is each agent's 30-min statement embedding, projected on the goal direction $\hat g$. Three free $\to$ assigned pairs exist (\#11$\to$\#12, \#16$\to$\#17, \#37$\to$\#38). In each pair one field $\lambda$ fits the mean shift, and the model predicts the rest. Alignment rises 1.4--3.1 free-week SDs, outside linear response. \textbf{The core test fails}: fluctuations do not predict who moves ($r=-0.37,-0.69,+0.20$; Stouffer $p=0.89$). \textbf{Round 1b} uses shared goal vectors, a second embedding model, dedupe and style residualization. The test fails in all 8 input configurations. Variance along $\hat g$ grows, and transverse variance holds. In a new native test a reassigned goal moves one agent 10--20 SDs, beyond any point its unforced content visits. An agent-set goal moves agents, but again not in fluctuation order. The holdout is frozen and has not run.}

\hmodel{Model 11: mean-field O($n$) in $n=32$ whitened embedding dimensions. $\mathbf s_i$ are agent $i$'s unit statement vectors. $y=\hat{\mathbf z}\cdot\hat g$ is the alignment with the goal field direction. $x_i$ is its 30-min window mean, with free-week mean $\mu_i$, noise-corrected variance $\kappa_{2,i}$ and third cumulant $\kappa_{3,i}$. The goal is a linear field $\lambda=\beta h$ along $\hat g$. $K$ is the cumulant generating function, $J_0$ the mean-field coupling, $g$ the loop gain and $C_F$ the free-week window covariance.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
P_A(\mathbf s)\propto P_F(\mathbf s)\,e^{\lambda\sum_i\hat g\cdot\mathbf s_i},\qquad G=F-\lambda m\\
K_A(t)=K_F(t+\lambda)-K_F(\lambda)\ \Rightarrow\ \kappa^A_n=K_F^{(n)}(\lambda)\\
\Delta\mu_i=\lambda\kappa_{2,i}+\tfrac{\lambda^2}{2}\kappa_{3,i},\qquad \kappa^A_{2,i}=\kappa_{2,i}+\lambda\kappa_{3,i}\\
\chi_{\rm swarm}=\chi_0/(1-\beta J_0\chi_0),\qquad g=\beta J_0\chi_0=1-V_{\rm indep}/V\\
\Delta\boldsymbol\mu=\lambda\,C_F\,\hat g
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{The promise was to read a swarm's steerability from its unsteered fluctuations. That promise does not hold here. A goal change is a large kick (median about 2.5 SDs), not a small field. The agents that wander most are not the agents that follow most. An assigned goal acts as a common target and a new collective mode, not as a gentle tilt of existing preferences. Do not forecast compliance from pre-goal behavior: the fluctuation--response correlation runs from $-0.69$ to $+0.20$. The instrument (goal-direction projection, noise-corrected variance) is cheap on any agent log. The fluctuation--response physics it tests is a curiosity in this village.}

\hbottom{Goals are quenches toward a common target, not Legendre pushes: free-week fluctuations do not predict who follows, in both embedding models.}
