\hwhat{H39 asks which operator levers change \emph{what} agents do (a field: occupancy shifts) and which only speed them up (a catalyst: escape at fixed occupancy). A kinetic decomposition of behavior-state chains in matched windows measures both around nudges, human messages, @-mentions, context erasures and step levers in 32 non-holdout periods. \textbf{Round 1b} uses the nudge's leading @ as its only target, DQ8's presence-cut lever design, and Jev v3.1 behavior states as a second state space (V4). \textbf{Nudges are both}, now in two state spaces. On B4, $K=+0.13$ and the idle share moves $-0.048$ (the CI now excludes 0). On V4, $K=+0.25$, the waiting share moves $-0.08$ and waiting escape rises $\times1.6$. Human messages are fields. @-mentions steer content (+0.33 SD), not behavior. Forced erasure is a field toward work in both state spaces; its $K$ flips sign between them. Natives: the nudger switch-off is a catalytic loss in both state spaces (NE43), and nudges are not more catalytic under the 12-h pause default (NE44). The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Models 02/10 give a kinetic Potts (Markov-jump) agent. The states are $s\in\{$work, chat, idle, consolidate$\}$, with Arrhenius rates $k_{ij}$, well depths $G_i$ and symmetric barriers $B_{ij}$. A field $h$ tilts the wells; a catalyst $c$ lowers the barriers. For each lever, matched 30-min windows give the kicked and control transition matrices $T^K,T^C$ and their stationary distributions $\pi^K,\pi^C$. From these come the escape probability $e_s=1-T_{ss}$, the field index $\phi$, the catalytic index $K$ and the catalytic fraction $\rho$.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
k_{ij}=\nu\,e^{-(B_{ij}-G_i)/T},\qquad \pi_i\propto e^{-G_i/T}\\
s_{ij}=\tfrac12(\delta\ln k_{ij}+\delta\ln k_{ji})\quad\text{(catalytic part)}\\
a_{ij}=\tfrac12(\delta\ln k_{ij}-\delta\ln k_{ji})\quad\text{(field part)}\\
\phi=\mathrm{SD}_{\pi^C}\!\left[\ln\pi^K_s-\ln\pi^C_s\right],\qquad \rho=|K|/(|K|+\phi_{\rm exc})\\
K=\ln\Big(\textstyle\sum_s\pi^C_s e^K_s\big/\sum_s\pi^C_s e^C_s\Big)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{To unstick an idle agent, send one directed nudge. Escape rises ${\sim}$50\%, and the agent stays out of idle while it acts. To steer what an agent writes without a change in how busy it is, mention it. To change topics, set a goal; that works in the large regime-III village. The field/catalyst split depends on a convention, the nudge verdict rests on one period (G51), and the effects are individual and diluted. The nudger switch-off (08-21) removes catalysis, not a field, and barely moves the swarm. Every message carries a field. Prompt tweaks, room changes and most fixes show nothing at day scale.}

\hbottom{No operator lever is a pure catalyst: nudges both unstick and tilt agents, while human messages, mentions, erasures and goals act mainly as fields, on activity or content by regime.}

\hcaveats{The nudge class rests on one period (G51); G38 has the same signs but is not significant. Fields are invisible below ${\sim}$100 episodes, so ``catalyst'' or ``neither'' in small periods does not exclude a field. The field/catalyst split depends on a load-distribution convention, and $|K|<0.10$ has no clear reading. B4 states mean different things in regimes I and III. Mentions are partly common-cause. The card flags design choices made after seeing data: the no-consolidate erasure chain, the content-TV check and the post hoc P9 switch-off. Of 28 class-level tests, the nudge field alone does not survive Bonferroni.}
