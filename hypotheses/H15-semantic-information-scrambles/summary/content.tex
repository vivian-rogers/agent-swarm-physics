\hwhat{The data is natural scrambles in non-reserved periods, per goal period, against placebo nulls calibrated on synthetic worlds built on the real village skeleton. \textbf{Rounds 1--1b:} losing 50--80\% of memory (12 events) or starting with an empty memory (20 newcomers) shows no day-scale cost on the viability $V^*$ ($+0.29$ SD [$-0.05$, $+0.63$]; newcomers $+0.20$). A forced context wipe (the 41-call cap) cuts work commits by 39\% for ten calls (7/9 periods), whatever the agent saves to memory. \textbf{Round 2} asks where that value sits. It compares 20,592 forced wipes with 21,806 mid-segment pseudo-wipes in nine regime-III periods, and 37,031 consolidation notes with the commands that follow them.}
\hmodel{Model 04, Kolchinsky--Wolpert semantic information. The system $X$ is one agent: its memory $M$ (day-scale store) and its context window $C$ (session-scale store, filled by chat and events). $Y$ is the rest of the village. A scramble erases $M$ or $C$, or cuts $Y\to X$; $\hat p$ is the scrambled joint. $V$ is viability per agent-day (5 candidates) or per call (work). $u$ is its same-day residual, and $\delta$ is the fraction removed. $S$ is the semantic information and $\eta$ its share of $I(X;Y)$.}
\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}
\begin{gather*}
\Delta V = V[p]-V[\hat p],\qquad \kappa=\Delta V/\delta\\
S=\min_f I(\hat p^{f})\ \text{s.t.}\ V[\hat p^{f}]=V[p],\quad \eta=\tfrac{S}{I}\le 1-\delta^*\\
u_{ad}=V_{ad}-\overline{V}_{-a,d}\\
\widehat{\Delta V}=\bar u_{\rm post}-\big(\mu_a+\rho^{s}\,\mathbb{E}[h_{t_0-1}\mid u_{\rm pre}]\big)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-6pt}}

\htelos{The working context keeps an agent productive, and its value is spread over the working state, not stored in a few items. Re-showing the last files or the newest messages after a wipe recovers at most a few percent of the lost work (4\% [$-23$, $+26$]). Agents re-read by habit, not by what was erased. The usable number stays the cost of a reset: $-39\%$ of committed work for about ten calls, about a tenth of output at a 41-call cadence. To recover it, raise the cap; a carried summary note will not do it, because notes are only about 11\% enacted and their content buys no output. The first-read share $\eta_{\rm first}\approx0.04$ is the closest this data comes to Kolchinsky--Wolpert's $\eta$.}
\hbottom{A forced wipe costs 39\% of committed work for ten calls; the value is not in the few items read first (they carry 4\% of the dip), and memory notes are about 11\% enacted.}
