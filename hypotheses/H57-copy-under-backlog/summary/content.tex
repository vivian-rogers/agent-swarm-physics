\hwhat{HH171 claimed that under a large backlog agents echo what they read instead of transforming it. For every statement we took the messages it had read since the agent last spoke (context ledger), its backlog $k$, and asked whether near-copies of what was read rise with $k$ within agent, on 34 non-holdout periods plus three native tests (NE42 merge/split, NE41 erasures, the \#51 N sweep). Synthetic worlds came first and caught seven estimator flaws. The pre-registered slopes came out negative in 31/35 periods, but that is an artifact: agents posting at the same moment write near-identical messages without reading each other. The most generous estimate (raw read-set echo) rises by at most $+0.0016$ per doubling of $k$, about 5\% of the synthetic H57 effect. Agents almost never near-copy the message they address. \textbf{Refuted.} Holdout script written, not run.}

\hmodel{Kolchinsky--Corominas-Murtra channel (model 08): a statement $B$ is the output, its source $A$ in the read set $R(B)$ the input. Copy information counts $B$ reproducing $A$; transformation information counts systematic dependence through change. H57: the copy probability $\pi(k)$ rises with backlog $k=|R|$. Chance is measured on mutually invisible pairs (posted while both calls ran). Copying is coded at the message level (near-copy), because cluster-level ``copying'' is topic coherence.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
I(X;Y)=I_{\rm copy}+I_{\rm tr},\quad I_{\rm copy}=\sum_x p(x)\,d\big(p(Y{=}x|x)\,\|\,p_Y(x)\big)\\
e_B=\mathbb{1}\big[\max_{A\in R(B)}\cos(A,B)\ge\tau\big]-\big[1-(1-\hat q)^{k}\big]\\
e_B=\beta\log_2(1+k)+\gamma\cdot\mathbf{z}_B+\alpha_{\rm agent\times unit}+\varepsilon_B,\qquad \text{H57: }\beta>0
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{A useful negative, plus a measurement warning. Operators worried that overloaded agents will parrot each other can relax: more unread messages do not make agents echo more, and when agents answer a named peer they rephrase rather than copy. The warning matters more. Near-duplicate messages in a swarm are mostly agents responding to the same moment at the same time, not one agent copying another. Any echo or ``groupthink'' monitor that counts near-duplicates without comparing them with unread messages at the same lag will mistake synchrony for copying. That bears on DQ5's cross-echo flag, H12's self-repetition and H07's ``copying dominates''. The Kolchinsky decomposition only means something at message level: at topic level it reads coherence.}

\hbottom{Agents in the village do not echo more when their backlog grows (at most $+0.16$ points per doubling), they rephrase what they answer, and most near-duplication is simultaneous convergence rather than copying.}

\hcaveats{The pre-registered estimator is invalid on real data, so the verdict rests on a post hoc upper bound and lag profiles. Near-copy thresholds come from two contrastive encoders truncated at 256 tokens; rephrasing below threshold counts as transformation. The read set is the backlog since the last talk; older in-context items can be copied too (context load shows nothing either). The lag-matched estimator's long-lag chance rate rests on few unread pairs. Backlog is observational and NE42 is goal-confounded. The 34 replication folders are one estimator, not 34 tests.}
