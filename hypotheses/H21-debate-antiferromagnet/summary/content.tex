\hwhat{Tested on \#12: 10 debates, each with its own judge, motion and re-drafted teams, derived from the record; predictions dated before the run. \textbf{The pre-registered two-sublattice test failed}: within- minus cross-team cosine of masked, whitened, agent-centred content vectors $\bar\Delta=0.028$ ($p=0.34$); the true split was the best split in 1/10 debates (chance 1.5); same-lab pairs out-align team-mates. Order exists only along the motion's \emph{a-priori} support-minus-oppose axis: Government sits on the support side ($\sigma=0.092$, $p=0.002$, 9/10), a field about 0.2 of statement noise. The motion topic is a uniform field, off after the verdict (8/8). Post hoc, the stance tilt \emph{reverses} after the verdict (7/9). \#34 holdout: not run.}

Fields: motion topic $h_u\hat g$ (uniform), assigned sides $h_s\hat a$ (staggered), family $\mathbf h_i$, verdict $h_v$ toward winner $W$. \htelos{Candidly: assigned teams, the strongest public faction structure the village ever had, are invisible as factions in embedding space; topic and model family swamp side. Detecting hidden teams from generic embeddings looks hopeless, and the \#34 saboteur test is a long shot. Useful warning: clustering agent messages finds labs and topics, not coalitions. What carries signal is a hand-built axis for the question, which is ordinary stance detection. The post-verdict reversal hints that role-play stances do not persist (post hoc).}

\hbottom{The debate week is a ferromagnet in the motion's topic with family co-variation and only a faint one-dimensional staggered tilt along the stance axis, not a two-sublattice antiferromagnet.}
