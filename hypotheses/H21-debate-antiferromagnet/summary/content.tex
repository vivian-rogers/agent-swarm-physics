\htelos{Practical lesson: look for factions in reply stance, not in embeddings. Clustering messages finds labs and topics, never even the assigned teams; a cheap zero-shot stance label on reply pairs, read in aggregate against an agent-field null, recovers 8 of 10 drafted teams. The flip side matters as much: agents built on these models do not form camps on their own, not over a contested vote and not in a debate about the news. Opposition appears where a protocol assigns it and disappears when the protocol ends. For an operator, that makes a stance-based faction alarm useful mainly as a check that assigned roles are being played, and a sign of something unusual when it fires unassigned.}

\hbottom{In what they talk about the debaters form a ferromagnet in the motion's topic; in how they reply, the drafted teams form two sublattices that the assignment switches on and the verdict switches off, and no camps form where nobody assigns sides.}
