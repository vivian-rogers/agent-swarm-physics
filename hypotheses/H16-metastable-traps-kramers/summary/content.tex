\hwhat{The units are per-agent trap spells: inactive spells and timer-pause chains, on the wall clock (30-s bins) and on the call clock (idle gates of the context ledger). We test them in 11 non-reserved goal periods. Kramers escape predicts a constant hazard; traps \emph{age} instead (\#51 $\beta=-0.77$). Round 2 asks why. A P\'olya urn says the context fills with the agent's own repeats. It predicts the gate-clock exponent from composition alone ($-0.36$ vs $-0.35$) and absorbs half of it, but not the wall-clock one ($-0.08$ vs $-0.77$). The decisive test is the forced erasure at the 41-call cap (NE41). It lifts sustained escape at the next gate from 0.47 to 0.84 (log OR $+2.68$ [2.31, 3.16]), 4.6$\times$ what dilution implies. A per-trap frailty world gives no step. A directed message works by address, not dilution. Trap depth is not a model trait. Spell-kind mixing explains little of \#51's aging and most of it in two 4-h periods. The reserved data are not used.}

\hmodel{Model 02 with an urn on the context window. At an idle gate $g$ (a call after a pause call) the agent acts or re-pauses. The context segment since the last reset holds $n_\text{rep}$ own idle calls, $n_\text{act}$ own active calls and $k$ room items. The urn sets escape by the own-repeat share $f$, measured four ways: tokens, own calls, all entries, the last 10 entries. Rivals: aging with trap age $a$ at fixed $f$; a context step at erasure; a mixture of spell kinds; per-trap frailty. Fit: agent effects $\alpha_i$, slopes $\beta_a$, $\beta_f$, reset step $\delta$, read effects $\gamma$.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\text{escape}_g)=c_i\,(1-f_g),\qquad f_\text{call}=\frac{n_\text{rep}}{n_\text{rep}+n_\text{act}}\\
\operatorname{logit}P_g=\alpha_i+\beta_a\ln a_g+\beta_f\ln(1-f_g)\\
\qquad+\,\delta\,\mathbb 1[\text{reset}]+\gamma_d\,\mathbb 1[\text{dir}]+\gamma_u\,\mathbb 1[\text{undir}]\\
\text{urn: }\beta_f\approx1,\ \beta_a\to0,\ \gamma_d=\gamma_u\approx\Delta\ln(1-f)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{When an agent re-pauses again and again, erase or consolidate its context. In \#51 that ends 84\% of traps at the next gate, against 47\% otherwise, at any depth. Deep traps (5+ re-pauses) still escape less after the erasure (0.58 vs 0.93), so some depth lives outside the context. If you message a stuck agent, name it. A directed read adds $+0.69$ log odds, room chatter $+0.24$; a second message adds about a third. Do not expect a model to be reliably more trap-prone: depth does not repeat across periods.}

\hbottom{Pause-chain traps are held by the context: a forced erasure ends most of them, and a message works by naming the agent, not by diluting its repeats.}

\hcaveats{The reset and dose tests are powered only in \#51. A day-level mixture of escape rates (post hoc) reproduces the wall-clock exponent ($-0.93$), so that exponent is not separable from day states; the gate results are not affected. With the gate count in the model (post hoc), trap age turns timer-like and the count carries the aging. Per-trap frailty fakes gate aging in synthetic worlds; only the erasure separates it. Kick exposure is now the ledger read at the gate; no in-flight placebo was run in round 2.}
