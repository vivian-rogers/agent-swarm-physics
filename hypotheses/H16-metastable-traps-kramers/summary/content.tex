\hwhat{Four trap types (inactive spells, timer-pause chains, error loops, repeated-command loops) were tested in 11 non-holdout goal periods (3 regime I, 8 regime III). Predictions were written after a synthetic validation and before touching data. Kramers predicts memoryless escape; instead, traps \emph{age}. Within agent, the escape hazard falls roughly as $t^{-0.8}$ (G51: $\beta=-0.77$ [$-0.81,-0.73$], 4,321 escapes; G38: $-1.71$). Each re-pause lowers the odds of acting at the next timer expiry (G51: $-0.38$), and error loops age too ($-0.72$). Kicks help but saturate: one directed message during a pause raises the odds of acting $\times$1.5--2.9 (5/7 periods), and more messages add little (14/16 powered dose curves additive or saturating, not exponential). Messages never broke error loops (0/10) and did not shorten long regime-I silences (0/3). $\beta J_0=0.09$--$0.41$; swarm activity is unimodal. The barrier was unidentifiable. Holdout (\#32, \#45): not run.}

\hmodel{Models 01/02 (mean field) plus Kramers escape. Agent activity $x$ moves in a double well $U(x)$ (idle and active basins) at temperature $T$. A trap is a stay in the idle basin, escaped at rate $k$ over barrier $\Delta U$ (attempt rate $\nu$). A kick (a message reaching the agent) adds force $f$. Swarm: Curie--Weiss coupling $J_0$, field $h$, active fraction $m$. Fitted: hazard $h_{it}$ with agent effects $\alpha_i$, aging slope $\beta$, dose effects $\gamma_n$.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
k=\nu\,e^{-\Delta U/T},\qquad G(x)=-\ln P(x)=U(x)/T+\text{const}\\
k(n)=k_0\,e^{nf/T}\ \text{(Kramers)}\quad\text{vs}\quad k(n)=k_0(1+c\,n)\ \text{(triggers)}\\
\ln\!\big[-\ln(1-h_{it})\big]=\alpha_i+\beta\ln t+\textstyle\sum_{n}\gamma_n\,\mathbb 1[\text{dose}_{it}=n]\\
f(m)=-\tfrac{1}{2}J_0m^2-hm-Ts(m);\ \text{bistable iff }\beta J_0(1-m^2)>1
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{Modestly useful, mostly as a correction. Facts an operator could use: a stuck agent gets stickier the longer it sits, so intervene early rather than wait. The lever that works is one directed message landing during a pause; the agent reads it at its next timer expiry. Repeating it, or chatting at the room, adds little. Chat does nothing for error loops, which need another fix (tooling, a reset). There is no swarm-wide tipping point at today's coupling. The physics added little: ``Kramers'' failed, the free-energy barrier was a smoothing artifact, and ``aging plus saturating triggers'' is ordinary survival analysis. Effects are modest and unconfirmed on the holdout.}

\hbottom{Agent traps are real and individual, but they age instead of escaping by Kramers kinetics, and kicks act as saturating triggers at scheduled decision points rather than as barrier-lowering forces.}
