\hwhat{At each talk turn we counted the $k$ messages that arrived in the agent's room since its previous turn and asked whether its message names each pending sender, fitting within agent-days in 16 non-holdout goal periods. Addressing falls with $k$ in all 16 (held-out wins over ``no budget'' in 16/16): $\hat\beta\approx0.46$--$0.78$, pooled $0.63\pm0.02$. The curve saturates; it is not a literal $1/k$ (that won in only 5/16), and recency-only wins 5/16. In \#51, timer-wake batches of exogenous size give $\hat\beta=0.50$, so it is not just turn timing there; small periods are inconclusive. Across periods, per-pair uptake falls with room size ($\rho=-0.55$) while senders addressed per turn do not. Failed: mention bypass, the 05-04 merge, half the room-size contrasts, and a placebo. Holdout script written, not run.}

\hmodel{Mean-field coupling (model 01) with Hawkes-style per-pair cross-excitation (09). Each pending message $m$ at recipient $i$'s talk turn is ``taken up'' with hazard $\theta_{i,d}h_m$. Sender $j$ is addressed (a clean @-mention) if any of its messages is taken up. $\theta_{i,d}$: agent$\times$day propensity. $h$: the attention rule (constant, $1/k$, saturating with $k_0$, recency with $\rho$, power $\beta$). $\gamma$: boost for messages naming $i$. $J_{ij}$: per-pair coupling. $N$: room size. $B$: senders addressed per turn.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
P(r_{\tau j}=1)=1-\exp\Big(-\theta_{i,d}\textstyle\sum_{m\in P_j(\tau)}h_m\Big)\\
h_m=e^{\gamma\,\mathbf 1[m\to i]}\times\Big\{1,\ \tfrac1k,\ \tfrac{1+k_0}{k_0+k},\ \rho^{\,\mathrm{rank}_m-1},\ k^{-\beta}\Big\}\\
J_{ij}\propto B\,k^{-\beta}\sim B\,N^{-\beta}\quad(\beta=1:\ \text{literal } J/N)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{For operators the usable fact is a scaling rule: in a shared channel, each agent's chance of engaging any one peer falls roughly as $N^{-0.6}$, while what it says per turn stays about the same. Bigger rooms get diluted, not more collaborative. Splitting a channel raises within-room engagement by about $(k$ ratio$)^{0.6}$, which matches \#35's split ($2.05\times$). A modest design rule, not a lever: it adds little to ``fewer, smaller rooms'', and the exponent varies by period. The response signal is shaky. Agents name peers they are already talking with, even ones whose message they couldn't yet see, so mentions are partly conversation state, not uptake. Swarm builders should not tune anything off $\hat\beta$ until a cleaner reply measure confirms it.}

\hbottom{Attention in the village dilutes sub-linearly ($\hat\beta\approx0.6$) and robustly, giving coupling $\propto N^{-0.6}$ rather than a literal $J/N$; recency, conversational engagement and a failed placebo keep it a descriptive scaling law rather than a validated mechanism.}
