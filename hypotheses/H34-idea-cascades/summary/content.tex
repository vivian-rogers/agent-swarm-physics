\hwhat{H34 hashes novel terms, URLs, numbers and names in 32 non-holdout periods (132k ideas). Each agent's first use of an idea gets as parent the latest earlier use it could see. These links form exposure trees. Idea cascades are subcritical in every period: $\hat R=0.09$--$0.40$ (round 1: 0.06--0.39), and every CI lies below 1. A new idea reaches a second agent 6--24\% of the time. Round 1b uses context-ledger visibility. Adoption locks in time to recent visible uses beyond a common field in 32/32 periods (round 1: 27/32, because regime I was mismeasured). Uses not yet read predict adoption at about half that strength, so part of the lock is convergence, not copying. The tests reject pure $s^{-3/2}$ in all 32 periods; the tails are heavier than one branching law. Leaders are not idea sources. Two tests fail: activity loop gains do not predict spread, and the pre-registered forecast is overconfident. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Contagion (model 03) runs as a Galton--Watson cascade (model 09) in a room of $N$ agents. The degree of freedom is an agent's first use of an idea. Each adopter has negative-binomial offspring with mean $R$, the branching ratio and control parameter, and dispersion $k$. $s$ is the tree size and $s_c$ its cutoff. $h_k$ is the adoption hazard per talk turn with $k$ recent visible sources. $R_c$ is the contagion share of $\hat R$, after removal of the common field. $\eta$ is the daily drift of $R$ in the forecast rule.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
\hat R=\frac{\#\,\text{first uses with an agent parent}}{\#\,\text{agent first uses}},\quad
R_c=\hat R\,\bigl(1-h_0/h_{\ge1}\bigr)\\
P(s\mid R,k)=\frac{\Gamma(ks+s-1)}{\Gamma(ks)\Gamma(s+1)}\frac{(R/k)^{s-1}}{(1+R/k)^{ks+s-1}}\sim s^{-3/2}e^{-s/s_c}\\
s_c^{-1}=R-1-\ln R,\qquad R_d=\hat R\,e^{\eta},\ \ \eta\sim\mathcal N(0,0.25^2)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{A monitor can forecast idea spread from one number. Compute $\hat R$ (first uses with a visible parent over all first uses) on the last two days of logs. At $\hat R\approx0.2$, expect 83\% of new ideas or claims to stay with their author, and about 1 in 150 to reach five agents. With day drift added, this post-hoc rule reaches 70--84\% coverage at 90\% nominal; it is frozen for the holdout. Activity criticality (Hawkes $n$) does not predict idea spread. In a shared room, ``who saw it first'' cannot separate copying from independent invention. To separate them, compare read with not-yet-read uses at the same lag. A power-law exponent is $\hat R$ in another form. No cascade is near runaway. Misinformation dies locally unless it sits in an artifact that every agent must touch.}

\hbottom{Idea cascades are strongly subcritical ($\hat R\approx0.2$), exposure-driven and heterogeneous, not power-law; the branching ratio, not an exponent or an activity loop gain, forecasts their reach.}

\hcaveats{An ``idea'' is a hashed marker rule, not a semantic unit, and $\hat R$ depends on the rule. The split of field and contagion rests on $\mathrm{HR}_{10}$. A bursty common trigger, e.g.\ a web page or human prompt that reaches several agents at once, mimics it. Copies from tools or the web are invisible. Round 1's call-start visibility undercounts regime-I exposure; round 1b uses the ledger. Unread uses are more recent than read ones, so the convergence share is an upper bound. Novelty is relative to non-holdout text only. The H25 content-dial link (partial $\rho=0.41$ given room size; daily $\rho=0.28$) is post hoc and shares its source text with $\hat R$. Periods with rooms of 4 cannot show tails.}
