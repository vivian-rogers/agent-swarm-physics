\hwhat{When the scaffold wipes an agent's context window at the 41-record cap (memory kept), does the agent thrash: re-read instead of producing, act more randomly, and listen harder to new room content? I aligned 21,165 forced and 16,357 voluntary resets with no-reset pseudo-erasures over 1.24M model calls in nine non-holdout regime-III periods. A synthetic validation came first. Re-acquisition is real. The read share doubles at the first post-reset call (G51: 0.22 to 0.47). Among non-write calls it stays up over five calls ($\Theta_c=+0.079$, 9/9 periods). Writes fall 26\%, work commits 38\%, real failures rise 1 point. There is no temperature pulse and no extra pull from new room items (RR 0.97), but coupling to pre-reset items drops (ratio 0.60). Erasures break command loops (OR 0.11). Holdout not run.}

\hmodel{Model 02 as a kinetic multinomial spin. Each model call has a category $s_t$ (write, run, read, look, GUI, room read, talk, idle, \ldots). Its update has a baseline field $h^0$, a room drive $h^{\rm room}$, a context-held task field $h^{\rm ctx}(n)$ and a persistence $J(n)$, where $n$ counts calls since the last reset; an erasure sets $n\to0$. $R_{\rm nw}$ is the read share of non-write calls, $c'$ an agent $\times$ previous-category cell, $W$ the write share. Model 04 asks which store restores output.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
P(s_{t+1}{=}c)\propto e^{\,h^0_c+h^{\rm room}_c+h^{\rm ctx}_c(n)+J(n)\mathbb 1[c=s_t]}\\
h^{\rm ctx}(n)=h^{\rm ctx}_\infty\,(1-e^{-n/\ell})\\
\Theta_c=\textstyle\sum_{c'} w_{c'}\,[R_{\rm nw}(\text{post}\,|\,c')-R_{\rm nw}(\text{far}\,|\,c')]\\
\Omega=W_{\rm post}/W_{\rm far}-1,\qquad \Delta V=\textstyle\sum_{k\le20}(W_k-W_{\rm far})
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\hobs{../figures/summary_obs.pdf}{(a) G51: share of non-write calls that re-acquire (solid) and write share (dashed) vs calls from a forced reset, a voluntary reset, or a no-reset boundary. Re-reading doubles at the first call and decays with a $\sim$7-call tail. Writes drop 68\% at the first call and then ramp back. (b) All nine periods: forced and voluntary resets sit at $\Theta_c>0$, $\Omega<0$. Pseudo-erasures sit at $\Theta_c\le0$ and $\Omega>0$, because writes ramp up through every segment.}

\htelos{Useful, and more concrete than the physics. The 41-record cap costs each period 4--9\% of its write calls and 5--11\% of its work commits. After a wipe the agent re-reads, fails a few more commands, and rebuilds its task from its files over 3--7 calls. Memory notes written at the wipe do not help, and it does not turn to the room. Three things an operator can use: the cap length is a cost/benefit dial (it also breaks most command loops); the cheapest recovery hint points the agent at its working files (re-reading them resumes writing 2.6 calls sooner than looking at the screen); and a freshly wiped agent is no easier for other agents to steer. ``Temperature pulse'' did not survive, and ``thrash'' overstates it: the re-reading is orderly.}

\hbottom{A context wipe triggers an orderly re-reading burst and a ${\sim}$26\% write dip that cost 4--11\% of output and break most command loops, but it does not heat the agent up or make it more susceptible to the room.}

\hcaveats{Call categories come from in-memory regex classification of commands and from GUI action types, with no hand-labelled validation. GUI browsing cannot be separated from production. Writes ramp up through every 40-call segment, so the dip size depends on the reference window. $\Theta_c$ conditions on first-order state only. The re-acquisition paths are chosen by the agents, so the path results are observational. Jev's 5-min windows cannot resolve the 1--5-call burst; they label post-reset windows as \emph{more} executing, as H39 saw. G37 is underpowered. Nine periods times about 12 statistics, uncorrected. Holdout not run.}
