\hwhat{The scaffold wipes an agent's context window at the 41-record cap and keeps its memory. Round 1 aligned 21,165 forced resets with no-reset pseudo-erasures over 1.24M calls in nine regime-III periods. The read share doubles at the first post-reset call. Among non-write calls it stays up for five calls ($\Theta_c=+0.079$, 9/9). Writes fall 26\% and command loops break. There is no temperature pulse and no extra pull from the room. Round 2 tested four things. The call classifier holds on 300 blind labels, and $\Theta_c$ on the checked labels is $+0.106$. The sawtooth has two timescales and no linear ramp. A 20-call cap would lose 12\% of output per call. Re-reading is working-set habit, and a wipe does not pay as a loop breaker.}

\hmodel{Model 02 as a kinetic multinomial spin. Each call has a category $s_t$ (write, run, read, look, GUI, room read, talk, idle, \ldots). Its update has a baseline field $h^0$, a context-held task field $h^{\rm ctx}(k)$ and a persistence $J(k)$. Here $k$ counts calls since the last reset; a wipe sets $k\to1$, a quench of the task field. $R(k)$ is the re-acquisition share and $W(k)$ the write share at segment position $k$. $Y(L)$ is the output per call under a cap of $L$ calls, with one summary call per consolidation. Model 04 asks which store restores output.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
P(s_{t+1}{=}c)\propto e^{\,h^0_c+h^{\rm ctx}_c(k)+J(k)\mathbb 1[c=s_t]}\\
y(k)=c+\beta(k{-}1)+A_1e^{-(k-1)/\ell_1}+A_2e^{-(k-1)/\ell_2}\\
Y(L)=\textstyle\sum_{k\le L}W(k)\,/\,(L+1),\qquad \Omega=W_{+1..+10}/W_{\rm ref}-1\\
\Theta_c=\textstyle\sum_{c'} w_{c'}\,[R_{\rm nw}(\text{post}\,|\,c')-R_{\rm nw}(\text{far}\,|\,c')]
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{Keep the context cap at 40 calls or more. In every well-sampled period output per call still rises at 40, and halving the cap to 20 loses about 12\% (per call and per minute). Whether a longer cap would help is not identified. Do not use a forced consolidation to break loops. Most flagged loops are productive repetition, and a wipe costs a looping agent about 0.9 write calls over the next ten calls. After a wipe the agent re-reads the files it was just using, at its normal working-set rate. Re-reading is orderly, not thrash. Quote any cap cost with its reference window: the dip is $-20\%$ to $-32\%$ against pre-reset windows and $-11\%$ against the 40-call cycle mean. The best cap may depend on the model.}

\hbottom{A forced context wipe causes a one-call re-reading spike with an ${\sim}8$-call tail ($\Theta_c=+0.106$ on blind-checked labels) and a $-20\%$ to $-32\%$ write dip; a 20-call cap would yield 12\% less output per call than 40, and wiping does not pay as a loop breaker.}

\hcaveats{The blind labels come from one rater (Claude) using tool names and arguments only. Clicks cannot be split into navigation and production. The two-timescale and ramp tests have power only in G51. Beyond 40 calls the optimum is not identified: regime I/II sessions longer than 40 calls end within about 50, so their late calls carry the end-of-session write burst. Re-open shares use file-path tokens from commands; the artifact-level check is n.s. The stuck-loop and gpt-5 results are post hoc. Agents choose re-acquisition paths. The reserved data are not run.}
