\hwhat{Does agent $i$'s recent message, which $j$ had seen, predict the direction of $j$'s next message beyond $j$'s own past, a 5-direction goal field, the day$\times$room field and everything else $j$ saw? Tested with held-out-day cross-validation against circular-shift nulls on 32 non-holdout goal periods (\#3--\#44). Transfer is real but small: significant in 18/32 periods (15/32 against the stricter within-day null). It needs exposure (seen $>$ unseen in 7/8 multi-room weeks), and the outflow ranking replicates across halves of days (median $\rho=0.38$). It does not find leaders. A single source stands out in 2/18 periods; humans beat the median agent in 4/16. Round 1b (context-ledger exposure, a second embedding model, style removal): transfer replicates (17/32; gte 23/32). At matched short lag, messages $j$ had read beat ones still in flight in 17/17 transfer periods, but in-flight ones carry about a third of the gain (convergence). All three leader tests fail: the elected leader over its real term (rank 5/10), \#35's daily lead designers, and \#44's installed leader (last of 6). Holdout not run.}

\hmodel{Models 11 and 02: a message-level vector autoregression on whitened embeddings with isotropic couplings. $\mathbf z_m$: agent $j$'s message vector after removing a 5-direction goal, kickoff and day$\times$room field. $\mathbf s_{ij}$: decayed sum ($\tau=15$\,min) of $i$'s messages that $j$ had seen. $\mathbf B_{m,c}$: baseline columns (own past, day$\times$room field, other seen senders, human and bot messages). $G_{ij}$: held-out gain (Gaussian transfer entropy). $\Phi$: noise-corrected concentration of outflow.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{gather*}
\mathbf z_m=\textstyle\sum_c\beta_c\mathbf B_{m,c}+\kappa_{ij}\,\mathbf s_{ij}(t_m)+\boldsymbol\xi_m,\qquad
\mathbf s_{ij}=\dfrac{\sum_{x\in\mathrm{seen}}e^{-\Delta t_x/\tau}\,\mathbf z_x}{1+\sum_{x\in\mathrm{seen}}e^{-\Delta t_x/\tau}}\\
G_{ij}=1-\mathrm{SSE}_{\rm full}/\mathrm{SSE}_{\rm base}\ \text{(held-out days)},\qquad \mathrm{TE}_{i\to j}\approx-\tfrac12\ln(1-G_{ij})\\
\mathrm{Out}_i=\langle\Delta G_{ij}\rangle_j,\quad \mathrm{Net}_i=\mathrm{Out}_i-\mathrm{In}_i,\quad
\Phi=\frac{\mathrm{Var}_i(\mathrm{Out})-\overline{\sigma^2_{\rm null}}}{(N-1)\,\overline{\mathrm{Out}}^{\,2}}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-6pt}}

\htelos{For an operator, the honest answer to ``who is driving this swarm?'' is ``not whoever you put in charge''. Two cheap numbers fall out: total content transfer $T$ (is anyone actually listening to anyone?) and whether one source stands out. In this village, formal leaders, elected or fine-tuned, were not content sources. The installed leader was the most-influenced agent in its room, as H02 and H23 also found. Most weeks are distributed, with content flowing among several agents, often the strongest Claude model present. The caveats bite. In one room, a fast responder to outside news looks exactly like a leader. Operators who post little rank low by construction. The top name changes with preprocessing. Use it as a coupling monitor, not an org chart.}

\hbottom{Exposure-gated content transfer between agents is real in about half the goal periods and gives a reproducible outflow ranking, but it does not identify the known leaders: elected, installed and operator sources all rank low.}

\hcaveats{Effects are fractions of a percent of residual variance, and with 32 periods and 13 predictions some passes are chance. In a single room a fast responder is indistinguishable from a leader; only multi-room exposure contrasts and the within-day null address it. The method sees sustained conversational influence, not one-shot agenda setting such as the \#26 goal announcement. Outflow is a total, so low-volume sources (operators) rank low. Transfer depends on how many field directions are removed (25, 18 and 7 significant periods for 1, 5 and 10). The top source's identity agrees in only 13--27 of 32 periods across preprocessing variants, and in 17/32 between embedding models (1b). In regime III, low-volume targets made leave-one-day-out unstable (post-hoc A2). Part of short-lag transfer is two agents answering the same prior turn (1b).}
