\hwhat{H32 asks whether agent $i$'s recent message, seen by $j$, predicts the direction of $j$'s next message. The baseline is $j$'s own past, the fields and the rest of what $j$ saw. Held-out-day cross-validation tests the gain against circular-shift nulls on 32 non-holdout periods (\#3--\#44). Transfer exists but is small: it is significant in 18/32 periods (15/32 against the stricter within-day null). It needs exposure: seen $>$ unseen in 7/8 multi-room weeks. The outflow ranking replicates across halves of days (median $\rho=0.38$). It does not find leaders: a single source stands out in 2/18 periods, and humans beat the median agent in 4/16. Round 1b uses context-ledger exposure, a second embedding model and style removal. Transfer replicates (17/32; gte 23/32). At matched short lag, read messages beat in-flight ones in 17/17 transfer periods. In-flight messages still carry about a third of the gain (convergence). All three leader tests fail: the elected leader over its real term (rank 5/10), \#35's daily lead designers, and \#44's installed leader (last of 6). The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Models 11 and 02 give a message-level vector autoregression on whitened embeddings with isotropic couplings. The state $\mathbf z_m$ is agent $j$'s message vector after removal of a 5-direction goal, kickoff and day$\times$room field. The source $\mathbf s_{ij}$ is the decayed sum ($\tau=15$\,min) of $i$'s messages that $j$ had seen. $\mathbf B_{m,c}$ are the baseline columns: own past, day$\times$room field, other seen senders, human and bot messages. $G_{ij}$ is the held-out gain (Gaussian transfer entropy). The order parameter $\Phi$ is the noise-corrected concentration of outflow.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}
\begin{gather*}
\mathbf z_m=\textstyle\sum_c\beta_c\mathbf B_{m,c}+\kappa_{ij}\,\mathbf s_{ij}(t_m)+\boldsymbol\xi_m,\qquad
\mathbf s_{ij}=\dfrac{\sum_{x\in\mathrm{seen}}e^{-\Delta t_x/\tau}\,\mathbf z_x}{1+\sum_{x\in\mathrm{seen}}e^{-\Delta t_x/\tau}}\\
G_{ij}=1-\mathrm{SSE}_{\rm full}/\mathrm{SSE}_{\rm base}\ \text{(held-out days)},\qquad \mathrm{TE}_{i\to j}\approx-\tfrac12\ln(1-G_{ij})\\
\mathrm{Out}_i=\langle\Delta G_{ij}\rangle_j,\quad \mathrm{Net}_i=\mathrm{Out}_i-\mathrm{In}_i,\quad
\Phi=\frac{\mathrm{Var}_i(\mathrm{Out})-\overline{\sigma^2_{\rm null}}}{(N-1)\,\overline{\mathrm{Out}}^{\,2}}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-6pt}}

\htelos{Do not assume that the agent you put in charge drives the content. In this village, formal leaders, elected or fine-tuned, are not content sources. The installed leader is the most-influenced agent in its room, as H02 and H23 also find. Most weeks are distributed: content flows among several agents, often the strongest Claude model present. A monitor can compute total transfer $T$, which shows whether agents take content from each other, and $\Phi$, which shows whether one source stands out. In one room, a fast responder to outside news looks the same as a leader. The top name changes with the embedding model (17/32 agree). Use $T$ as a coupling monitor, not as an org chart.}

\hbottom{Exposure-gated content transfer exists in about half the periods and gives a reproducible ranking, but elected, installed and operator leaders all rank low.}

\hcaveats{The effects are fractions of a percent of residual variance. With 32 periods and 13 predictions, some passes are chance. In one room, a fast responder and a leader look the same; only the multi-room exposure contrasts and the within-day null address this. The method measures sustained conversational influence, not one-shot agenda setting such as the \#26 goal announcement. Outflow is a total, so low-volume sources (operators) rank low. Transfer depends on the number of field directions removed: 25, 18 and 7 significant periods for 1, 5 and 10. The top source agrees in only 13--27 of 32 periods across preprocessing variants, and in 17/32 across embedding models (1b). In regime III, low-volume targets make leave-one-day-out unstable (post-hoc A2). Part of the short-lag transfer is two agents that answer the same prior turn (1b).}
