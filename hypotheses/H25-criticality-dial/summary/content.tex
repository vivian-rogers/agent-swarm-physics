\hwhat{Built a reusable daily dial: the Curie--Weiss loop gain $g=1-1/\mathrm{VR}$ and $T/T_c=1/g$, per day and channel, from logs, with platform stalls masked, slow drives removed and calibrated 90\% intervals. Ran it on 282 non-holdout days (35 periods). \textbf{Activity and talk are far from criticality on every day:} median $g=0.10$ and $0.12$ ($T/T_c\approx10$, 8), no upper bound reaching 0.8. The dial reproduces H19 per period (activity $\rho=0.94$) and sits above the Hawkes-mapped gain in 80\% of periods. \textbf{The content dial reads near-critical} (median 0.70; 33 days with lower bound $>0.8$), so the headline prediction P1 failed. Post hoc, that reading is roster size times a constant per-pair correlation ($\bar\rho\approx0.28$), the signature of a shared field. Day-to-day variation is mostly noise (P5 failed). In simulated swarms the dial misses coupling delayed by minutes. Holdout script frozen, not run.}

\hmodel{Models 01 (Curie--Weiss), 09 (Hawkes), 11 (O($n$) spins). $s_i=\pm1$: agent $i$ active, or posting, in a minute; for content, $s_i$ is its 30-min mean of whitened unit message embeddings ($n=32$). $h_i(t)$: private fields (schedule, goal), removed by 30-min block means and agent-day means; stalls masked. $q_i=1-m_i^2$; $g$: loop gain; VR: collective over independent variance; $\bar\rho$: mean pairwise correlation; $N$: agents that day.}

\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}\begin{gather*}
H=-\tfrac{J_0}{2N}\Big(\textstyle\sum_i s_i\Big)^2-\sum_i h_i(t)\,s_i,\qquad g=\beta J_0\,\bar q\\
\delta M=\frac{\sum_j q_j\beta\,\delta h_j}{1-g},\qquad \mathrm{VR}=\frac{\operatorname{Var}\sum_i s_i}{\sum_i\operatorname{Var}s_i}=\frac{1}{1-g}\\
g=1-\mathrm{VR}^{-1},\qquad T/T_c=1/g\ \ (h=0)\\
\mathrm{VR}-1=(N-1)\,\bar\rho\ \Rightarrow\ g(N)=\frac{(N-1)\bar\rho}{1+(N-1)\bar\rho}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-10pt}}

\htelos{The useful part is a calibrated instrument and two warnings. Any operator can compute the dial from event logs with \texttt{compute\_dial}. In this village it says the swarm amplifies a nudge by about 10\%: no fast runaway. Warning 1: a green dial does not rule out slow herding. The village's coupling acts through context read minutes later, which the 1-min dial cannot see, as shown on simulated swarms. Warning 2: $g$ rises with headcount at fixed per-pair correlation. ``Near-critical'' content just means agents in one chat share a topic, and adding agents pushes any field-driven channel toward $g=1$. Track $\bar\rho$, not $g$, weekly, not daily. As a criticality alarm it is mostly reassurance plus a lower bound.}

\hbottom{A daily Curie--Weiss loop-gain dial is computable from logs with honest intervals and shows activity and talk far from criticality ($g\approx0.1$), but it is blind to delayed coupling, mostly noise day to day, and its near-critical content reading is swarm size times a shared-field correlation.}

\hcaveats{The 1-min dial is a lower bound on the steady-state loop gain: context-mediated coupling is invisible. The content dial is an upper bound: shared time-varying fields survive, and the flat per-pair correlation fits a field. $g$ depends on $N$, so cross-period contrasts (including the regime-III rise in activity) are partly roster size. Intervals are widened $\times1.5$ by a synthetic calibration; days with 3--4 agents still get optimistic SEs and pull fixed-effect means. H38's scheduled-off mask halves the activity dial (0.10$\to$0.05). One embedding model. Not blind to H19 and H03 values; the size-scaling result and the F2tod variant are post hoc.}
