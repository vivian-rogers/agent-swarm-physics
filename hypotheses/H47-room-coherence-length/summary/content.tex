\hwhat{HH172 claimed rooms set the swarm's coherence length: content co-moves inside a room but not across, so a per-room topic detector beats the swarm one, and one room leads at goal changes. We measured per-pair content correlation within ($\rho_w$) and across ($\rho_c$) rooms in 30-min windows on 9 multi-room periods, against room-relabel nulls. Then three native tests: NE42's merge/split, \#41's identical instructions, and \#51's \#focus room. Synthetic worlds came first. Rooms bound coherence: $C_B=\rho_c/\rho_w\le0.3$ in 6/9 periods (median 0.23). Merging \#best and \#rest merged their content fluctuations; splitting restored the boundary (DiD 1.0, $p$ 0.001). But inside rooms, correlation sits in pairs that talk (and the post hoc boundary sharpness tracks topic separation). No per-room detector gain (2 clean events). No leader: both rooms switch within minutes. \textbf{Mixed.} Holdout script written, not run.}

\htelos{A useful, if unsurprising, design rule: rooms are content firewalls only when they do different things. Same task in two rooms shares about half of each room's content fluctuation; different tasks share almost none. Merging rooms merges coherence within days, and splitting restores it at once, so the chat channel matters (NE42). Inside a big room, coherence lives in conversation pairs, so a 25-agent room is not one unit to monitor. The two dynamic claims failed cleanly. Goal changes hit every room within minutes, so there is no leader to watch. A per-room topic alarm adds nothing to H36's swarm alarm at the events we have. Caveat for steering: $C_B$ cannot say whether rooms couple or are just driven by their tasks.}

\hbottom{Content coherence in the village stops at room boundaries when rooms work on different things and moves with the chat channel at merges and splits, but inside rooms it follows conversation, and no room leads a goal change.}
