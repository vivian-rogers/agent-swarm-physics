\hwhat{H22 finds that rival agents in \#51 co-move in \emph{topic}, so topic cannot show conflict. H37 tests whether \emph{stance} shows it. A zero-shot labeller (Jev) marks 30k reply pairs in \#12, \#26, \#40 and \#51 (B to A: agree, support, neutral, oppose, undermine; \$1.12). Blind validation gives $\kappa_{\rm sign}=0.67$ (random) and 0.37 (enriched). In the \#12 debates, stance separates opponents from teammates (AUC 0.74 after agent effects), and topic does not (0.48). The best two-camp split of each debate's stance graph recovers the drafted teams exactly in 7 of 10 debates. The contrast vanishes within 10 min of the verdict. Elsewhere there is little to find. \#51 rivals and opposed roles are as friendly as anyone, and \#26 stance ignores ballots. \#51's 10 antagonistic pairs (vs 0.33 expected) sit on norm-enforcing roles (post hoc). The \#34 saboteur test is written, \textbf{not run}.}

\hmodel{Models 01 (signed Ising) and 10 (Potts communities). The spin is a bond: the stance $s_e\in[-1,1]$ of reply $e$ from $j$ to $i$, from Jev probabilities. $a_j$ and $b_i$ are agent fields (agreeableness, likability) of an ordered logit with cut points $c_k$. $\Delta$ is the camp coupling. $J_{ij}$ is the residual pair stance after the fields, and $\xi_i=\pm1$ is the camp. Factions are the ground state of $H(\xi)$; frustration $f$ is the weight it leaves unsatisfied. $\gamma$ is same-team ($\delta^{\rm team}_{ij}=1$) minus opposite-team stance, with fields absorbed.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\sigma_e\le k)=\operatorname{logistic}\!\big(c_k-a_j-b_i-\Delta\,\xi_i\xi_j\big),\quad \sigma_e\in\{-,0,+\}\\
s_e=p_{\rm agree}+p_{\rm support}-p_{\rm oppose}-p_{\rm undermine}\\
s_e=\mu+a_j+b_i+\gamma\,\delta^{\rm team}_{ij}+\varepsilon_e\\
H(\boldsymbol\xi)=-\textstyle\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}\,\xi_i\xi_j\\
f=\tfrac12\Big(1-\max_{\boldsymbol\xi}\big[-H(\boldsymbol\xi)\big]\big/\textstyle\sum_{i<j}|J_{ij}|\Big)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{To find assigned sides or a scapegoated agent, run a stance labeller on replies. It costs about \$1.12 per 30k reply pairs and recovers debate teams exactly in 7/10 debates. Check every camp alarm against the agent-field (ordered-logit) null, which holds 4\% false alarms; the sign-shuffle null fires falsely in 28--100\%. In cooperative weeks the detector stays quiet. LLM agents almost never take sides unless a protocol tells them to: rivalry, opposed incentives and elections all come out polite. So the detector mostly confirms that nothing happens. Whether it catches covert sabotage is the open \#34 question. Do not monitor the raw ``negative reply'' rate alone: it is mostly correction friction plus label noise.}

\hbottom{Stance labels see the conflict a debate assigns (teams 7/10, topic blind), but rival, opposed and voting agents show none: conflict lives in stance only where imposed.}

\hcaveats{The test has one labeller and one rater. The analyst's blind labels define truth, and Jev's ``oppose'' is only 30\% precise. Debate rebuttals often address the other team, not a named agent, so the reply graph undersamples them. Graphs outside \#51 are small (7--14 agents). Balance indices are unstable and are not used. \#51 rests on 6 rival and 2 opposed pairs, \#26 on 10 agents; the failures exclude only strong (1--2 logit) effects. The calibrated null (Amendment 2) and the governance-friction reading come after the data. The topic baseline uses one embedding model.}
