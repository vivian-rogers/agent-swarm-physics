\hwhat{The claim is that content settles after a kickoff once every agent has read every other agent. The settling time is then a bulk mixing time on the read-out graph, not whole-graph $\lambda_2$. The DQ1 context ledger gives two read-out times per period. $T_{90}$ is the time until 90\% of room-mate pairs have read each other; $t_{\rm mix}$ is the mixing time of the read-out chain. The test compares both with the kickoff-remanence settling time $\tau$ (1-h bins, two embedding models) on 27 non-holdout periods. Native tests: NE42, \#38, \#51 joins, NE32. Coverage takes minutes ($T_{90}$ median 0.24 active h). Settling takes hours ($\tau$ median 4.5 h, about $10\times$). Neither predictor beats a constant (LOPO gains $-0.01$ to $-0.14$). But $\tau$ is detectable in only 11/27 periods, and a noise-calibrated synthetic has no power. \textbf{Inconclusive}; the shallow-coverage version fails on magnitude. The holdout script exists and is not run.}

\hmodel{Vector spins (model 11) that update only at read-out calls. Agent $i$'s content $x_i\in\mathbb{R}^{32}$ is its whitened statement mean. The kickoff quenches it toward the target $\hat k$ (H54). At each call, $x_i$ moves toward the messages that newly entered its context (ledger batch $B$), with weight $\alpha$. $W_{ij}$ counts $j$'s messages read by $i$ per active hour; $u_i$ is the reading-turn rate; $Q$ is the read-out chain. Rivals: a field clock, the own-call clock, the day clock and $\lambda_2$ (H31).}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
x_i\leftarrow(1-\alpha)\,x_i+\alpha\,\langle x_{s(m)}\rangle_{m\in B},\qquad Q_{ij}=u_i\,\frac{W_{ij}}{\sum_k W_{ik}}-\delta_{ij}u_i\\
C_k(t)=\Pr_{(i,j)}\big[n_{ij}(t)\ge k\big],\qquad t^{\rm bulk}_{\rm mix}=\operatorname{med}_i\min\{t:\mathrm{TV}(e_ie^{Qt},e_ie^{Q\infty})\le\tfrac14\}\\
A_{\rm ex}(t)=\langle\cos(v_i,\hat k)\rangle_i-\langle\cos(v_i,\hat k_q)\rangle_{i,q}=A_\infty+(A_0-A_\infty)e^{-t/\tau}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{Reading is not the bottleneck in broadcast rooms. Every agent reads every room-mate within minutes of a kickoff ($T_{90}$ 0.24 active h), yet content relaxes for hours. Do not merge rooms or raise call rates to speed convergence on a new goal. If read-out drives settling at all, each read moves an agent by about 1/250. Coverage times from the ledger are cheap, but one silent agent sets a whole room's coverage. The scaling question stays open: per-period settling times are too noisy and too varied in shape to rank 27 periods.}

\hbottom{Agents read each other within minutes but content settles over hours; the noise is too high to test scaling with a slower read-out time.}

\hcaveats{The settling estimate is fragile. A single exponential fits in about half the periods. The rest show a fast drop then recovery, multi-day declines, or no kickoff signal (\#51's private goals). H20's day-scale $\tau_q$ exists only in 10 long periods. The bge and gte detected sets overlap in 9 of 16 periods. Coverage counts chat messages only, not artifacts. Regime-I/II read times use low-confidence call starts. The analysis looks at 19 predictors $\times$ 2 models; only P1 has multiplicity control, so the bge leads are not tests. The real-like calibration comes after the data.}
