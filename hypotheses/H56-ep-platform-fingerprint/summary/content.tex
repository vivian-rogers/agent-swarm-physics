\hwhat{The claim: the entropy production (EP) of agents' turn-level action chains jumps at scaffold changes (prompts, tools, harness), not at goal, roster or room changes. EP would then flag undocumented platform changes. The estimator is a within-agent, count-matched EP change over 3+3 days. It runs at 107 dated events (changelog, NE catalog, kickoffs, joins, rooms) and at every day for blind change-point detection. It runs on all records and on an agent-only chain without scaffold records. Synthetic chains come first: the estimator is calibrated and detects $\pm$50\% changes. On real data, scaffold changes do not stand out (0/17 tool-change hits; class $p=0.23$). Neither does NE14b ($-28\%$, $t=-1.3$), nor the oracle swap (NE40), dated here to 04-20 from answer style ($t=-0.2$). Goal kickoffs move the agent-only chain more ($p=0.007$). Family differences survive scaffold removal. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Kinetic Potts (model 02), read through model 05. Each agent is a Markov chain on H14's 11 action classes (or 6 coarse states). Its EP per transition is the pair-KL between forward and reversed transitions. The AIK bound on antisymmetric transition indicators $g_{ab}$ estimates it (Newton step, cross-fitted). H56 treats goal, roster and room changes as fields that move occupancy (potentials $U$). It treats scaffold changes as changes in the cycle forces $F$, so only scaffold changes move $\Sigma$.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
\Sigma=\sum_{a,b}\pi_aP_{ab}\ln\frac{\pi_aP_{ab}}{\pi_bP_{ba}},\qquad \ln\frac{P_{ab}}{P_{ba}}=F_{ab}+U_a-U_b\\
\hat\Sigma_N=\tfrac{2}{k(k-1)}\textstyle\sum_{f\neq f'}\bar g_f^{\top}K^{-1}\bar g_{f'},\quad g_{ab}=\mathbb 1[a\!\to\! b]-\mathbb 1[b\!\to\! a]\\
\Delta_i=\hat\Sigma_i(\text{post})-\hat\Sigma_i(\text{pre})\ \ \text{(both sides subsampled to }m_i=\min n\text{)}\\
t=\bar\Delta/\mathrm{SE}(\Delta_i),\qquad f_+=\#\{\Delta_i>0\}/n
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{Do not monitor behavioral EP for silent platform changes. It tracks the work: goal kickoffs move it more than tool changes do, and families differ by tool-use style, not by harness. Monitor plain log features instead: event-type mix, tool-output format and schema fields. These features find four undocumented changes. They are the answerer swap (04-20), a search-tool schema change (07-29), a two-day search malfunction and the end of the bookends (08-04). EP stays a descriptor of how directed the agents' tool workflows are. One post hoc lead needs a holdout check: at scaffold changes, agents' EP moves in the same direction more often than on random days.}

\hbottom{Action-chain entropy production does not fingerprint the platform: scaffold changes, even NE14b and the undocumented oracle swap, sit inside the work-driven day-to-day spread.}

\hcaveats{Changes under about 30\% of fine-chain EP are invisible, and coarse chains are blind; NE14b's $-28\%$ sits at the edge. The event catalog is dense and confounded: only 1 tool change, 2 prompt changes and 5 kickoffs have clean windows. Changelog dates are merge dates. Providers can respond in opposite directions (NE14b), and the mean-change $t$ dilutes that. Amendment 3 repairs, after the data, a placebo pool too small to give $p<0.11$; no verdict depends on it. NE40's date comes from post hoc stylometry. All values are lower bounds (non-Markov chains). The holdout is not run.}
