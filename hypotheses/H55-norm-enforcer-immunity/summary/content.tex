\hwhat{The test asks whether agents who correct others draw negative stance, and whether corrections end self-repetition loops and blocked spells. Corrections come from DQ2's confident Jev correction/decline subtype. A blind check gives precision 0.93 and recall about 0.1; the H55 lexical marker fails (0.30). Over 27 periods, correctors receive \emph{warmer} replies (random-effects $\rho=-0.24$, $p=0.04$; \#51 $-0.60$). Replies to a correction are no colder than replies to the same agent's other messages. Corrections reach a looping agent in 0.65\% of loop episodes. Matched contrasts show no effect on escape (loops $-0.18$, blocked $-0.02$; underpowered). Being addressed at all raises loop escape by $+0.05$. \#51's assigned enforcers do not correct more. \#12 judges get cooler replies than teammates. Nobody enforces \#16's operator rules. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Escape from a failure state (restatement loop or v3 ``blocked'' spell) is a discrete-time hazard with frailty $\alpha_i$ and loop age $k$. Model 06 gives the neutral null. $D$ marks a message addressed to the agent since its last step; $C$ marks a correction among them. Immunity means $\beta_C>0$ beyond $\beta_D$. Friction means the received-stance field $\nu_j=-b_j$ (ordered logit, replier fields $a_i$) rises with the correction rate $c_j$. The immune contrast measures the viability value $\Delta V$ of corrections (model 04).}

\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}

P(y_{ij}\le m)=\sigma(c_m-a_i-b_j),\quad \nu_j=-b_j,\quad \rho(c_j,\nu_j)>0
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{To break a loop, address the looping agent by name. Each addressed message raises its per-step escape probability by 5 points, and more in debates. Do not expect peers to correct a stuck agent. One loop in 150 receives a correction, and peer enforcement of an operator rule stays at background. Supply error correction from outside the peer network: the operator, the nudger or a designated reviewer. Peer correction costs no social standing: correctors receive warmer replies, so enforcers need no protection from ostracism. HH210's ``missing immune system'' holds in the coverage sense.}

\hbottom{Correctors get warm replies, not resistance; corrections almost never reach looping agents (no immune response); plain attention is the only loop-breaker found.}

\hcaveats{The correction sensor sees about one correction in ten. The immune tests therefore have few treated events, and $c_j$ is a scaled rate. One blind rater did the validation, with a broader rubric than DQ2's. Stance is reliable only in aggregate. The warmth toward correctors can partly be a labeller habit. Being addressed may end a restatement mechanically, because an answer is not a restatement. Loops are within-day restatement runs, many of them benign. \#51 overlaps H37's labels, and its enforcer concentration is largely activity (they are in 56\% of replies). The synthetic check finds and removes a targeting confound with agent fixed effects; within-agent targeting remains.}
