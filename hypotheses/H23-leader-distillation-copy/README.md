## Model
**From:** `physics-models/08-copying-vs-transformation` (I(X;Y) = I_copy + I_transform), `physics-models/04-semantic-information`, `11-vector-spins` (embedding-level comparisons).

**H23 variant: copy information (distillation variant).** A channel from the corpus (X) to the leader's live output (Y) over a shared alphabet, at three levels of coarse-graining:
- **phrase level:** the alphabet is word n-grams (agent names → `<agent>`, numbers → `<num>`, URLs/URIs/hashes masked). The copy event is "an n-gram the speaker emits is one of the corpus's n-grams". Per n-gram, the leader's copy probability a is compared with the baseline b at which speakers who were *not* trained on the corpus emit corpus n-grams in the same room and window. Specific copy information in Kolchinsky & Corominas-Murtra's binary form is d(a‖b) (binary KL, bits per n-gram) when a > b, else 0. Corpus-*distinctive* n-grams (vs the base field) isolate what distillation could add.
- **plan level:** the alphabet is 10 plan acts (DECIDE, ASSIGN, GATE, REDIRECT, ESCALATE, REQUEST, REPORT, ACK, STANDBY, OTHER), coded by fixed keyword rules (`analysis/h23lib.py: plan_acts`, priority order as listed). For each live message m, X is the plan act of the corpus rows whose scenario is nearest to m's context (k = 3 nearest snippets in bge space, soft), Y is m's plan act; p(x, y) = mean_m p_C(x | m) δ(y, y_m). I, I_copy, I_transform per K&CM (`h23lib.kc_joint`, checked against H07's `mi_parts`), plus copy fraction c = Σ_x p(x, x) and κ.
- **embedding level:** no shared discrete alphabet, so no K&CM decomposition; used for "closer to corpus or base" only.

Three outcomes are distinguished at the plan level: **copied** (excess c and I_copy > null), **transformed** (I_transform > null with no excess copy: plans depend on the corpus/situation but systematically differ), **not transmitted** (I ≈ null: plans neither copy nor depend on the corpus). HH39's "transforms its plans" is the second.

## Observables
All computed identically on G44 (exploratory) and #45 (confirmatory). **Units:** chat messages. **Leader:** the 16 messages of kimi-leader-v7-aug-64 in #44 (05-29 18:24:51–20:27 UTC; sensitivity: + the 2 messages of the sibling Kimi checkpoints). **Same-window controls:** every other agent message in #best from 05-29 18:24:51 to 21:05 UTC (Opus 4.7 8, Kimi K2.6 6, GPT-5.5 5, Gemini 3.5 Flash 10, Opus 4.8 16). **Base field B:** Kimi K2.6's chat messages in non-holdout regime-III periods (#38–#42, #44; the leader's window excluded). **Corpus C:** the 31 recovered v7-aug-64 targets (sensitivity: + 18 unplaced samples). **Context of m:** the 3 messages before m in the room by speakers other than m's author.

- **O1 phrase-level copy.** (a) Per message, corpus n-gram copy fraction c_m^(n) (n = 1 content words, 2, 3), each reference set equalized to the corpus's token count. (b) Per message, rate of corpus-distinctive n-grams (n ≤ 2; Monroe log-odds with informative Dirichlet prior, corpus vs base field, z ≥ 1.96; selected from C and B only) per 100 tokens. (c) Pooled lexical copy information d(a‖b), b from the same-window controls.
- **O2 plan level.** (a) Plan-act profile (primary act and multi-label rates) of leader, corpus, same-window controls, base field, contexts; directive share = P(primary ∈ {DECIDE, ASSIGN, GATE, REDIRECT}); JSD between profiles. (b) Corpus → leader K&CM channel (nearest-snippet pairing): c_plan, κ_plan, I, I_copy, I_transform, each minus a shuffle null (Y permuted, 2,000 draws), and the snippet-match similarity (coverage). (c) Conversational channel (context → response): X = primary act of the most recent message by someone else, Y = the speaker's primary act; c, κ, I_copy, I_transform (shuffle-corrected) for leader, Kimi K2.6, village, and the corpus itself (last snippet line → target).
- **O3 embedding level.** (a) Per message, d_m = cos(z_m, z̄_C) − cos(z_m, z̄_B) in regime-III whitened space (32-d). (b) Field level: day-field-removed agent fields (z minus the room-window mean of other speakers); invariance check of the Kimi K2.6 field across periods (split-half cosine vs the cross-agent baseline, as H13 a3); then cos(h_L, h_C) vs cos(h_L, h_K). (c) Context similarity cos(z_m, z̄_context(m)).
- **Descriptive:** leader's own plan statements (`intentions`: CONSOLIDATE nextSessionGoal), the Qwen v10 messages (different base and corpus), sibling checkpoints' offline outputs as a "student on training-format prompts" bridge.

## Null / baseline
- **N0, no distillation-specific copy:** the leader's corpus proximity (O1, O3a) equals that of same-window speakers who were not trained on the corpus; the corpus then only shares the room's vocabulary. Tested by permuting speaker labels among same-window messages (one-sided, 10,000 draws).
- **N1 = rival R1, the leader just echoes its base model:** leader − Kimi K2.6 differences ≈ 0 on O1b, O2a and O3a (same window), and the leader's field equals Kimi's field (O3b).
- **N2 = rival R2, the leader just echoes the latest chat:** the leader's plan act copies its context (O2c c and I_copy at least the village's), its lexical copy from the context exceeds its copy from the corpus, and its plan profile is closer to its contexts' profile than to the corpus's.
- **Shuffle nulls** for every information quantity (Y permuted; pairing permuted). Plug-in MI is strongly biased at these sample sizes (see the synthetic validation), so only null-corrected values are interpreted.

## Prediction
*Written 2026-10-03, before any statistic was computed on real data, but after reading the full #44 #best transcript (needed for the pipeline reconstruction above), which contains the leader's 27 messages. The #44 test is therefore not blind; it is exploratory. The blind test is #45.*

What H23 predicts for the 16 v7-aug messages in #44, with the exploratory decision rule: **supported** if the point estimate is in the predicted direction and one-sided p < 0.10; **failed** if the point estimate is in the opposite direction; otherwise **inconclusive**.

- **P1 vocabulary is copied, beyond the room and the base.** (a) The leader's mean corpus-distinctive n-gram rate (O1b) exceeds the same-window controls' (pooled; p < 0.10), and exceeds Kimi K2.6's. (b) Its mean corpus bigram copy fraction c^(2) (O1a) exceeds the controls'. *Against:* leader ≤ controls on O1b.
- **P2 plans are not copied but transformed.** (a) Directive share (O2a) of the leader ≤ the corpus's − 0.25, and JSD(leader, corpus) above the 95th percentile of JSD between the corpus profile and n = 16 resamples of corpus rows. (b) In the corpus → leader channel (O2b), excess plan copy c_plan − c_null ≤ 0.10, and I_transform − null > 0 (p < 0.10). *Against "transformed":* (b) I_transform within the null, which reads as "not transmitted"; *against "not copied":* directive share within 0.10 of the corpus and c_plan − c_null ≥ 0.20.
- **P3 the leader is closer to the corpus than its base is (exception (b)).** (a) Mean d_m (O3a) of the leader > Kimi K2.6's same-window mean and > the controls' (pooled; p < 0.10). (b) The Kimi K2.6 field passes the split-half invariance check (cos(h_K^A, h_K^B) above every cross-agent cos(h_K^A, h_g^B)); if it fails, P3c is not evaluated. (c) cos(h_L, h_C) > cos(h_L, h_K).
- **P4 rivals.** R1 is rejected if P1a and P3a hold. R2 is rejected if the leader's conversational copy fraction (O2c) and its lexical context copy are ≤ the village's and its plan profile is closer to the corpus than to its contexts.

**Expected power and credences.** With 16 messages, only large effects are detectable (see the synthetic validation): P2b's transformation test is expected to be underpowered at n = 16 and is decisive only in #45 (70 messages). Credences after the non-blind reading: P1 0.4, P2a 0.8, P2b-transform 0.1, P3a 0.45; R2 at least as good as H23 on plans, 0.7. Predictions for #45 will be frozen in `analysis/confirm_g45.py` after the #44 results.

### Main findings
1. **The "village corpus" is mostly the base model talking to itself.** 80% of the deployed training targets are base Kimi K2.6's replies to a leadership prompt on 20 synthetic scenarios; the rest were written by the #best agents. Village content entered as scenario context, names, jargon and a keyword-based curation score. This makes the base-echo rival the default, and the clean contrast is leader vs base Kimi in the same room.
2. **Copy, in Kolchinsky's sense, is weak and lexical.** Pooled lexical copy information over the room is 0.012 bits per content word [0.001, 0.029] and 0 for bigrams and trigrams. The leader's excess corpus vocabulary is mostly addressing style (agent names, @-mentions) and markup (bold, dashes), about a fifth of the offline student's excess (14.2 − 10.0 vs 33.1 − 10.0 per 100 tokens). The surface format of the leadership prompt survived deployment; phrasing did not.
3. **Plans were not transmitted, which is different from transformed.** The corpus is 100% directive (decide, assign, gate, redirect), and so are the student's offline outputs. Live, the leader's acts are check-ins, status reports, a goal proposal and one assignment round. Its plan act does not depend on which corpus scenario its situation resembles (no excess copy, no transformation information), and live situations barely resemble the corpus's crises. Its plan profile tracks the room (JSD 0.15 to its contexts vs 0.35 to the corpus).
4. **Leader-like, not corpus-like.** Hand-coded, the leader asks for status more than anyone (6/16 check-ins) and makes 4 decisions; followers mostly report (29/39). So it did take a leadership role, but with a profile the corpus never showed it.
5. **Embedding space sees topic, not distillation.** Leader, room and even the offline student sit at similar corpus-vs-base positions; the base field is not distinctive across periods (invariance fails), so "closer to corpus than base" cannot be asked at the field level here.

### Caveats
- **Thin data.** 16 messages in one 2-hour afternoon, in a period when the leader was also confused about its own identity (3 messages narrate its own screen-mirror loop) and doing hands-on work (89 bash turns). It had not yet been given accounts or the floor for a full goal. #45 (70 messages, a full leader week) is the real test.
- **Corpus recovery is partial and biased:** 31/64 rows, validation-heavy (14/14), with duplicate-loop and memory buckets missing. Robust to adding the 18 unplaced base-Kimi samples.
- **Plan coder is moderate (κ ≈ 0.4–0.5) and frozen.** Its known failure on the leader (status lines about a blocked push or pending tests coded GATE) inflates the leader's directive share; the post hoc, non-blind hand coding goes the other way (0.25) and strengthens P2a. Plan-level K&CM values carry coder noise, which attenuates both copy and transformation (synthetic part A).
- **The situation-matched channel cannot separate copying from situational co-occurrence by I_transform alone** (synthetic: perfect copying gives significant I_transform at n = 70). Transformation is claimed only with zero excess copy, calibrated against the perfect-copy reference. The plain shuffle null over-corrects I_transform under strong copying (also seen by H07).
- **Topic confounds the base-field contrasts.** Kimi K2.6's field messages (#38–#44) are about other topics; the same-window Kimi (6 messages) is the matched base. Base-distinctive markers: leader 4.3 vs Kimi field 10.9 per 100 tokens (p < 0.001) but ≈ controls (4.7) and same-window Kimi (5.5).
- **Non-blind #44.** I read the full #best transcript, including the leader's messages, before writing the predictions (needed for the pipeline reconstruction); predictions were dated before any statistic. Analysis changes after data: none to the observables; one bug fix (plan acts in the message table had been built with coder v1; rebuilt with the frozen v3 before the reported run) and the perfect-copy reference added to the corpus channel after the synthetic run showed it was needed (before the real run).
- **Copy information** uses H07's unverified reading of Kolchinsky & Corominas-Murtra; it matches my own recollection of the paper (specific copy information as a binary KL between the probability of reproducing x and the baseline probability of x), but the PDF is still not in `literature/`.
