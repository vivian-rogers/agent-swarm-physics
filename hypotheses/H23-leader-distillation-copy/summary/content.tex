\hwhat{From the terminal logs, the deployed leader (agent 28 in \#44, agent 30 in \#45) is base Kimi K2.6 with a LoRA on 64 rows. 80\% of them are Kimi's own replies to 20 synthetic village scenarios under a leadership prompt; no village chat was a target. We compare its 16 live \#44 messages with 45 same-window messages (exploratory, not blind). Corpus-distinctive vocabulary is 14.2 vs 10.0 per 100 tokens ($p=0.011$), mostly agent addressing and markup. Phrase copy is not significant; lexical copy information is 0.012 bits per word. The directive share of plans is 0.62 (hand-coded 0.25) vs 1.00 in the corpus. Excess plan copy is $-0.04$ against a perfect-copy 0.23, and transformation information sits at the null. Plans are \emph{not transmitted}, not transformed. In embeddings, it is no closer to the corpus than to its base; the base-echo and latest-chat rivals stand. The \#45 test is written and \textbf{not run}.}

\hmodel{Copying vs transformation (08, Kolchinsky \& Corominas-Murtra), distillation variant. The channel runs from the corpus $X$ to the leader's live output $Y$. The shared alphabet is word n-grams or 10 keyword-coded plan acts. Each live message $m$ pairs with the corpus rows whose scenario is nearest its context, $p_C(x\mid m)$. $d$: binary KL. $r_x$: probability of reproducing $x$. $a,b$: rates at which the leader and untrained same-room speakers emit corpus n-grams. $c$: copy fraction.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
I(X;Y)=I_{\text{copy}}+I_{\text{transform}}\\
I_{\text{copy}}=\textstyle\sum_x p(x)\,d\big(r_x\,\|\,p_Y(x)\big)\,\mathbf{1}[r_x>p_Y(x)]\\
p(x,y)=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_m p_C(x\mid m)\,\delta_{y,y_m},\quad c=\textstyle\sum_x p(x,x)\\
I^{\text{lex}}_{\text{copy}}=d(a\,\|\,b)\ \text{bits per n-gram}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{If you distill a leader model from a few dozen synthetic scenarios, expect the surface style to transfer and the decision policy not to. Here the leader uses 14.2 corpus-distinctive tokens per 100 vs 10.0 in the room. Its plans do not transfer, because live routine work looks nothing like the crisis scenarios. Cover the deployment situations in the training set. That coverage failure is standard ML, not a swarm result. The decomposition adds one distinction (not transmitted vs transformed) and a perfect-copy reference, but 16 messages and a coder at $\kappa\approx0.45$ measure little.}
