\hmodel{Model 08 (Kolchinsky \& Corominas-Murtra), distillation variant: a channel from the corpus $X$ to the leader's live output $Y$ on a shared alphabet (word n-grams, or 10 keyword-coded plan acts). Live message $m$ is paired with the corpus rows whose scenario is nearest its context, $p_C(x\mid m)$. $d$: binary KL; $r_x$: probability of reproducing $x$; $a,b$: rates at which the leader and untrained same-room speakers emit corpus n-grams; $c$: copy fraction.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
I(X;Y)=I_{\text{copy}}+I_{\text{transform}}\\
I_{\text{copy}}=\textstyle\sum_x p(x)\,d\big(r_x\,\|\,p_Y(x)\big)\,\mathbf{1}[r_x>p_Y(x)]\\
p(x,y)=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_m p_C(x\mid m)\,\delta_{y,y_m},\quad c=\textstyle\sum_x p(x,x)\\
I^{\text{lex}}_{\text{copy}}=d(a\,\|\,b)\ \text{bits per n-gram}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{For anyone distilling a swarm into a leader model, the lesson is mundane: SFT on a few dozen synthetic crisis scenarios carried the surface style (naming agents, markup, terse format) into deployment but not the decision policy, because live situations (routine project work) looked nothing like the training scenarios. That coverage failure is standard ML, not a swarm insight. The decomposition adds one useful distinction (not transmitted vs transformed) and a calibration (the perfect-copy reference), but 16 messages and a coder at $\kappa\approx0.45$ measure little. The real surprise is the pipeline: the ``village distillation'' was the base model distilling itself.}

\hbottom{The fine-tuned leader carried a trace of its corpus's addressing style into the room but none of its plans, which were not transmitted rather than transformed, and its corpus was mostly its own base model talking to itself.}
