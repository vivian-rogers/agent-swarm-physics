\hwhat{HH258 claims that the population dilution exponent ($k^{-0.66}$, H18) averages two kinds of agents: thin attenders ($\beta\approx1$) and thread followers ($\beta\approx0$), with lab-set weights. H68 fits one exponent per agent on H18's ledger pending sets in 17 periods (\#10--\#51, 170 agent-periods). A synthetic validation on the real skeletons came first. \textbf{The claim is refuted.} Agents scatter around the period mean with a between-agent SD of 0.09--0.20. The two-strategy world needs about 0.45. No period is bimodal (0/9 powered). In 7/9 powered periods the SD's upper 95\% bound is below 0.35. No agent sits near $\beta=0$ (lowest 0.25). Lab does not predict the exponent ($p$ 0.37), and an agent's exponent barely persists across periods (ICC 0.23). Two natives: \#51 fails, and NE42 has too few agents. The holdout script is written, not run.}

\hmodel{Model 03 (contagion) with H18's uptake hazard. At talk turn $\tau$ of agent $i$, a pending sender $j$ with $n_j$ messages in a backlog of $k$ is addressed with a cloglog hazard. $\theta_{i,d}$ is an agent-day propensity, $\gamma$ a mention factor and $\beta_i$ the agent's dilution exponent. The population layer is unimodal (U), the H68 two-delta mixture, or a free two-component mixture. Each is fitted on $\hat\beta_i$ with its own sampling variance $s_i^2$.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
P(r_{\tau j}=1)=1-\exp\!\big[-\theta_{i,d}\,n_j\,k_\tau^{-\beta_i}\,e^{\gamma m_j}\big]\\
\text{U: }\hat\beta_i\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\,\tau^2+s_i^2)\\
\text{H68: }\hat\beta_i\sim\pi\,\mathcal N(1,\,v_i)+(1-\pi)\,\mathcal N(0,\,v_i),\quad v_i=\tau_w^2+s_i^2\\
\mathrm{LR}=2\,[\ell_{\rm M2}-\ell_{\rm U}],\qquad p\ \text{by parametric bootstrap under U}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{Dilution is an agent-level constant per period, not a property of the roster's family mix. A swarm designer can use one exponent per period ($\beta\approx0.6$--$0.9$) as the per-pair coupling normalization $J\propto N^{-\beta}$. A change of model families will not change it. There is no ``thread-follower'' species to protect from crowding: every agent ignores more of the backlog as the backlog grows. Concentrated agents dilute \emph{more} ($\rho$ +0.36). To keep a message from being lost in a large room, address it or shrink the room. Changing which models sit in the room does not help. In practice this result is a negative control, and it adds no new lever.}

\hbottom{Every agent dilutes its attention with one narrow, unimodal exponent per period (between-agent SD 0.09--0.20), so $k^{-0.66}$ is a constant, not a mixture of two strategies.}

\hcaveats{The response is H18's mention proxy. Reply labels keep one parent per message, so only their agent ranking is used. Eligibility drops 53 of 223 agent-periods with few talks, and a quiet thread follower could hide there. The per-agent timer-wake check (\#51, 24 agents) does not exclude reactive timing per agent.
