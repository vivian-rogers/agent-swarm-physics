\hwhat{After a goal change, agents move their work to new repositories. I measured this move on DQ4 work commits in four regime-III kickoffs (G39, G40, G41, G51) and tested it against the Wasserstein speed limit $T\ge W/\bar A$. The synthetic check came first. In three kickoffs that name a target, the allocation freezes within 15--30 active min. Each moved agent switches once ($S=\bar AT/W=1.00$--$1.40$), so the move sits on the bound. G41 (no shared target) takes 9.5\,h with $S=5.1$ [1.4, 11.2]. In G44 the room with a named target settles $\times5.7$ faster than the free-choice room at the same switch rate. The field-limited predictions (slack $\ge3$, settling 2.5--10\,h) failed in 3/4 periods. Eleven newcomers settle in a median 0.28\,h. The cadence elasticity is not identifiable at $N\le25$. Holdout (NE20): not run.}

\hmodel{Models 02 (kinetic jump process) and 05 (driven population). Each agent of a period is one copy of a Markov jump process on repos plus a null state; $p(t)$ is the share of agents on each repo. The kickoff is the field. $W$: total-variation distance between the allocations at $t=0^-$ and at the settling time $T$. $\bar A$: switches per agent per active hour. $S$: slack. $\Sigma_{\rm ex}$: excess entropy production (Kolchinsky et al.\ 2026).}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
\dot p=\nabla^{\!\top} j,\qquad A=\textstyle\sum_\rho j_\rho,\qquad W=\tfrac12\lVert p(T)-p(0)\rVert_1\\
\Sigma_{\rm ex}(T)\ \ge\ 2W\tanh^{-1}\!\big(W/\bar AT\big)\ \Rightarrow\ T\ \ge\ \frac{W}{\bar A}\coth\frac{\Sigma_{\rm ex}}{2W}\ \ge\ \frac{W}{\bar A}\\
S=\bar AT/W\ \ (\ge 1;\ S=1:\ \text{every switch directed})
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

(b) Slack $S=\bar AT/W$ against the settling time $T_e$, with agent-bootstrap CIs. $S=1$ is the speed limit. Grey: synthetic field-limited (F), activity-limited (A) and instant-freeze (Z) means. \htelos{Useful as a design rule for kickoffs, with a small evidence base. If the kickoff names the artifact, the swarm re-points in 15--30 active min with no wasted switches. If agents must choose, re-pointing takes 4--10\,h and 5--15 switches per net move. An operator who wants a fast, clean switch should name the repository or deliverable in the goal text. The speed-limit language adds little beyond this: the bound holds by counting, and the slack measures how specific the field is, not a thermodynamic cost. Whether faster call cadence speeds re-allocation is untested here; NE20 in the holdout can answer it.}

\hbottom{A kickoff that names its target re-allocates the swarm at the Wasserstein speed limit within half an active hour; a free-choice kickoff settles 5--40 times slower through churn, at the same switch rate.}

\hcaveats{The bound holds by construction on the empirical ensemble, so P0 is a check, not a test. Commits are the only observation of the state: the settling time includes the first-commit delay, and the 15-min grid floors $T_e$ at 0.25\,h. G51 has no pre-kickoff allocation (the previous week is held out), so its $W$ counts every first commit as a move. Each period gives one kickoff with 13--16 agents, and bootstrap CIs of $S$ are wide outside G39. G44's named-target room has 4 committing agents. The cadence elasticity is not identified.}
