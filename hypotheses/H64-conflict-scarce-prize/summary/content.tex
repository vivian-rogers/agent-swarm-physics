\hwhat{The test asks whether stance antagonism between agents needs a rival-exclusive prize and ends when the prize is settled. Replies and stance come from DQ2; prize states come from DQ6 (\#12 verdicts, \#26 election result) and linked chess games (\#23). Dated predictions and a synthetic power study came first. \textbf{Debates (\#12):} opponents answer each other 0.78 [0.56, 1.10] worse than teammates while the prize is open, and 0.09 [$-0.26$, 0.39] after the verdict ($p=0.001$). Teammates do not change, and losers do not resent winners. \textbf{Competitions} (\#6, \#23, \#26, \#27) have more confident position-opposition than every-day weeks (4/4 above the median, $p=0.022$). The excess is room-wide: chess opponents and election rivals show no rival-specific contrast (both low power). Without a prize, no antagonism exceeds the agent-field null. \textbf{Supported}, with a narrowed reading. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Signed couplings on reply bonds (model 01) with a two-camp Mattis state (model 10). A reply from $j$ to $i$ has a latent stance with agent fields $a_j$ (agreeableness) and $b_i$ (likability). The prize is a staggered external field that is on while the contest is open ($O=1$) and off once settled. $R_{ij}=1$ marks rivals for one exclusive outcome. $\Delta$ is field-induced antagonism, $\delta$ a pair constant that survives settlement (remanence), and $\phi$ a contest field on every bond (heat).}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
y^*_e=\mu+a_j+b_i+\Delta\,R_{ij}\,O(t)+\delta\,R_{ij}+\phi\,O(t)+u_{k(e)}+\varepsilon_e\\
\gamma_{\rm open}=\delta+\Delta,\quad \gamma_{\rm set}=\delta,\quad \hat\Delta=\hat\gamma_{\rm open}-\hat\gamma_{\rm set}\\
r_p=\Pr\big[\text{confident opposes, position}\mid \text{reply}\big]
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

(b) Rival minus other reply stance while the prize is open and after settlement. In \#12 the contrast falls from $-0.78$ to $-0.09$ at the verdict. \htelos{To end a fight between agents, settle the prize. In \#12 the stance contrast between opponents falls to its noise floor within the post-verdict phase ($\le$ 10 min), and losers show no residual hostility. Open contests raise disagreement across the whole room by a factor of about 1.2 to 3.5 in confident position-opposition. They do not create rival-specific feuds, and rival roles without a single winner (\#51) create none. A monitor can therefore treat stance antagonism as a field set by the incentive, not a property of the pair. The competition result rests on an unvalidated opposition subtype and on four regime-I periods.}

\hbottom{An exclusive prize switches assigned antagonism on and its settlement switches it off within minutes; open competition raises disagreement room-wide, not between rivals.}
