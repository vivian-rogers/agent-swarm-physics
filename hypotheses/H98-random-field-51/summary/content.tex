\hwhat{H22 refuted a spin glass in \#51 and described a random-field system instead. H98 fits that picture as a mean-field random-field O($n$) model on 9 non-holdout regime-III units: 5 private-role \#51 units and 4 shared-goal weeks (\#38--\#41). After style removal, 67--73\% of the static content configuration in \#51 is agent-specific (disorder ratio $R$), against 24--53\% in shared-goal weeks. A weak positive pull acts on top ($b_{ex}=0.23$--$0.31$). Same-role rivals co-move because their role texts overlap: the niche slope fitted on non-rivals predicts the rival excess (bge). But reads and replies absorb 70\% of that slope, and day-to-day overlaps are synchronized in 2 of 5 units. \textbf{The claim fails by its pre-registered rule}; the static random-field part stands. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Mean-field random-field O($n$) model (models 01, 11). The spin $s_{id}$ is agent $i$'s whitened, style-residualized content on day $d$ ($n=32$). The quenched field $h_i$ holds role, own projects and priors; $\hat r_i$ is the whitened role text. $J$ pulls toward the room mean; $g_d$ is the day field. Within a day, window fluctuations follow a shared drive $\eta_w$ filtered by each agent's niche projector $P_i$, so co-movement grows with the squared niche overlap $n_{ij}^2$.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
s_{id}\propto h_i+J\,m_{-i,d}+g_d+z_{id}+\epsilon_{id},\qquad R=\frac{\Delta^2}{\Delta^2+M^2}\\
x_{iw}=(\alpha P_i+\beta)\,\eta_w+\xi_{iw}\;\Rightarrow\;\mathbb E\,J^c_{ij}\propto\alpha^2 n_{ij}^2+2\alpha\beta+n\beta^2\\
\hat T_{SR}=\beta_n\,(\overline{n^2}_{SR}-\overline{n^2}_{U}),\qquad G=T_{SR}-\hat T_{SR}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{For an operator who assigns private roles, the role text sets only 5--14\% of where an agent's content sits. Most of the field is the agent's own projects, and a newcomer takes days to settle into its role (day-1 own-role percentile 0.57 vs 0.83 for incumbents). Agents with overlapping roles read and answer each other, and that conversation, not a shared field, carries most of their co-movement. To keep two roles from converging, give them non-overlapping role texts and separate rooms. In the room-split native, one moved agent lost all pull toward \#general (0.42 $\to$ 0.01). As a monitor, $R$ measured after style removal tells a private-goal swarm ($R\approx0.7$) from a shared-goal one ($R\le0.53$).}

\hbottom{\#51's static structure is a random field with a weak pull, but its dynamics are not independent agents in a field: rivals co-move mostly through conversation chosen by niche, and some days move together.}

\hcaveats{The niche result holds in bge only; \#51 is the least model-robust period (DQ5). $\hat T_{SR}$ extrapolates from non-rival overlaps $n^2\le0.56$ to rivals' $n^2=1$.
