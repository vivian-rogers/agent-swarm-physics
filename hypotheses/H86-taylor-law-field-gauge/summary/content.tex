\hwhat{The test asks whether Taylor's law gives a one-number gauge of the shared field. Per period unit, each agent's activity is counted in 15-minute bins, on the raw day window and on DQ8's all-present (trimmed) window. The HH's coefficient $c_T$ (variance $=a\mu+c_T\mu^2$ across agents) fails: Taylor's exponent is $b\approx0.85$ and $c_T\le0$ in most of 70 units. The pair-covariance coefficient $c_\times$ works as a gauge. It is small (0.016 raw, 0.008 trimmed), and the shared share of excess variance is 0.08. Trimming removes 77\% of it in regime III, matching H38's 70--80\%. The gauge is now a per-unit column. The holdout script exists and is not run.}

\hmodel{Fluctuation scaling (model 14) with a multiplicative field $h(t)$ (model 02). Agent $i$'s count in bin $t$ is Poisson with rate $\mu_i\xi_t\zeta_{it}$. $\xi_t$ is a field shared by all present agents (scheduler, kickoff, operator) with variance $c_\times$. $\zeta_{it}$ is the agent's private burstiness with variance $s_i$. $c_\times$ is read from pair covariances and needs no spread in $\mu$. $\varphi$ is the shared share of super-Poisson variance.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
V_i=\mu_i+\bigl[c_\times+s_i(1+c_\times)\bigr]\mu_i^2,\qquad \mathrm{Cov}(y_i,y_j)=c_\times\,\mu_i\mu_j\\
c_\times=\frac{\sum_{i<j}\mathrm{Cov}_{ij}}{\sum_{i<j}\mu_i\mu_j},\qquad
\varphi=\frac{c_\times\sum_i\mu_i^2}{\sum_i(V_i-\mu_i)},\qquad r_\times=1-\frac{c_\times^{\rm trim}}{c_\times^{\rm raw}}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

(b) Shared-field coefficient per unit, raw against trimmed grid. Regime-III units fall toward the 75\%-removed line; regime-I units sit on the diagonal.}

\htelos{Report \texttt{taylor\_c\_shared} on \texttt{activity\_trim} (and \texttt{activity\_raw}) from \texttt{per\_period\_estimates} next to any synchrony or influence claim. A trimmed $c_\times$ near 0.008 with $\varphi<0.1$ means the swarm's activity fluctuations are about 90\% private. Do not use Taylor's $b$ or $c_T$ as field measures in a clocked swarm: they track clock regularity. A raw $c_\times$ far above the trimmed one (e.g.\ 2.2 on 2026-03-31) flags a village-off gap inside the day window. Talk shares about 2.6 times more of its variance than activity ($\varphi$ 0.21 vs 0.08).}

\hbottom{Taylor's $c$ measures clock regularity, not the field; the pair-covariance $c_\times$ measures the field, which is small and mostly the scheduler.}
