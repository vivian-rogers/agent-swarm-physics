\hwhat{A kinetic-Ising fit asks whether rooms make agents coupled blocks, on 99 non-holdout days (\#35--\#44, \#51 to 09-04). Within-room talk coupling beats cross-room coupling in 6/6 regime-III two-room windows (Fisher $p=6\times10^{-7}$). The pooled effect of co-location on talk ($z=5.1$) falls to $z=0.2$ once ledger read counts enter. \textbf{The holdout NE15} split passes the pair DiD ($\kappa_x$ $-0.022$, $p_{\rm perm}=0.02$) but misses the pooled test, so it is \textbf{inconclusive}. \textbf{Round 2} asks which channels cross rooms. Rooms split artifacts too: cross-room same-day co-editing is $\le0.04$ of the within-room rate in 5/8 windows, and NE42 cut pairs go $0.56\to0$ at the split. Cross-room commit coupling through a shared repo is not detectable (power 0.33). Room-size dilution exists ($k^{-0.65}$, post hoc) but predicts only $\times1.2$ per re-cut, not the $\times6$. Novel repos leak across rooms through command output at $+0.26$ adoptions per exposure (lift 21); history search shows none.}

\hmodel{Model 02 (kinetic Ising). The spin $s_i(t)=\pm1$ says whether agent $i$ talks (talk spin) or acts (activity spin) in 1-min bin $t$; $s_i'=s_i(t{+}1)$. The field $h_i$ is the daily schedule; a cross-day surrogate removes it. $r_i$ is the room. $J_{\rm in}$ and $J_{\rm out}$ are the same- and cross-room couplings; a cut predicts $J_{\rm out}\to0$. $\Sigma_g$ is the Aguilera--Ito--Kolchinsky lower bound on the entropy production $\sigma$, with dual weights $\theta$. $\hat R$ and $D$ are block correlations and variances. $\alpha_{ij}$ and $\gamma_d$ are pair and day effects.}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\footnotesize\setlength{\jot}{1pt}
\begin{gather*}
P(s_i'\,|\,\mathbf s)=\frac{e^{s_i'H_i}}{2\cosh H_i},\quad H_i=h_i+\textstyle\sum_j J_{ij}s_j\\
J_{ij}=J_{\rm in}\,\delta_{r_ir_j}+J_{\rm out}\,(1-\delta_{r_ir_j})\\
\sigma=\textstyle\sum_{i<j}(J_{ij}-J_{ji})\langle g_{ij}\rangle,\quad g_{ij}=s_i's_j-s_is_j'\\
\Sigma_g=\max_\theta\big[\theta\cdot\langle g\rangle-\ln\langle e^{-\theta\cdot g}\rangle\big]\le\sigma\\
J^{\rm MF}=D^{-1/2}\big(I-\hat R^{-1}\big)D^{-1/2}\\
\kappa_{ij,d}=\alpha_{ij}+\gamma_d+\beta\,\mathrm{coloc}_{ij,d}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{A room is a read filter, and it is also a work filter: the operator's split separates the repositories as well as the chat. To decouple two agents, stop them reading each other. With read counts known, co-location adds nothing ($z=0.2$). To move one item across rooms, put it in a command's output (a shared listing or README). One such exposure gives about a 1-in-4 adoption within 2 h, $3\times$ a same-room chat read. History search does not do it. Do not expect a room cut to change pair coupling by attention alone. Dilution gives $\times1.2$ for a $4\times$ smaller room; the observed jumps ($\approx0$ when merged, $\times2$ after the split) carry a goal field too.}

\hbottom{Rooms partition both chat and artifacts; items leak across mostly through command output and chat relays, and dilution is too small to explain the re-cut jumps.}
