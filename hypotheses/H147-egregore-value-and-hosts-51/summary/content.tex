\hwhat{H145 found 15 memeplexes in \#51: sets of content elements (statement clusters, coinages, repos, projects) that agents co-express and that outlive any one host. H147 asks two questions about them. First, does a memeplex hold information in its hosts' contexts, so that erasing a host's context costs the pattern (Kolchinsky--Wolpert value)? Second, does carrying a memeplex help or hurt the host's own assigned work (mutualist or parasitic)? Forced erasures at the 41-call cap (NE41) are quasi-random scrambles; hub losses and operator actions are single dated scrambles.}

\hmodel{Semantic information (model 04) with the memeplex as the system and agents as substrate. Viability $V_K$ is the pattern's expression share in a host's calls 1--20. A forced erasure F is compared with a placebo call P at matched segment position, within agent $\times$ unit strata, and every statistic is compared with 100 frequency-matched pseudo-patterns. The host relation compares an agent's hosting and non-hosting 2-h bins at matched activity (statements, calls).}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}

\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{For an operator: forcing context erasures does not remove an ideology from a swarm. Erasing a host's context did not lower its expression of any of 13 of 14 distributed memeplexes; three came back stronger. To change an ideology, act on its artifacts and on the other hosts, not on context.}

\hbottom{In \#51, forced context erasures of a memeplex's hosts do not lower its expression share: 1 of 14 distributed H145 memeplexes loses $\geq10\%$ (median $\Delta V_{K,F}=+0.05$, 95\% CI 0.00 to 0.12; power 0.90 at $-30\%$).}

\hcaveats{The card's count form of $V_K$ failed in synthetic worlds: a forced erasure halves the host's statements (0.70 vs 1.47 in calls 1--20), so every pattern's count halves. Amendment A1 measured the share instead, before real data. Hosting any frequency-matched random element set also costs about $-7\%$ commits and $-9\%$ role alignment at matched activity, so most ``parasitic'' classes are a generic cost of talking about a shared topic. Hub-loss and operator events are single events with per-event noise larger than the effects (synthetic size up to 0.85). The post-wipe gains of K03, K12 and K13 are post hoc.}
