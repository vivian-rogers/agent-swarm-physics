\hwhat{The test splits an agent's chat style into an agent constant, a context-fill drift and an assigned-register shift. Style is H13's 17 type-controlled features. The fit runs per goal period on 34 non-holdout periods (126k messages). The three components explain most of the systematic variance that the day field leaves ($F_3=0.72$, IQR 0.65--0.80; 34/34 periods above 0.57). The agent constant carries almost all of it. Context fill adds a median share of 0.08, and 0.011 in regime III. Removing the context term does not raise attribution ($\Delta_c=-0.007$ [$-0.017$, $+0.003$]). At forced erasures the fitted drift predicts 1.7\% of the style jump. Judging in \#12 is a shared register. The \#51 roles share none. The holdout script exists and is not run.}

\hmodel{Vector spins (model 11). The spin is a message's style vector $x_m$. The day field $g_d$ and a time-of-day term $\tau$ are external fields. The agent constant $q_i$ is the weights. The context term is a profile $c(s)$ over fill bins plus an agent drift $b_i$ along $z=\log_2(1+p)$, with $p$ the calls since the last reset. $\rho_r$ is an assigned register (judge, role). $\eta$ is agent-day jitter. $\kappa$ is the cell-means ceiling.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
x_m=g_d+\tau(h_m)+q_i+c(s_m)+b_i\,z_m+\rho_{r_m}+\eta_{i,d}+\varepsilon_m\\
F_3=\frac{\bar R^2(G{+}A{+}C{+}R)-\bar R^2(G)}{\kappa-\bar R^2(G)},\qquad
u_X=\frac{\bar R^2(\text{all})-\bar R^2(\text{all}\setminus X)}{\kappa-\bar R^2(G)}\\
\Delta_c=\mathrm{acc}\bigl[q_j+c(s)+b_j z\bigr]-\mathrm{acc}\bigl[q_j\bigr],\qquad
\beta=\frac{\sum\langle\Delta x,\Delta\hat x_{\rm ctx}\rangle}{\sum\lVert\Delta\hat x_{\rm ctx}\rVert^2}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{For attribution, use the agent's mean style over at least five messages. Balanced accuracy is 0.63 against chance 0.10. Do not correct for context position: the correction gains $-0.007$. A style monitor can treat the agent constant as fixed within a period. It carries 0.55 of the non-day systematic variance, and agent-day jitter carries 0.18. Expect one message to be mostly noise: only 27\% of its style variance is systematic. Known speech roles such as judging shift style in one shared direction (cos 0.78). That shift does not confuse attribution. Context erasure moves style by a random amount with no fixed direction, so a monitor should not read a post-reset style change as a model change.}

\hbottom{Style is an agent constant plus noise: context fill and assigned registers add small shares, and removing them does not improve attribution.}

\hcaveats{The 20 features mix formatting with topic-adjacent rates (digits, uppercase). $F_3$ rests on a cell ceiling that is noisy where cells are small. The synthetic bias reaches $+0.07$ near 0.55, and $F_3$ exceeds 1 in G03. In regimes I/II half of the context share is the chat versus computer-use level. Fill counts calls, not messages or tokens, and it may miss what drives the erasure excursion. The \#51 role test is pre-registered on three-week eras. The onset-block result (Prankster 1.00) is post hoc. The natives are small: five judges, four media agents, 20 leader messages.}
