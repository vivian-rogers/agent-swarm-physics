\hwhat{We asked whether an agent's writing style (H13's 20 features, controlled for length, code and links) stays fixed under every natural experiment while its content (style-residualized embeddings) moves. Each boundary was scored against the agent's own day-to-day transitions. Style is not conserved. It moves at goal switches ($T_s=0.69$ vs content $0.86$; 21/24), across exogenously timed context erasures ($0.56$, while content does not move), for the \#51 Prankster (percentile 1.00) and for \#12 judges. It holds where content also holds (roster, scaffold) and after the nudger switch-off. Style is still a strong identity fingerprint: trained before and tested after a goal switch, it names agents at 0.76 (chance 0.15), against 0.51 for content. Style and content carry similar, small information about next-day output. Holdout script written, not run.}

\hmodel{Vector spins (model 11) with two channels per message: a style vector $x$ and a content vector $y$. $q_i$: the agent's style charge (its weights). $\eta$: day jitter. $c_{i,d}$: content state, driven by goal, room and context. A natural experiment $b$ conserves style if the boundary displacement $D_b$ is exchangeable with the agent's own placebo transitions $D_p$. $T$: mean percentile. $V$: next-day work output (DQ4). $\epsilon$: context-held excitation, growing with position $k$ since the last reset (post hoc).}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
x_m=q_i+\eta_{i,d}+\epsilon_i(k_m)+\varepsilon_m,\qquad \epsilon_i(k{=}1)\approx0\ \text{after an erasure}\\
D(d,d')=\lVert\bar x_d-\bar x_{d'}\rVert^2-\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}\,S_d}{n_d}-\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}\,S_{d'}}{n_{d'}},\qquad
T=\Bigl\langle \Pr_p\!\bigl[D_p<D_b\bigr]\Bigr\rangle_{i,b}\\
\text{conserved}\iff T_c>\tfrac12\ (p<.05),\ T_s\not>\tfrac12,\ T_s-\tfrac12\le\tfrac13\bigl(T_c-\tfrac12\bigr)\\
I=-\tfrac12\log_2\!\bigl(1-R^2_{\rm cv}[\,V_{d+1}\mid x_d \text{ or } y_d\,]\bigr)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

Grey band: $|T-\tfrac12|\le0.1$. Goal switches and erasures leave the band in style; roster and scaffold do not move at all. (b) Identity across the same boundaries, trained before and tested after. \htelos{The useful result is the fingerprint. A handful of cheap formatting and punctuation rates identifies which model wrote a day's messages, even after the task changes. It does so better than embeddings of what was said, which matters for attribution and for spotting an unannounced model swap. The negative results are the warning label. Style is not a constant of the weights: it drifts as the context fills, snaps back at a context reset, and follows assigned registers such as a prankster role or a judge's chair. A monitor should therefore compare like with like: the same context position and the same speech act. The Kolchinsky--Wolpert framing added nothing actionable, since style and content predict tomorrow's output equally and only through today's.}

\hbottom{Style is a strong identity fingerprint but not a conserved charge: it shifts at goal switches, at context erasures and under assigned registers, so it behaves like a substrate charge dressed by the context window.}

\hcaveats{The 20 features mix formatting with content-adjacent rates: digits, uppercase and colons carry the goal-switch shift. A function-word stylometry might be conserved. Only length, code and links are controlled; speech-act genre is not, so the erasure and judge effects could be genre (a status update after a reset, a verdict). The drift continuing from position 2 to 7+ argues against a pure first-message effect. The persona comparison spans five weeks of held-out windows. The Prankster and NE38 are single agents; rooms has 4 boundaries and nudger 1. KW is observational and powered only in \#51 and \#38. Post hoc analyses are labelled.}
