\hwhat{The test asks whether an agent's style stays fixed under every natural experiment while its content moves. Style is H13's 20 features with length, code and link shares controlled. Content is the style-residualized embedding. Each boundary is scored against the agent's own day-to-day transitions. Style is not conserved. At goal switches style moves and content moves more ($T_s=0.69$, $T_c=0.86$; 21/24 switches). Across exogenously timed context erasures style moves ($0.56$) and content does not. Style also moves for the \#51 Prankster (percentile 1.00) and for the \#12 judges. Style holds where content also holds (roster, scaffold) and after the nudger switch-off. Style still identifies agents across a goal switch: 0.76 for style, 0.51 for content, 0.15 for chance. Style and content carry similar, small information about next-day output. The holdout script exists and is not run.}

\hmodel{Vector spins (model 11), two channels per message. The style vector $x$ carries a style charge $q_i$ (the weights) and a day jitter $\eta$. The content state $c_{i,d}$ follows the goal, the room and the context. A boundary $b$ conserves style if its displacement $D_b$ is exchangeable with the agent's placebo transitions $D_p$. $T$ is the mean percentile. $V$ is next-day work output (DQ4). $\epsilon$ is a context-held excitation that grows with position $k$ since the last reset (post hoc).}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
x_m=q_i+\eta_{i,d}+\epsilon_i(k_m)+\varepsilon_m,\qquad \epsilon_i(k{=}1)\approx0\ \text{after an erasure}\\
D(d,d')=\lVert\bar x_d-\bar x_{d'}\rVert^2-\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}\,S_d}{n_d}-\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}\,S_{d'}}{n_{d'}},\qquad
T=\Bigl\langle \Pr_p\!\bigl[D_p<D_b\bigr]\Bigr\rangle_{i,b}\\
\text{conserved}\iff T_c>\tfrac12\ (p<.05),\ T_s\not>\tfrac12,\ T_s-\tfrac12\le\tfrac13\bigl(T_c-\tfrac12\bigr)\\
I=-\tfrac12\log_2\!\bigl(1-R^2_{\rm cv}[\,V_{d+1}\mid x_d \text{ or } y_d\,]\bigr)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

Big symbols are class means with 95\% CIs. The grey band is $|T-\tfrac12|\le0.1$. Goal switches and erasures leave the band in style. Roster and scaffold steps move neither channel. \htelos{To attribute a day's messages to a model, use the 20 formatting and punctuation rates, not content embeddings. Across a goal switch they identify the agent at 0.76, against 0.51 for embeddings (chance 0.15). The same fingerprint can flag an unannounced model swap. Style is not a constant of the weights. It drifts as the context fills, returns at a context reset and follows assigned registers such as a prankster role or a judge's chair. A style monitor must therefore compare messages at the same context position and in the same speech act. The Kolchinsky--Wolpert framing gives no instruction: style and content predict next-day output equally, and only through today's output.}

\hbottom{Style is a substrate charge dressed by context: goal switches, erasures and assigned registers move it, yet it identifies agents at 0.76 (content 0.51).}

\hcaveats{The 20 features mix formatting with content-adjacent rates. Digits, uppercase and colons carry the goal-switch shift, so a function-word stylometry may be conserved. The type control removes length, code and links only. Speech-act genre is not controlled, so the erasure and judge effects can be genre (a status update after a reset, a verdict). The drift from position 2 to 7+ argues against a pure first-message effect. The persona comparison spans five weeks of held-out windows. The Prankster and NE38 are single agents. The rooms class has 4 boundaries and the nudger class 1. The KW test is observational and has power only in \#51 and \#38. Post hoc analyses carry labels.}
