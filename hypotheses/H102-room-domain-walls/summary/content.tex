\hwhat{HH332 says that rooms doing different things form two content domains. Agents who hop between rooms would sit inside the wall, and the wall would widen with their cross-room read-outs. H102 projects style-residualized content on the inter-room axis in 9 non-holdout units (\#35--\#44 and the \#focus weeks of \#51). It follows the five \#focus hoppers and one \#36 hopper. \textbf{The domains are real; the wall is not.} Where rooms do different work, stayers split into two groups ($D$ 5.3--6.1; relabel 95th percentile $\approx1$). Hoppers stay inside their home domain. The one frequent hopper sits at $s=0.06$ against a stayers' 95th percentile of 0.21. Hopping read-outs have no effect ($\kappa_R$ $-0.014$ [$-0.042$, 0.024]). A hopper's talk follows the room it is speaking in. HH332's kill rule fires. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Model 11 (kinetic soft-spin, $n=32$) read along one axis like an Ising order parameter (model 01). Agent content $\mathbf{x}_i$ relaxes toward the content it reads, with weights $\pi_{ij}$ from the context ledger, and toward its domain field $\mathbf{h}_D$ (room work or instructions). The wall coordinate $s$ is 0 at the home-domain centroid and 1 at the other domain. $p_i$ is the share of read items from the other domain. Coupling predicts $s\approx\kappa p$; a room-local field predicts that $s$ jumps with the room of the statement.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
\mathbf{x}_i\leftarrow(1-\lambda)\mathbf{x}_i+\lambda\big[w\textstyle\sum_j\pi_{ij}\mathbf{x}_j+(1-w)(\mathbf{h}_{D(i)}+\mathbf{a}_i)\big]+\boldsymbol{\eta}\\
s_i=\frac{(\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{c}_\mathrm{home})\cdot\mathbf{u}}{(\mathbf{c}_\mathrm{other}-\mathbf{c}_\mathrm{home})\cdot\mathbf{u}},\qquad s_i\approx\kappa\,p_i\\
D=\frac{\bar z_B-\bar z_A}{\sqrt{(\sigma_A^2+\sigma_B^2)/2}}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{For an operator: to keep two groups' content apart, give them different work ($D\approx5$--6). Identical instructions in separate rooms split less ($D\approx1.5$--2.4). Letting a few agents visit the other room does not blend the domains. At the observed visit rate (about 0.3\% of read items), a visitor's home-room content does not move ($|\kappa|<0.04$ per e-fold of reads). Visitors adapt while present and bring nothing back. To move content across rooms, post into the other room or move an agent there. This is a usable design rule with modest evidence: one frequent hopper and short visits.}

\hbottom{Rooms that do different work form two content domains separated by a sharp, empty wall; hoppers switch domains with the room they speak in and carry nothing home, so bridging agents do not widen the wall.}
