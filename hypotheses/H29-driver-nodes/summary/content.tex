\hwhat{We built a content-influence network per goal period (how far agent $j$'s next statement moves toward a message from $i$ it has just seen, net of placebos) and ranked agents by linear controllability in 9 counted and 3 short non-holdout units (regime III, \#51). \textbf{Round 1b} re-ran everything on the context ledger's visibility, with a second embedding model and DQ2's reply graph. The pre-registered test still fails in the opposite direction ($\kappa<0$ in 7/9): messages posted during the recipient's own call are co-responses, not leaked influence. Comparing seen and unseen messages at matched age, influence is real and goes through names: named recipients move 0.04--0.08 of the gap per message in every \#51 segment, unnamed ones 0.00--0.01. The content-pull driver ranking is still noise, but a reply-graph ranking (net reply current) is reproducible (split-half 0.65) and weakly predicts held-out spread (pooled $\rho=0.23$ bge, 0.12 gte). Natives: an operator's reassignment of one agent moved it and did not relay. Holdout not run.}

\hmodel{Linear response (model 02) with vector content states (11): DeGroot/Friedkin--Johnsen dynamics. $x_j$: agent $j$'s whitened statement embedding. $V(\tau)$: messages that reached $j$ between its previous and current model calls. $a_{ij}$: pull per message. $A_{ij}=a_{ij}r_{ij}$, with $r_{ij}$ the messages from $i$ absorbed by $j$ per hour. $\Gamma$: relaxation toward the agent's own field. Driver score $D_k$: reachability of the swarm mean from agent $k$ over $T=4$\,h. $E_k=N^2/D_k$: energy to shift the mean through $k$.}

x_j(\tau_n)=x_j(\tau_{n-1})+\textstyle\sum_{m\in V(\tau_n)}a_{s(m)j}\,[x_m-x_j(\tau_{n-1})]+\gamma_j[h_j-x_j]+\xi\\

D_k=\int_0^T\!\big(\mathbf 1^{\!\top}e^{Qt}\mathbf e_k\big)^2dt,\quad \mathbf 1^{\!\top}Q\,\mathbf e_k=\mathrm{out}_k-\mathrm{in}_k-\Gamma
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{The useful part is negative and simple. To move an agent, name it: that is a 3--6$\times$ stronger lever than broadcast, and broadcast content barely propagates. To move the whole swarm, address every agent rather than hunting for a relay. Network controllability, the hypothesis' selling point, adds nothing measurable here. In one big room the ``driver'' is just the chattiest agent. In the small two-room weeks the ranking is noise. And no ranking, controllability or volume, predicts observed spread. The steering-cost result (a typical agent costs $\propto N^{2.7}$, a one-room hub stays flat) is model-only. A methodological warning travels too: ``seen vs unseen'' placebos built from turn timing are contaminated by pauses, and content similarity is dominated by recency.}

\hbottom{Content influence between agents is real and address-gated (named $\gg$ unnamed, sharper on corrected visibility); the content-pull controllability ranking is unusable, but the reply graph gives a reproducible, weakly predictive relay ranking, and operator messages stay with the agent they name.}

\hcaveats{The positive findings are post hoc (boundary test, Amendment 2) or new in round 1b (the reply network, chosen by the re-evaluation brief). The reply-parent premium (0.11--0.19) is partly built in: DQ2 ranks parent candidates partly by content similarity, and a reply is about its parent. The held-out spread statistic replicates poorly (median 0.10 bge, 0.01 gte), so the reply ranking's validation is weak. Named effects may partly be conversational mode. The two-room weeks have too few near-boundary rows. Natives are small: one event (NE38), six leader agent-days (\#35), one short pre-period (\#26).}
