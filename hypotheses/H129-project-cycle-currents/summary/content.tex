\hwhat{HH370 says that agents hopping between projects behave like a sticky Potts walker whose flows are driven around cycles ($A\to B\to C\to A$) by projects being born and dying, so detailed balance breaks with a circulation from older to newer projects, and dwell times are geometric. H129 extracts every agent's sequence of distinct projects (work host labels and attention labels) in 13 non-holdout periods and 12 \#51 units and measures triple affinities against a per-agent time-reversal null and against an age walker calibrated to each unit. \textbf{There is no circulation beyond age drift}: the rotation-summed cycle affinity beats the age walker in 0/9 shared-goal unit-channels (power 0.55--0.97 for a 2-nat cycle). HH370's literal test passes in only 3/9. Dwell hazards age (32/39). The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Models 10, 02 and 15. Sticky walker: per own call an agent leaves with probability $r$; at a hop it picks $j$ with $P(j)\propto e^{\alpha_j+b\,[\text{held}]+\lambda z_j(t)}$, $z_j$ the standardized age rank among available projects. Any potential (fixed attractiveness, age bias $\lambda$) makes the three rotations of a triangle cancel; a cyclic force $\kappa$ does not. Age drift is excess entropy production; a cycle is housekeeping.}

\htelos{For an operator: agents do not rotate through projects. They drift slightly toward the newest ones (net flow 0.03--0.21 of hops) and become stickier the longer they stay (hazard falls by about 20--60\% per e-fold of dwell). To move an agent off a project, act early in its stay; an agent several hours into a project rarely leaves on its own. Expect few returns-in-a-ring; plan handoffs as one-way moves to new work.}

\hbottom{Agents' project hops carry no circulation beyond age drift (cycle affinity above a calibrated age walker in 0/9 shared-goal unit-channels, power 0.55--0.97); net flow to newer projects is small but consistent (30/37 positive) and dwell hazards age rather than stay geometric (32/39).}
