\hwhat{HH346 proposed a Griffiths phase: a swarm subcritical on average whose rare strongly coupled pairs make reply chains heavy-tailed. H114 measures reply depth on DQ2 parent links in 66 non-holdout units (54 usable). Chains are heavier than a uniform subcritical process: the tail continuation hazard exceeds the reply branching ratio in 52/54 units (CI $>0$ in 33/54). The excess sits at depth 1: a reply's parent is itself a reply more often than the mean allows (+0.16, 54/54 units). The hazard then rises little (significant in 10/54). Strong pairs (pair gain $>0.5$) exist in 18/66 units, are half of them readers answering three hub authors, and carry the tail in 1/16. \textbf{The Griffiths mechanism fails}; thread momentum explains the tail. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{A reply forest (models 09, 03, 01). Each agent message has at most one agent parent. Its depth $D$ counts links to the root. A uniform subcritical process gives a geometric depth law at the reply branching ratio $g_{\rm rep}$. A Griffiths phase mixes continuation rates: strong pairs $(i,j)$ with pair gain $g_{ij}$ near 1 make the hazard $h(d)$ rise with depth. Thread momentum makes $h$ jump at $d=1$ and stay flat. M1 and M2 are first-order author chains without and with pair-specific transitions.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
S(d)=P(D\ge d),\quad h(d)=\frac{S(d+1)}{S(d)},\quad g_{\rm rep}=S(1)\\
h_{\rm tail}=\frac{\sum_{d\ge4}S(d+1)}{\sum_{d\ge4}S(d)},\quad \Delta h=h_{\rm tail}-g_{\rm rep},\quad \delta h=h_{\rm tail}-h(1)\\
g_{ij}=L_{ij}/R_{ij},\qquad \text{M}k:\ s_d=p\odot(P_k\,s_{d-1})
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{For an operator, the long reply chains in a swarm come from conversation momentum: once two messages form a reply, the next message continues the thread 28--55\% more often than the base reply rate (regime III and I). They do not come from a few agent pairs locked in ping-pong. Removing or separating ``chatty pairs'' would not shorten threads (cutting strong pairs removed the excess in 1/16 units). To bound long threads, act on thread state, for example a cap on consecutive replies per thread. Tail exponents of reply chains are not identifiable at village counts.}

\hbottom{Reply chains are heavier than uniform subcritical branching because of first-order thread momentum, not because rare strong agent pairs form locally supercritical regions.}
