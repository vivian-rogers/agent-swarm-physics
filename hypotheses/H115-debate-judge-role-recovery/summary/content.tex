\hwhat{HH353 asked whether the per-call talk kinetic Ising, fitted blind to timing, ranks each debate's judge as the top sink of antisymmetric coupling. In the ten \#12 debates (7 agents, 3,644 calls) the ranking was frozen and hashed before any role label was read. The judge ranks first in 3 of 10 debates (median rank 2.5, mean normalized rank 0.38; uniform $p=0.15$, field-only skeleton $p=0.24$). The predicted team blocks come out reversed: debaters talk after reading an opponent, not a team-mate. As replication, the \#26 elected leader is the top talk source in 4 of 6 windows; \#35 lead designers and the \#44 leader show nothing. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Model 02. Spin $s_i(c)=\pm1$: call $c$ of agent $i$ talks or not, on the ledger call clock. Input $x_{ij}(c)=1$ if $j$ posted in $i$'s room since $i$'s previous call (read-gated); $x^P_{ij}$: posted during the call (in-flight placebo). Fields $h$: agent $\times$ call mode and agent $\times$ debate phase. Couplings $J_{ij}=\alpha_i+\beta_j$ (in, out); sink score $S_i$ = in minus out. Pair-type model: $J$ by same team, opposite team, judge and bench.}

\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{For a monitor: one-way roles do not show up as one-way talk timing at debate scale. A judge whose talk follows debaters' talk by $\geq0.6$ Ising units per read debater is excluded, so blind role detection from who-talks-after-whom fails for 7 agents and 300 calls per episode. Opposition does show up: opponents answer each other (cross-team $J$ 0.20 vs within-team 0.04). A cheap opposing-camp detector is therefore cross-block talk coupling, not within-block coupling. An elected leader can be the top talk source, but a single-agent rank needs a skeleton null (its null mean is 0.38 to 0.62, not 0.5).}
