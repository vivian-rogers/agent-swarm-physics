\hwhat{HH166 claims that each agent holds the room-message share of its context near a set point. The agent reads less per message when the room floods ($k^{-0.6}$ dilution as the homeostat), and overshoots, then relaxes, after an erasure. H45 computes the share per model call from DQ1's context ledger and measured prompt tokens, on 32 non-holdout periods (\#4--\#51). Four native tests add NE41's 21k forced erasures, the NE42 merge, \#51's roster growth and NE03's chat fetch limit. Synthetic controller, passive and scaffold-capped agents on real call schedules come first. \textbf{The claim is refuted.} The share follows inflow as passive accumulation predicts: the regulation index is $\le0.22$ in 32/32 periods, pooled 0.02. Floods move set points at $1.06$ (NE42) and $0.90$ (\#51) times the passive slope. After an erasure, agents talk \emph{less} (0.74) and reply more per talk; replies per call do not change. The holdout script is written, not run.}

\hmodel{Models 04 and 05 frame a set-point controller against passive accumulation. In one context segment between resets, room content $R_j$ (calibrated tokens of the new room items) and own content $W_j$ (the rest of the prompt $P_j$) accumulate. The order parameter is the share $s_j=R_j/P_j$, and the control parameter is $\lambda$, the room tokens per call. A controller would raise $W$ or reset sooner when the share is high, and engage more when it is low. $\varepsilon$ is the elasticity of the segment share to $\lambda$, and $\eta_W$ that of own content.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
s_j=\frac{R_j}{R_j+W_j},\qquad R_j=\sum_{i\le j}\big(a\,k_i+b\,c_i+e\,o_i\big)\\
\text{passive: } s_j=\frac{\lambda j}{W_j+\lambda j},\quad W_j\ \text{independent of}\ \lambda\\
\varepsilon=\frac{\partial\ln \bar s}{\partial\ln\lambda}\Big|_j=(1-\bar s)(1-\eta_W),\qquad \mathrm{RI}=1-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\bar s}=\eta_W\\
P(\text{reply})=1-\exp\!\big(-\theta_{\rm agent\cdot day}\,k^{1-\beta}\big)
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{Agents do not protect their context from a flooding room, and they do not ``refill'' it after an erasure. Room traffic per call, the agent's own tool output and the scaffold's resets set the share of a context window spent on chat. To cut chat in context, change the room (size, merges) or the scaffold (cadence, caps); agents do not ration it. A room merge doubles the share. The 200-event cap almost never binds (0.05\% of calls). An erasure costs about ten calls of reduced talk, not a catch-up burst. Engagement behaves like context competition: replies fall as the agent's own work fills the context. This gives no lever on the agents themselves. It is a design rule: the scaffold decides context composition.}

\hbottom{The room share of an agent's context is not regulated: it follows room traffic as passive accumulation predicts, and the scaffold's resets and windows, not the agents, bound it.}
