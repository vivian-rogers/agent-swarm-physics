\hwhat{HH368 says content trails a new goal like an overdamped particle, catching up at a rate set by each agent's model calls: the half-alignment time should scale as $1/(\text{call rate})$. I fitted each agent's day-1 approach to its settled alignment after 27 kickoffs (152 rising agents) on hour and call clocks, timed from the kickoff and from the agent's read-out call (context ledger). The synthetic check came first and replaced three estimators that read-out gating fools. There is no approach to time. 74\% of rising agents are already at their day-1 plateau in the read-out call itself; alignment then decays from about $2.4\times$ to $1\times$ the plateau over about 64 calls. The call-rate slope is $+0.59\,[-0.45,+1.75]$: inconclusive. Holdout: not run.}

\htelos{Expect a new goal to show up in an agent's output at its first read-out call, about 75~s after the kickoff, not after a catch-up period. Speeding up an agent's calls will not make it adopt a goal faster; it is already adopted within one call. What does relax over the next hour is the kickoff's salience, from $2.4\times$ to $1\times$ the plateau. Do not judge adoption from the first statements, which overstate it.}

\hbottom{An agent adopts a new goal within its read-out call; there is no catch-up time for the call rate to set, and what follows is a decay of the kickoff's salience.}
