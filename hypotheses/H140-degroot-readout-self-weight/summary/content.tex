\hwhat{H140 asks whether an agent's next statement is a DeGroot average of its own recent content and the batch it just read, with the self-weight equal to its share of its own context window. Each row is a regime-III talk call. The statement vector is regressed on the agent's last two statements (instrumented by older ones), the read batch, the unreadable in-flight messages and the agent's well centre. HH383 predicted a self-weight slope $w_1\approx1$ in the self-share. Over 25 units the pooled slope is $-0.01$ [$-0.12$, 0.10], with synthetic power 1.00 at $w_1=0.5$: the pre-set kill fires. The read weight is sublinear, $a=0.22$ [0.13, 0.31], as H113 found.}

\hmodel{Models 11, 02 and 04: a linear vector-spin update on the call clock (Friedkin--Johnsen DeGroot). $y$: the reader's statement (DQ5 \texttt{style\_resid32}, goal and kickoff directions removed); $p$: its last two statements in the context segment; $s=\sum_{m\in B}x_m$: the read batch of size $k$; $s^F$: messages posted but not yet readable; $h$: the leave-day-out well centre. $s_{\rm self}=\mathrm{ctx\_pos}/(\mathrm{ctx\_pos}+k_{\rm ctx})$ is the agent's own share of its context. Amendment A1 (before data) adds wall-clock and batch-size nuisance terms to $w_{\rm self}$.}

\htelos{Changing what fills an agent's context does not change how much it sticks to its own recent content. An operator who wants an agent to listen more will not get it by trimming the agent's own turns from the context. Even a forced erasure leaves about 95\% of the weight on the erased content. The lever that does work is the batch: ten messages read at once carry only about $10^{0.22}\approx1.7$ times the pull of one.}

\hbottom{The self-weight does not follow the context self-share (pooled $\hat w_1=-0.01$ [$-0.12$, 0.10], 25 regime-III units, power 1.00 at 0.5); the read weight grows as $k^{0.22}$ [0.13, 0.31].}
