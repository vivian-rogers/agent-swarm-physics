\hwhat{The claim: a Gittins-style index of trap age and time since the last read-out chooses whom to nudge at least $\times2.3$ better than the logged nudger and better than H35's once-early rule. Units are idle gates, the calls after an idle call, where a nudge posted during the pause is read. A fixed-effect outcome model gives the nudge effect $\hat g(x)$ per state; policies spend the logged budget, and values are cross-fitted on held-out day halves. Replication: G51 (nudger on), G38; natives NE43, NE44. \textbf{The claim fails.} On active calls the index scores $\times1.08$ [$-0.30$, $3.44$] over the logged nudger and $\times0.69$ against once-early. The logged nudger is worse than random gate choice ($\times1.46$). The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Feedback control of a restless bandit (model 04: value of a controller's information) with a kick read at the receiving call (model 09). The state is trap age $a$, gate index $k$ and time since the last directed read $r$. The myopic index is $\hat g(x)$; the Gittins-style variant allows one nudge per trap and waits when a later gate is worth more. Rivals: flat response (R0), logged near-optimal (R1), once-early optimal (R2), selection (R3).}

\hmath{\vspace{-5pt}\begin{gather*}
\mu(M,x)=\alpha_i+b(x,z)+M\,(\theta_0+\theta_a\ln a+\theta_k\ln k+\theta_r\ln r)\\
\nu(x)=\hat g(x)=\mu(1,x)-\mu(0,x),\quad V(\pi)=\langle\hat g\rangle_{\pi},\ |\pi|=B\\
\text{once-per-trap: nudge if }\nu(x)-\lambda\ge(1-h_0)\textstyle\sum_{x'}P_{xx'}V(x')
\end{gather*}\vspace{-7pt}}

\htelos{Use a simple rule, not an index. A nudge read at an idle gate adds 7.2 [5.0, 9.8] active calls in the next 30 min. The logged nudger aims at deep traps, where the response is lowest, so random gate choice beats it $\times1.46$ [1.22, 1.67]. Nudging each trap once at its second gate beats it $\times1.55$ [1.25, 1.81], as H35 found. A fitted index of age and read-out recency adds nothing on held-out days. The stake is small: all logged nudges together bought 0.19\% of the swarm's active calls. An operator who wants more output from nudges needs a larger budget or a work-directed message, not better targeting.}

\hbottom{An index nudge policy does not beat once-early; the logged nudger is worse than random gate choice, and once-early beats it by $\times1.55$.}
