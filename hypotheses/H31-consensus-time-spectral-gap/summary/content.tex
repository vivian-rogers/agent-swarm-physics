\hwhat{H31 tests whether consensus time scales as $1/\lambda_2$, the inverse spectral gap of the exposure graph. The test uses the non-holdout periods \#10--\#44. A rule, fixed before any outcome, defines three event classes: project-share majorities (63 events, 20 periods), the \#26 votes, and content convergence. Gradual project consensus takes a median of 4.5 active h. It scales sub-linearly, $\tau\propto\lambda_2^{-0.38}$ [0.05, 0.60], not as $1/\lambda_2$. Out of sample, the slope-1 law loses to a constant; the synthetic power is 0.13. About half of all consensus is frozen at the kickoff or arrives in one-window waves. Content never converges. \textbf{Round 1b} uses context-ledger visibility, deterministic labels, the DQ4 work ledger and DQ6 ballots. It changes no card verdict. The sub-linear lead weakens: its CI touches 0 under controls. Work commits reach majorities on the same repos at least as fast (2.0 vs 4.6 h). On the correct \#26 rounds, the votes settle in 57 s and 39 s. That is two to three read-out cycles, 70--100$\times$ faster than the $\lambda_2$ forecast.}

\hmodel{The state $x_i$ of agent $i$ is its project (shared labels from artifact mentions, or work commits) or its whitened statement embedding. The coupling $W_{ij}$ counts agent $j$'s messages first seen by $i$ per active hour (round 1b: context-ledger visibility). $L$ is the symmetrized Laplacian; its spectral gap $\lambda_2$ is the control parameter. The model is model 10's voter and Potts coarsening plus graph diffusion (HH68). Four rivals run on the same reading schedule: DeGroot averaging (time-respecting gap $\gamma_{tr}$), the voter model ($\tau\propto N/u$, $u$ = reading rate), a herding wave, and a field (kick-locked timing).}

\htelos{The textbook law, consensus time $\propto 1/\lambda_2$, gives an operator no usable forecast. In broadcast rooms, $\lambda_2$ mostly measures message volume. Consensus comes in three kinds: kickoff-set, one-window waves and hours-long. For a project, expect a majority about 4.5 active h after the project first appears (80\%: 1--13.5 h). Expect the work commits to get there as fast as the chat. For a deadline-bound vote, expect consensus within two or three read-out cycles of the call to vote, i.e.\ tens of seconds. Size vote windows to the read-out delay, not to the social graph. More talk does not buy agreement in content: content alignment never converges and decays after the kickoff.}

\hbottom{Consensus does not follow $1/\lambda_2$: projects take a constant $\approx$4.5 active h, half of all consensus forms at kickoff or in one window, and votes settle at read-out speed.}

\hcaveats{The test has 31 gradual events from 13 periods and one primary test, which fails. The sub-linear slope ($p=0.07$) loses significance under a regime effect, at the block level and without the pre-NE09 periods. $\lambda_2$, message volume, $N$ and regime co-vary. The frozen/instant/gradual split and the structure decomposition are post hoc. The leave-one-period-out winner flips between the constant and $u\lambda_2^{rw}$ on 2.4\% margins; the frozen holdout rule does not change. \#26 has three rounds. NE42 is goal-confounded and does not reverse. Work consensus uses public commits, which are dense from \#30 on. Content uses one embedding model.}
