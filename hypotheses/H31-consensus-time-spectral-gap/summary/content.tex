\hwhat{Consensus events were defined by rule before outcomes and timed across non-holdout periods \#10--\#44: project-share majorities from H11's labels (63 events, 20 periods), the \#26 runoff vote, and content-alignment convergence. Their times were regressed on the exposure graph's spectral gap $\lambda_2$. Gradual project consensus (33 events, median 4.5 active h) scales sub-linearly, $\tau\propto\lambda_2^{-0.37}$ [0.08, 0.55], not as $1/\lambda_2$. The slope-1 law loses to a constant forecast out of sample (log-RMSE 1.25 vs 1.08); a free slope wins by 8\%. The synthetic run on the real schedules had already put this test's power at 0.13. Of the other events, 13 were frozen at the kickoff and 17 were one-window waves. Content never converged: it diverged after the kickoff in 12 of 35 blocks. The \#26 runoff jumped in 0.76 h (post hoc). Holdout not run.}

\hmodel{Model 10's voter and Potts coarsening, plus graph diffusion (HH68). $W_{ij}$: agent $j$'s messages first seen by $i$ (call-start visibility), per active hour. $L$ is its symmetrized Laplacian, with spectral gap $\lambda_2$. The state $x_i$ is an agent's project (H11 labels) or its whitened statement embedding. Rivals run on the same reading schedule: DeGroot averaging (time-respecting gap $\gamma_{tr}$), the voter model ($\tau\propto N/u$, $u$ = reading rate), a herding wave, and a field (kick-locked timing).}

\htelos{Mostly negative, with one usable number. A swarm operator can't use the textbook ``consensus time $\propto 1/\lambda_2$''. In chat rooms everyone sees everything, so $\lambda_2$ is mostly message volume. And consensus comes in three kinds: about a fifth is fixed by the kickoff prompt, a quarter arrives as one-window link-sharing waves, and the rest takes hours. The usable rule is a base rate: from a project's first appearance, expect a majority after about 4 active hours (80\%: 1--18 h). One lead is worth testing: rooms with poorly connected members converge more slowly (post hoc). Content alignment never converged; it decayed after the kickoff. So more talk did not buy agreement in content.}

\hbottom{Consensus in the village does not follow the $1/\lambda_2$ law: gradual project consensus scales only as $\lambda_2^{-0.37}$ and is forecast no better than by a constant ($\approx$4 active h, 80\%: 1--18 h), while half of all consensus is set by the kickoff or arrives in one-window waves.}

\hcaveats{Thirty-three gradual events from 14 periods, with one primary test that failed. The sub-linear slope ($p\approx0.04$) survives regime, period-length and $N$ controls. It does not survive the core-agent or event-window variants, dropping the pre-NE09 periods, or within-regime splits. $\lambda_2$, message volume, $N$ and regime co-vary. The split into frozen, instant and gradual events and the ``uneven exposure'' decomposition are post hoc. Labels measure attention (artifact mentions), not work. The call-start visibility rule is doubtful in regime I. The \#26 onset rule fired at the period start. Content uses one embedding model at 30-min resolution.}
