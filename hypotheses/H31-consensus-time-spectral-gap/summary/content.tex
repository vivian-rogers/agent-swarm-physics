\hwhat{Consensus events were defined by rule before outcomes and timed across non-holdout periods \#10--\#44: project-share majorities (63 events, 20 periods), \#26 votes, and content convergence. Their times were regressed on the exposure graph's spectral gap $\lambda_2$. Gradual project consensus (median 4.5 active h) scales sub-linearly, $\tau\propto\lambda_2^{-0.38}$ [0.05, 0.60], not as $1/\lambda_2$; the slope-1 law loses to a constant out of sample (synthetic power was 0.13). About half of all consensus is frozen at the kickoff or arrives in one-window waves. Content never converged. \textbf{Round 1b} (context-ledger visibility, deterministic labels, DQ4 work ledger, DQ6 ballots) changes no card verdict, weakens the sub-linear lead (CI touches 0 under controls), and shows work commits reaching majorities on the same repos at least as fast (2.0 vs 4.6 h). On the correct \#26 rounds the votes settle in 57 s and 39 s: two to three read-out cycles, 70--100$\times$ faster than the $\lambda_2$ forecast.}

\hmodel{Model 10's voter and Potts coarsening, plus graph diffusion (HH68). $W_{ij}$: agent $j$'s messages first seen by $i$ (round 1b: context-ledger visibility), per active hour. $L$ is its symmetrized Laplacian, with spectral gap $\lambda_2$. The state $x_i$ is an agent's project (shared labels from artifact mentions, or work commits) or its whitened statement embedding. Rivals run on the same reading schedule: DeGroot averaging (time-respecting gap $\gamma_{tr}$), the voter model ($\tau\propto N/u$, $u$ = reading rate), a herding wave, and a field (kick-locked timing).}

\hmath{\vspace{-8pt}
\begin{gather*}
L=D-\tfrac12\,(W+W^{\top}),\qquad 0=\mu_1<\lambda_2\le\mu_3\le\dots\\
\dot x_i=\textstyle\sum_j W_{ij}\,(x_j-x_i)\ \Rightarrow\ \tau\propto 1/\lambda_2\ \ \text{(H31)},\qquad \tau_{\rm voter}\propto N/u\\
\log\tau_e=c+b\,\log(1/\lambda_2)+\epsilon_e:\quad \hat b=0.38\ [0.05,\,0.60]\ \ \text{vs. } b=1
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{Mostly negative, with two usable numbers. The textbook ``consensus time $\propto 1/\lambda_2$'' is no use to a swarm operator: in broadcast rooms $\lambda_2$ is mostly message volume, and consensus comes in three kinds (kickoff-set, one-window waves, hours-long). For a project, expect a majority about 4.5 active hours after it first appears (80\%: 1--13.5 h), and expect the work commits to get there as fast as the chatter. For a deadline-bound vote, expect consensus within two or three read-out cycles of the call to vote: tens of seconds, not hours. So size vote windows to read-out delays, not to the social graph. Content alignment never converged; it decayed after the kickoff. More talk did not buy agreement in content.}

\hbottom{Consensus in the village does not follow the $1/\lambda_2$ law: gradual project consensus is forecast no better than by a constant ($\approx$4.5 active h) in attention and in work, half of all consensus is set by the kickoff or arrives in one-window waves, and deadline-bound votes settle at the speed of read-out.}
