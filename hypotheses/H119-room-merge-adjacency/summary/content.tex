\hwhat{HH359 says that NE42's merge, one week in which \#best and \#rest shared one room, switches the read path between the two groups on and off. A kinetic Ising coupling fitted from talk timing should follow it. It should be near 0 across the wall, positive in the merged week, back near 0 after the split, and carried by read-outs. H119 fits per-pair class couplings on minute talk spins for 11 agents over \#39, \#40 and \#41. It adds a call-clock contrast of read against in-flight messages and four other room switches. \textbf{The cross coupling does not rise:} the merge contrast is $M=-0.02$ [$-0.13$, 0.09] per pair, against a synthetic power of 0.95 at 0.3. Within-room pairs lose their coupling in the merged week as well, and regain it after the split (read minus in-flight 0.47 [0.27, 0.66]). HH359's kill rule fires. The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Model 02, kinetic Ising on talk spins with a block-structured $J$. Each pair class $c$ (within, cross) shares one per-pair coupling $J_c$, and recipient fields per (day, 30-min block) absorb the schedule and the goal. Adjacency gating predicts $J_x\approx0$ when the groups sit in different rooms and $J_x\approx J_w$ when they share one. Attention dilution predicts that per-pair coupling falls as the room grows. On the call clock, a read-gated coupling raises talk after messages read just before a call ($J^R$), not after messages posted during it and not yet read ($J^U$).}

\htelos{For an operator: merging two groups into one room did not make them couple. For a week it removed measurable talk coupling for everyone: read-gated coupling of old room-mates went from 0.29 to 0.08 to 0.47 across \#39, \#40 and \#41. To link two groups, keep rooms small and move or post across instead of merging. A read path is necessary for coupling but not sufficient: in a 14-agent room the per-pair coupling falls below what five 4-hour days resolve.}
