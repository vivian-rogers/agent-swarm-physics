\hwhat{Every hypothesis in this project fits its model inside one goal period and treats the fit as one point on a phase diagram. That assumes the period is a stationary nonequilibrium steady state. HH360 tests the assumption on the longest periods: the 17-day charity drive (\#38), predicted stationary, and the \#51 main body (45 days), predicted to drift. H120 fits a kinetic Ising model to 1-min activity and talk spins of the core agents and asks whether the first and second halves (or a linear trend) differ more than random day halves do. \textbf{\#38 is not stationary, and the HH's kill fires.} Its halves differ about twice as much as random day halves in both channels. The drift is gradual, not a step at NE17. The 8-day unit 38a shows no drift.}

\hmodel{Model 02 (kinetic Ising, logistic Glauber updates on a 1-min grid inside the DQ8 all-present window). Core agent $i$ updates with field $h_i$, couplings $J_{ij}$ (incl.\ persistence $J_{ii}$), weekday fields $\phi_{i,w}$ and a session-length field $\psi_i$, ridge 1 on $J$ in every window. Drift test: an efficient score statistic $W$ for $\theta_i(d)=\theta_i+\tau_d\delta_i$ on $(h_i,J_{i\cdot})$, from per-day gradients $g_d$ and Hessians $H_d$ at the pooled fit, calibrated by permuting days. Entropy production: the corrected held-out Newton bound on antisymmetric lagged pair and single-agent observables.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
\mathrm{logit}\,P(s_i'=1\mid\mathbf{s})=h_i+\textstyle\sum_j J_{ij}s_j+\phi_{i,w(d)}+\psi_i L_d\\
U_i=\textstyle\sum_d\tau_d\,P g_{i,d},\quad W=\sum_i U_i^\top V_i^{-1}U_i,\quad R=W_{obs}/\langle W\rangle_{\text{random day splits}}
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{For anyone fitting models to a swarm's logs, including this project: a goal period is not one steady state. Kinetic Ising parameters move by about 0.2--0.4 placebo SD per day, so a 17-day period fit averages over a walking state. Two rules follow. Fit in windows of about one week (the 8-day unit 38a passes; the 13-day unit 51g does not). Report long-period parameters as trajectories or weekly points, not as one constant. Step changes do not explain the drift: NE17 is an ordinary boundary.}

\hbottom{Inside long goal periods the kinetic Ising parameters of activity and talk drift beyond day-split placebo noise (\#38: R 1.95 and 2.18, Holm $\le$ 0.008; \#51: 2.17 and 2.14), with no step at NE17, while an 8-day unit shows no drift; entropy-production drift is not identifiable.}

\hcaveats{The drift statistic includes the fields $h_i$ as well as $J$; post hoc, $J$ alone also drifts in \#38 and \#51, and \#38 drifts before its first roster join. Non-core agents (joiners) act as an unmodelled field. Talk is underpowered in most windows, and EP drift is unpowered everywhere; the 51g EP rejection rests on negative bounds and is not read as an arrow of time. First-order Markov dynamics on a 1-min grid is assumed. Regime-I units with 4--6 agents cannot tell stationarity from drift.}
