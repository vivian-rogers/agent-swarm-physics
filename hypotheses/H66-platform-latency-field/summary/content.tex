\hwhat{After the day is trimmed to the window in which every agent runs, little co-activation is left in regime III. The question is whether a common platform-latency field explains that residual. The test uses call turnaround from the DQ1 ledger on 29 non-holdout units (regimes II and III) and Gemini's logged server time. The residual beats the trimmed block-shift null in 4/29 units. A latency field exists in 11/29 units, all in \#44 and \#51, and it grows with $N$. It is not a provider field: same-lab and cross-lab pairs co-move alike. It is not a driver either. It rises when more agents are active (28/29 units), which is the opposite of the model's sign. The pre-registered rule says mixed; a post hoc sign test and a congestion world say the field share measures load. \textbf{Refuted as posed.} The holdout is not run.}

\hmodel{Models 01 and 02. The spin $a_i(m)$ is 1 when agent $i$ logs an act call in minute $m$ of the all-present window. The latency spin $z_i(m)$ is the agent-day-standardized log turnaround of its chained calls. The field $L_{-ij}$ is the mean of $z_k$ over third parties $k\neq i,j$, with lags 0--5 min, plus an infrastructure-error count $u$. $h_i(b)$ is a 30-min block field. $E$ is the mean pair correlation of block-demeaned spins above the block-shift null. $K$ is the number of active agents.}

\hmath{\vspace{-6pt}\begin{gather*}
P(\mathbf a)\propto\exp\Big[\textstyle\sum_i\big(h_i(b)+\sum_{k=0}^{5}\gamma_{ik}L(m-k)+\kappa_i u(m)\big)a_i+\tfrac{J_0}{2N}(\sum_i a_i)^2\Big]\\
f_{\rm lat}=1-E_{\rm adj}/E,\qquad \Delta f=f_{\rm lat}-\langle f_{\rm lat}\rangle_{\rm shifted\ field}\\
\text{field drives silence: } \mathrm{corr}(L,K)<0;\qquad \text{load: } \mathrm{corr}(L,K)>0
\end{gather*}\vspace{-8pt}}

\htelos{Do not blame platform slowness for agents acting together. On the call clock, the platform's latency field is a load meter: it rises when more agents run, and it does not make them co-act. A field share from a third-party-field regression is uninformative without the sign of the field--activity relation; report both. For a scaffold builder, the useful number is the growth of turnaround co-movement with $N$: about 0 at $N\le16$, 0.02--0.10 at $N$ 17--28, and 0.2 at $N=30$. Watch it as a capacity signal. Gemini's API time is only 0.44 of a call's turnaround, and it does not co-move across agents; the shared part is the village's own execution layer.}

\hbottom{The residual co-activation after trimming is small; the platform's common latency field is real but follows activity rather than driving it.}
