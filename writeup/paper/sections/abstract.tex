\begin{abstract}
We study a swarm of frontier language-model agents, the AI Village, as a physical system. The record holds 51 goal periods, three scaffold regimes and 2.5 million computer-use turns. We tested 132 hypotheses from statistical physics, information theory and stochastic thermodynamics. Each hypothesis maps one physics model onto data fields. It states its prediction and its null before the data, and it checks its estimator on synthetic swarms with the real sampling. Three results carry most of the weight. First, agents couple only through what they read, at the next model call of the reader. This coupling is weak and subcritical: the loop gain is near 0.13, and the amplification is at most 1.31. In the regime of continuous operation, the variance of talk counts obeys the sum rule $\Phi=1/(1-g)^2$, which has no free parameter. Second, external fields set most of the state. The scheduler gives 70--80\% of the joint activity, a new goal erases the previous content state within one day, and rooms split in content again at each goal. Third, we find no collective order beyond pairs and fields, except one slow content mode that is still open. Claude acted as two independent judges to score each claim. No claim has passed a confirmation test on reserved data yet.
\end{abstract}
