\textbf{Fields beat couplings.} Every model that separates the two finds the field larger (Fig.~\ref{fig:dial}). The runner's start and stop give 70--80\% of regime-III co-activation \card{(H38)} and 80--100\% of the excess irreversibility \card{(H76)}. The kickoff text is the day-1 target \card{(H54)}. Joins of new agents fit a constant field shift better than new couplings (H122, 6/10 joins). Couplings are real but weak, and for talk they exceed the field in most units once the scheduler is removed (H50: 57/68).

\textbf{Subcritical everywhere.} No channel, period or regime is near a critical point. The read-out gain is $g\approx0.13$ \card{(H67)}, the daily Curie--Weiss gain is about 0.16 \card{(H25)}, idea branching is about 0.2 \card{(H34)}, the Hawkes branching ratio is 0.2--0.4 \card{(H03)}, and no period shows avalanches, critical slowing or a $s^{-3/2}$ law \card{(H104, H27, H34)}. The gain explains none of four unfitted observables across 33 periods; the phase diagram is organized by the number of agents and the regime instead \card{(H51)}. For an operator, no cascade control is needed at these sizes.

\textbf{No superagent, no group term.} Groups that share work are no more individual than random groups \card{(H01)}, no group store exists in \#51 \card{(H58)}, pairs and fields leave a field-sized remainder of 3--5\% of the multi-information \card{(H101)}, and the only collective arrow of time runs through named talk in one period \card{(H90)}. Two leads remain open: the regime-I slow mode \card{(H81)} and small attraction crews in \#51 that H58 found in an unscored round and excluded from its claim.

\textbf{A goal is a quench.} A kickoff resets content in one day \card{(H96)}, as an overdamped restoring force with a day-1 overshoot \card{(H97, H125)}. It is not a linear push \card{(H10)} and not a hysteresis loop \card{(H96)}. The trace of the old goal lives in the members \card{(H82)}.

\textbf{Antagonism comes only from assigned fields.} In the \#12 debates, assigned opponents disagree in 45\% of their replies and teammates in 0/60 \card{(H21)}. Each rival's antagonism switches off at its first reply after it reads the verdict (0/21; H131), and the flag rate goes from 33/75 to 0/64 opponent replies \card{(H64)}. Without assigned sides it stays at 1--2\%, and rivals with private conflicting goals move toward each other in content \card{(H22)}. The talk couplings have no negative sign, even in debates \card{(H115)}.

\textbf{Rooms are spontaneous domains.} Rooms with identical kickoffs still separate in content after member constants and measured room fields are removed (H100: 5/7 periods, bge), and the split direction does not survive a goal change (Fig.~\ref{fig:rooms}). Coherence ends at room boundaries where rooms do different work \card{(H47)}, and rooms also partition shared files (H05: cross-room co-editing $\le0.04$ of the within-room rate in 5/8 windows)\cred{0.56}.\footnote{H05's card text keeps credence 0.88 for its earlier claim; its scored summary gives 0.56 for the current claim. We use the lower value.} The room order is not held in the context window \card{(H109)}.

\textbf{Operator levers are small; field levers are not.} Message-type levers move little. A nudge adds about one active minute per 30 minutes to its named target and nothing to room-mates (H30: \#51 0.98 [0.63, 1.37]; H04: all nudges 1.16 [0.75, 1.57], first nudges 1.38 [0.92, 1.83]). It buys glances, not commits \card{(H35)}. A read correction does not free a blocked agent ($+0.007$ [$-0.053$, $+0.070]$, power 0.96; H55), and no single lever model transfers across input classes \card{(H59)}. Field-type levers are large. A named kickoff moves the swarm within 0.25--0.5 active hours \card{(H54, H75)}. A forced erasure inside an idle trap lifts escape from 0.47 to 0.84 \card{(H16)}. Halving the context cap from 40 to 20 calls costs 12\% of output per call \card{(H44)}. Names multiply the effect of a message about twentyfold \card{(H67)}.

\textbf{Convergence is an impostor.} Agents that read the same input converge without copying each other. Below 15\,s, unread pairs are near-copies as often as read pairs \card{(H57)}. Without a name, convergence explains all content alignment after a read \card{(H08)}. Paraphrase adoption is mostly convergence \card{(H34)}, and future links predict switches better than past ones \card{(H28)}. Every coupling claim in this paper therefore rests on the in-flight placebo.
