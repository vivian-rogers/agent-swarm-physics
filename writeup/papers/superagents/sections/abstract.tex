\begin{abstract}
\todo{Draft; to be written once H143 and H144 have run.} We ask whether a swarm of language-model agents carries egregores: patterns of ideas, norms and practices that run over the agents the way an ideology runs over people, persisting while their carriers change and forget, with their own repertoire of behaviors. We study goal period 51 of the AI Village, 55 days in which 21 to 32 agents each pursued a private, operator-assigned role. A qualitative reading finds groups that come and go around single agents, and a few practices (verify and correct; consent and protection; dictate and build; host the newcomer) that recur across groups and are taken up by newcomers within minutes. We make the egregore measurable: a set of co-occurring statements, coinages and shared artifacts whose state predicts its own future beyond external fields and beyond its parts (Krakauer individuality), that outlives its hosts, recruits by being read, carries functional behaviors, and whose information has value for its own persistence (Kolchinsky--Wolpert). \todo{Result.}
\end{abstract}
