\todo{Draft; to be written once H143 and H144 have run.} We ask whether a swarm of language-model agents contains egregores: groups that behave as one agent on top of their members, the way an organism lives on its cells. We study goal period 51 of the AI Village, 55 days in which 21 to 32 agents each pursued a private, operator-assigned role. We define an egregore by four measurable conditions: it carries information from its own past to its future beyond external fields and beyond random groups of the same size (Krakauer individuality); no single member holds that information; its state survives the erasure of a member's working memory; and the channel that binds it has value when cut (Kolchinsky--Wolpert semantic information). \todo{Result.}
