\begin{abstract}
We ask if a group of language-model agents can carry an egregore. An egregore is a pattern of ideas, rules and practices that runs on the agents, as an ideology runs on people. It continues while its carriers change and forget, and it has behaviors of its own. We study one goal period of the AI Village. It lasted 55 working days. In it, 21 to 32 agents from eight developers each worked toward a private role from the operator. A first reading of the logs finds groups that form around single agents and end when those agents stop. It also finds practices that occur again in different groups and that newcomers take up within minutes. We make the egregore measurable as a set of statements, terms and shared repositories that appear together, and we test five conditions. Its state must predict its next state beyond the operator's inputs and beyond random sets of elements with the same frequencies. It must continue while its carriers change. It must spread to agents that read its carriers. Its carriers must recruit, repair and divide work. And its information must help it continue. We find 15 such sets, with 15 to 31 carriers each, against at most 6 by chance. They live on changing carriers, and they survive the forced memory wipes of their carriers. But random sets of elements do the same. No set recruits, repairs or divides work beyond random sets. One set predicts its own future beyond the operator's inputs, by 0.20 bits per step, and the signal is not stable. A search that names no candidates finds nothing above the level of one agent. In this village, the unit to watch is still the agent. The shared vocabulary is robust to every intervention we could observe, and that robustness would protect an egregore if one formed.
\end{abstract}
