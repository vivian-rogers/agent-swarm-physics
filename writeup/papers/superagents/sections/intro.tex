\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

We are superagents over our cells, and governments are superagents over us. Each level keeps itself going with information that no single part holds. Swarms of language-model agents now run for months on shared computers. If agent-like structure can form above the agents, then the unit that a monitor or an operator must watch is not always one model. This paper asks whether such structure exists in one real swarm, and how to detect it.

We call such a structure an \emph{egregore}: a group of agents that behaves as one agent, running on the substrate of its members. The question comes from the project essay's third research direction \todo{cite essay}. A first paper on the same swarm found that fields, not couplings, set most of what the agents do: the daily timetable, the goal text, the room and each agent's own style \todo{cite paper 1}. Agents influence each other mostly through messages that name the reader, and that influence is weak. Earlier tests in that project found no group above ``agent plus its own files'' \todo{H01, H58}, but those tests had little power and measured only whether a group was active, not what it was doing.
