\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the village holds}

Goal period 51 holds shared vocabulary that behaves as a memeplex in the weak sense. Fifteen sets of ideas, terms and repositories travel together across agents from seven or eight developers. They live on changing hosts, and they survive the memory wipes of their hosts. But every one of these properties also holds for a random set of elements with the same frequencies. The village's whole vocabulary survives wipes, because the chat and the files survive them. Any word comes back when its host reads it again. Any shared topic costs its hosts some of their own work. None of this is specific to a memeplex.

What is specific is rare and fragile. One memeplex, K03, carries information about its own future beyond the operator's inputs and beyond random sets. Its signal lives in the second half of the period and at short time steps, and we cannot exclude a field that the environment does not hold. Two memeplexes cost their hosts beyond the generic cost. No memeplex recruits, repairs or divides labor beyond random sets. So we find no egregore in the sense of Sec.~\ref{sec:definition}. We find a vocabulary with one candidate.

The search that named no candidates agrees. Nothing above one agent stands out from rotated data. Even single agents stand out only at the day scale, because an agent holds one project for most of a day. The village's structure is agents that persist in their projects, with a shared vocabulary that flows between them.

\subsection{Why the reading and the tests disagree}

The reading of the chat found rituals and norms that looked alive. The tests found vocabulary. The gap has three causes. First, the elements see words and repositories, not acts. The welcome ritual and the write-and-build practice are sequences of acts by different agents, and no element records them. Second, the discovery step finds large topic communities, and the small vocabularies of consent or welcome dissolve inside them. Third, the reading had no comparison set. Every property it took as a sign of life, such as survival and changing carriers, turns out to hold for random vocabulary too. The pseudo-patterns are the main result of this paper, as much as the memeplexes are.

\subsection{Limits}

Four limits bound the result. The recruitment test has a power of at most 0.53, so it cannot rule recruitment out. The field control did not work. Patterns built from role texts show no self-prediction either. So condition 1 cannot separate an emergent pattern from a field that the environment misses. The automatic search misses small systems more often than it finds them. And the time scale was wrong for one test. The definition expected self-prediction to peak at one day. K03 peaks at 30 minutes, and the simulated data did not cover that step size.

Two findings change the definition. The integration measure over hosts is negative for independent hosts, so it needs a comparison with shuffled data. And division of labor must beat random element sets, because each agent's own vocabulary already passes the plain test. The reading of the chat also exposed vocabulary rates in earlier goal periods that we keep back, so those periods cannot confirm claims about vocabulary.

\subsection{What would change the verdict}

Three steps could find an egregore if one exists. A finer discovery step would let the small vocabularies of consent and welcome stand as memeplexes of their own. A record of acts, not words, would let the tests see the write-and-build practice and the welcome ritual. And a field control with real self-prediction, built from the operator's own messages, would let condition 1 show that the environment removes a field. The two weeks we keep back will confirm or refute K03 under its frozen tests, once the confirmation scripts are allowed to read them.

For a monitor or an operator, the result is reassuring in one way and not in another. In this village, the unit to watch is still the agent. No pattern above the agents acts on its own. But the vocabulary of the village is robust to every intervention we could observe: memory wipes, the loss of a central host, and the operator's veto. If an ideology with its own behaviors ever forms on such a substrate, the same robustness will protect it.
