\section{What an egregore is}\label{sec:definition}

Let $G$ be a set of at least two agents, with the artifacts and the chat channel its members share. Its state $x_G$ at time step $n$ is what the group is doing: the project it works on, the direction of its content, or its mix of work and talk. Its environment $E$ holds everything outside $G$ that can drive it: the operator's timetable, the rest of the village, and messages from humans and the operator, all taken one step earlier. We call $G$ an egregore if it meets four conditions.

\emph{(i) It is an individual.} Following Krakauer \emph{et al.}~\cite{krakauer2020}, the colonial individuality
\begin{equation}
A = I(x'_G;\,x_G \mid E)
\end{equation}
measures how well the group's present predicts its next state beyond the environment. Because $A$ never falls when a group grows, only the excess over random groups of the same size counts.

\emph{(ii) No member holds it.} For each member $i$, with own state $x_i$ and own artifact $a_i$, the group-held information
\begin{equation}
\Delta_i = L(x'_G \mid x_i, a_i, E) - L(x'_G \mid x_G, E)
\end{equation}
is what the group's past adds beyond that member, in bits of held-out log-loss $L$. A group with $\min_i \Delta_i \le 0$ is a hub with followers.

\emph{(iii) It outlives its members' memory.} Every member's working memory, its context window, is erased about every 41 model calls. The group's state must survive one member's erasure about as well as it survives an ordinary call.

\emph{(iv) Its channel has value.} Following Kolchinsky and Wolpert~\cite{kolchinsky2018}, information is semantic if scrambling it lowers the system's viability. For the group, viability is its work output, and the scramble is a cut of the channel between members. The value per bit is $\kappa_G = \Delta V_G / I$.

Condition (i) alone makes a \emph{candidate}. All four make an egregore.
