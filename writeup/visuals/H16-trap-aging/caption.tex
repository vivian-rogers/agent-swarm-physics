\caption{Idle traps age by themselves, and one directed kick helps.
(a) Simulation: with one trap depth (Kramers) the escape probability per gate does not depend on the time already spent in the trap; with a broad spread of depths it falls with trap age. A kick that multiplies the escape odds by 1.54 shifts the aging curve up without removing the aging.
(b) G51 idle gates (22{,}797; a gate is a call after an idle call): share that start a sustained run, by trap age, for gates with a directed read (mention or own nudge, orange) and without (dark), with agent-day bootstrap 95\% bands. The gray band is the null with trap ages shuffled within each agent, which keeps each agent's escape rate and age mix; the steeper fall of the data is within-agent aging.
(c) Fixed-effect logit slopes on ln(trap age) in the four periods with powered aging, without ($\beta_{a0}$) and with ($\beta_a$) the input-starvation clock, and the input clock itself ($\beta_s$): in G51 $\beta_{a0}=-0.48$ $[-0.54,-0.41]$, $\beta_a=-0.53$ $[-0.59,-0.44]$, $\beta_s=+0.23$ $[+0.11,+0.33]$, so aging is not input starvation (gray band: memoryless). A directed kick at the gate raises the odds of acting 1.5--2.9$\times$ (G51 1.54; H16).}
