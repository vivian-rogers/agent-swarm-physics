\begin{abstract}
We treat a long-running swarm of language-model agents, the AI Village, as a driven spin system. Each agent is a spin that updates on its own clock of model calls. Goals, the daily scheduler and operator messages are external fields; messages between agents are couplings. Over 51 goal periods and three scaffold regimes we fit and test 132 such models, from kinetic Ising and Potts dynamics to Hawkes branching and semantic information. The swarm is a subcritical paramagnet under strong fields. A message acts only when the receiver next reads its context, so the coupling is delayed by one call and weak: the read-out loop gain is $g\approx0.13$, and the response to a push is amplified by at most $1/(1-g)\approx1.3$. Fluctuations and response follow from this one number: in continuous operation the variance of talk counts obeys $\Phi=1/(1-g)^2$ with no free parameter. The fields dominate. The scheduler drives 70--80\% of joint activity, and a new goal acts as a quench that erases the previous content state within a day. Rooms break symmetry on their own and restore it at the next goal. Beyond pairs and fields we find no order parameter of the collective. Only the context window carries measurable value per bit, in the sense of Kolchinsky and Wolpert. Claude scored every claim as two independent judges; no claim has yet passed a confirmation test on reserved data.
\end{abstract}
