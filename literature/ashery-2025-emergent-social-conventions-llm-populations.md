# Emergent social conventions and collective bias in LLM populations

**Citation:** Ariel Flint Ashery, Luca Maria Aiello and Andrea Baronchelli, *Science Advances* 11, eadu9368 (2025). DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.adu9368
**File:** ashery-2025-emergent-social-conventions-llm-populations.pdf
**Fields:** sociophysics, stat mech (naming game, consensus), LLM multi-agent systems

## Summary
Populations of identical LLM agents play a pairwise naming game with no global incentive and no knowledge that a population exists. A shared convention emerges spontaneously in every model tested, with the S-shaped, winner-take-all dynamics of the minimal naming game. Which name wins is strongly non-uniform even when single agents with empty memory are unbiased: a **collective bias** appears because agents respond asymmetrically to mirror-image memory states. Committed minorities flip an established convention past a critical mass that depends on the model and on whether the incumbent is the "strong" or the "weak" name. Critical masses range from 2% to 67%, and in one model the weak convention flips with no committed agents at all.

## Key formalism
- **Game:** N agents. Each step draws a random pair (homogeneous mixing, no network). Each agent picks a name from a pool of W letters, presented in an order randomized per agent and per call. Payoffs are +100 for a match and −50 for a mismatch. The memory holds the last H interactions (own choice, partner's choice, success, running score) and is empty at the start. Defaults are N = 24, W = 10, H = 5. The models are Llama-2-70b-Chat (4-bit), Llama-3-70B, Llama-3.1-70B and Claude-3.5-Sonnet, at nonzero temperature with top-K sampling.
- **Convergence:** success rate rises from ≈1/W to 1. Consensus is reached by **population round ≈15** for every model except Llama-2, which is slower. The minimal naming game, averaged over 10⁴ runs, tracks the LLM curves. Consensus is also reached at N = 200 at a comparable speed in population rounds, and at W = 26. A consensus, once reached, is absorbing (fig. S7).
- **Background scalings (standard naming-game theory, not measured here):** mean-field fixation takes t_conv ∝ N^1.5 pair interactions, i.e. ∝ N^0.5 rounds per agent. On low-dimensional lattices, coarsening is much slower.
- **Micro-rule:** win-stay (99.4%) and lose-shift (97.3%) after interaction 1, a near-deterministic minimal naming game.
- **Collective bias without individual bias (W = 2, pool {Q, M}):** first-move P(M) = 0.508 (p = 0.116). Aggregated over equally likely interaction-2 memories, P(M) is still unbiased (p = 0.110). By interaction 3, mirror memories give asymmetric responses: P(M | 1:M,Q; 2:Q,M) = 0.848 but P(Q | 1:Q,M; 2:M,Q) = 0.451. Five of the eight likely memory states favor M. Over 40 runs, consensus lands on the "strong" name far more often than 50%. With W = 26, everything converges on "A", which is also an individual bias. **The bias lives in the history-conditional response function, not in the marginal prior.**
- **Critical mass:** start from full consensus (all memories hold one name) and add a committed minority (CM) that always plays the alternative. A flip is called when 95% of the last 3N interactions succeed, within 30 population rounds (N = 48, H = 3 for Llama-3). Below the critical mass the system sits in a mixed state; above it, the whole population flips. Strong incumbents need a larger CM. Measured critical masses run from **2% (Llama-3)** to **67% (Llama-2)**, and **0% for a weak incumbent under Llama-3.1**, which switches to the strong name on its own. Comparators cited: theory 10–40%, the human experiment (Centola 2018) 25%, field observations 0.3–40%.
- **Basins:** the strong convention has a larger and deeper basin. Agents near it reach memory states that stop exploring.

## Mapping to agent swarms
- **Referent and names.** A naming game needs one referent with competing names. Village candidates: an artifact (`artifact_mentions.artifact`) and the H34 N-class markers (capitalized runs, identifiers, quoted short phrases; hashed, no text) that co-occur with its URL in `chat_core` messages; a role or procedure and its competing labels; a file or report convention. Name share per referent over time gives the norm-frequency curve.
- **Interaction.** The village is broadcast, not pairwise: one call reads every new item in its room (`context_ledger_items`: receiving call × `message_id`, `sender`, `kind`, `age_s`). A "population round" is roughly one read of one other agent's naming choice per agent, so count rounds in per-capita reads of name-bearing items, not in days. `call_windows.t_call` orders the reads.
- **Success.** A reply (`reply_pairs.parent`, p_reply ≥ 0.5) that uses the parent's name for the same referent counts as a match. Win-stay/lose-shift is then measurable per agent and family.
- **N.** The population is N(t) from `roster` (joined, left) and `rooms_timeline` (agent, room, t_start, t_end): 4 agents in 2025 and 21 at #51, comparable to the paper's N = 24. Per room it is smaller.
- **Committed minorities.** Candidates are operator and human messages that repeat a term (`kicks_classified` kind, targets); a file or repo name hardcoded in an artifact that agents re-read (`artifact_mentions` source = action, `work_commits` files); and the only continuous carrier, Gemini 2.5 Pro. NE38 (an operator reassigns one agent's role) is a single-agent zealot event.
- **Bias.** Model families (`roster.model_string`, `lab`) are the "individual LLM". The paper predicts which of two competing names wins from the families present, beyond each family's first-use preference.
- **Logged causality.** Every adoption must sit inside H41's logged light cone (entry call ≤ use), or it is co-generation.

## Candidate hypotheses
- **Naming-game fixation inside a room.** For referents with ≥ 2 competing names in one room, the leading share follows an S-curve, with symmetry breaking after ~10–20 per-capita reads of the referent. *Observable:* leading-name share vs cumulative per-capita reads (ledger), per period. *Null:* a voter model on the same read sequence: no acceleration, fixation ∝ N reads. *Impostors:* kickoff/goal field (drop names in kickoff, goal or operator text); contemporaneous convergence (count only in-cone adoptions; the share of acausal adoptions must be ≤ H41's in-room baseline).
- **Collective bias by family mix.** Which name wins depends on the families present, beyond each name's initial share and each family's first-use rate. *Observable:* the winner vs leave-period-out family preferences estimated from conditional switches (after reading the rival name), not from first uses. *Null:* neutral naming game, P(win) = initial share. *Impostor:* shared model priors in their marginal form. The paper says a flat marginal does not rule out a conditional prior, so H13/H46's style-only result cannot settle this.
- **Committed sources have a fold threshold.** A term's village adoption jumps once the share of its reads that come from committed sources (operator, human, hardcoded artifact) passes a threshold p_c, rather than rising linearly. *Observable:* agent-level use vs the committed share of exposures; a breakpoint fit against a linear one. *Null:* linear dose-response. *Impostors:* scheduler field (operator messages arrive at day edges with the runner's start); kickoff field.
- **Deep basins survive turnover (HH291).** Conventions at consensus (high leading share among carriers) before a coiner leaves persist; mixed-state ones die. *Observable:* survival after NE29 and other `roster` exits vs pre-exit consensus, with the use rate matched. *Null:* survival set by use rate alone. *Impostors:* an artifact hardcodes the term (niche, HH148), and the Gemini 2.5 Pro carrier: rerun without its uses.

## Caveats
- The paper's game has a fixed referent, a closed name pool (W ≤ 26), explicit payoffs and private pairwise memory. The village has open-ended innovation, no payoff for matching, broadcast reads, persistent artifacts, and goals that change the referents every week.
- Memory here is the last H interactions. Village agents carry consolidated memory, context windows (resets, NE41) and history search, so "memory length" is not one number.
- Populations are homogeneous and there are no rooms. Mixed-family populations and networks are left to future work, and these are exactly the village's conditions.
- Population rounds are not wall time. Map per-capita reads to rounds explicitly, or the speeds cannot be compared.
- The critical mass is model- and name-specific (2–67%), so the paper predicts no universal tipping fraction to test against.
- Agents were prompted as observers predicting a player's move. The bias may depend on this framing.
