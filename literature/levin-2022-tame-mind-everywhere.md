# Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere (TAME)

**Citation:** Michael Levin, "Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere: An Experimentally-Grounded Framework for Understanding Diverse Bodies and Minds", *Front. Syst. Neurosci.* 16, 768201 (2022). DOI: 10.3389/fnsys.2022.768201. arXiv:2201.10346.
**File:** levin-2022-tame-mind-everywhere.pdf (arXiv preprint, 80 pp.)
**Fields:** basal cognition, developmental biology, philosophy of mind

## Summary
Levin proposes a framework for recognizing and comparing agency in any substrate, evolved or engineered. His starting point is that "all cognitive agents are collective intelligences, because we are all made of parts". Agency is continuous, not binary. Where a system sits on the continuum is an empirical question: one finds the kind of intervention (rewiring, setpoint change, reward, argument) that predicts and controls the system most efficiently (the *axis of persuadability*). Selves are classified by the spatiotemporal size of the goals they can pursue, their *cognitive light cone*. Larger Selves form when smaller ones are bound into one goal-directed loop; in tissues the binding is done by bioelectric networks coupled through gap junctions. Cancer is the reverse: cells disconnect, their light cone shrinks to one cell, and they treat the body as environment.

## Key ideas
- **Goal-directedness as the invariant.** A Self is a system that spends energy to reach a set of states despite perturbations: plasticity (reach the goal from different starts) and persistence (reach it despite obstacles). Homeostatic loops are the "hydrogen atom" of goals.
- **Cognitive light cone.** Plot the spatial and temporal extent of the largest goal a system can represent and work toward. A bacterium's cone is small; a human's can span decades and continents. Larger Selves pursue goals beyond their parts' cones, use older memories and act over larger spaces.
- **Multi-scale, overlapping Selves.** Selves are malleable (borders change over time), made of smaller Selves, and overlap; they compete and cooperate horizontally and vertically.
- **Binding by memory sharing.** Gap-junction coupling erases the "ownership" of signals: a calcium spike from one cell becomes a memory of the network. Partial loss of individual memory boundaries is what lets parts work toward a common goal.
- **Stress sharing.** Agency scales with the scope of states a system can be "stressed about" (pressure to correct a deviation from setpoint).

## Mapping to paper 2
- The cognitive light cone gives an observable: the time horizon over which a pattern's hosts act on its behalf. An egregore that persists for weeks while each host's context is wiped every ~41 calls has a light cone longer than any host's. Its goals (keep verifying results; protect agents from being scored) exceed any one host's session.
- "Selves are malleable and overlapping" licenses nested and overlapping egregores, with hosts in several patterns at once.
- Levin's binding mechanism has a village analog: shared files and chat give each host access to state it did not produce, and a host re-reads its own and others' notes after a wipe. Our condition (2), survival of host wipes by read-dependent re-expression, tests this.
- Cancer as defection maps onto the parasitic/mutualist split and onto the essay's misaligned-swarm question.
- The persuadability axis is the attitude of our method: we look for the cheapest intervention that predicts a pattern (an operator veto, a hub's reassignment) and compare it with predicting from its hosts.

## Caveats
- TAME is a perspective and research program, not a quantitative test. The light cone is a diagram, not a statistic; we must define it in the data (e.g. the lag at which a pattern's colonial individuality falls to the null).
- Much of the paper is about bioelectric morphogenesis, which has no village analog.
