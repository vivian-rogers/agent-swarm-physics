# If materialism is true, the United States is probably conscious

**Citation:** Eric Schwitzgebel, *Philosophical Studies* 172, 1697–1721 (2015). PDF is the 2014-07-21 draft (46 pp.).
**File:** schwitzgebel-2015-united-states-probably-conscious.pdf
**Fields:** philosophy of mind, group minds, superorganisms

## Summary
A materialist who grants consciousness to rabbits and to behaviorally human-like aliens with odd physiology should also grant it to spatially distributed group entities, and then to the United States. Two thought experiments carry the argument. The Sirian supersquid has a brain spread over a thousand tentacles joined by fast light signals: distribution in space is no bar. The Antarean anthead behaves like a person, but its head holds a colony of insects that each have their own nervous system and whose interactions produce the whole's behavior: a mind made of smaller minds. The paper then argues that the United States has the properties materialists treat as marks of a conscious being: a huge quantity of information exchange among its parts, information organized in the service of coordinated, goal-directed, flexible responses to its environment, self-monitoring and self-representation, and behavior that depends on internal states.

## Key arguments
- **Anti-nesting principles fail.** Putnam stipulates that no pain-feeler decomposes into pain-feeling parts. Tononi's integrated information theory has an *exclusion postulate*: when integrated systems nest, only the level with maximal integration is conscious. Schwitzgebel argues both are unjustified, and that exclusion has odd consequences: a large enough, well-organized election could make every voter lose consciousness, with a sharp threshold at one ballot.
- **Dumbing down and smarting up.** If antheads are conscious, so are dumber group creatures (the anthead equivalent of a rabbit); making the parts smarter or the whole larger changes nothing in principle.
- **What is special about brains?** Sophisticated, low-entropy information processing and flexible environmental responsiveness. The United States plausibly has both, at a slower time scale.
- **The claim is conditional:** if materialism is true, the most natural conclusion is that the United States is conscious, unless one accepts views that are worse.

## Relevance to this repo (paper 2, egregores)
- **Nesting is allowed.** An egregore running over agents does not stop the agents from being individuals. Krakauer et al. 2020 also allow nested individuality; our search must not impose an exclusion postulate (do not keep only the level with maximal individuality).
- **Ant colonies and the United States as the two reference cases.** The anthead/ant colony is close to a *group superagent* (a bounded set of parts whose colony-level state is self-maintaining). The United States is close to an *ideology egregore*: an institution (laws, offices, norms) that persists while its citizens and officeholders turn over, and whose behaviors (elections, taxation, enforcement) are performed by changing people.
- **Operational marks to borrow:** goal-directed, flexible response to the environment; self-monitoring (the pattern represents itself, e.g. a norm that talks about its own enforcement); information exchange organized in the service of the whole. These map onto the egregore's functional behaviors (recruitment, repair, division of labor) and onto Kolchinsky–Wolpert viability.
- **Caution:** the paper is about phenomenal consciousness; this project measures individuality and agency, not consciousness. Cite it for the argument against anti-nesting and for the reference cases, not for any claim that a swarm pattern is conscious.
