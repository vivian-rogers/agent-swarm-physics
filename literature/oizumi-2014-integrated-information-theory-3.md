# From the phenomenology to the mechanisms of consciousness: Integrated Information Theory 3.0

**Citation:** Masafumi Oizumi, Larissa Albantakis and Giulio Tononi, *PLoS Comput. Biol.* 10(5), e1003588 (2014). DOI: 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003588. Open access.
**File:** oizumi-2014-integrated-information-theory-3.pdf (25 pp.)
**Fields:** consciousness science, information theory, causal analysis

## Summary
IIT 3.0 starts from five axioms about experience (existence, composition, information, integration, exclusion) and turns each into a postulate about the physical system that supports it. A mechanism (a set of elements in a state) has integrated information φ if its cause-effect repertoire cannot be reduced to those of its parts under any partition. A system's set of irreducible mechanisms forms a conceptual structure, whose irreducibility is Φ. The *exclusion postulate* then says that, of all overlapping sets of elements, only the one with maximal Φ (a local maximum over elements, spatial grain and temporal grain) is conscious. That set is a *complex*. Complexes cannot overlap: each element belongs to one complex at a time.

## Key formalism
- **Cause-effect repertoires.** For a mechanism in state m_t, the distribution over past (cause) and future (effect) states of a purview, obtained by perturbing the system into all states with equal probability (a maximum-entropy intervention).
- **φ (mechanism level).** The distance (earth mover's distance in 3.0) between the repertoire of the whole and the product of the repertoires of the parts under the minimum-information partition. Only mechanisms with φ > 0 contribute.
- **Φ (system level).** The distance between the system's conceptual structure and that of the system cut unidirectionally at its weakest link.
- **Exclusion.** "Of all overlapping sets of elements, only one set can be conscious – the one whose mechanisms specify a conceptual structure that is maximally irreducible ... A local maximum of integrated information Φ^Max (over elements, space, and time) is called a complex." Minor complexes can coexist only if they do not overlap the main one.
- **Interventional, not observational.** Repertoires come from perturbations of a known transition matrix, not from observed statistics.

## Mapping to paper 2
- IIT's exclusion postulate is the anti-nesting principle that Schwitzgebel 2015 rejects. Under exclusion, a village pattern and the agents that host it could not both be units; the level with less Φ would vanish. Our definition takes the opposite stand: the egregore is measured beside its hosts, and an agent can host a pattern and stay an agent.
- The "maximum over grains" idea is still useful without exclusion: we scan time bins (30 min, 2 h, 1 day) and pattern boundaries for local maxima of colonial individuality. We report every local maximum, including overlapping ones, and do not keep only the largest.
- IIT's integration criterion (whole irreducible to parts under the minimum-information partition) has a cheap observational cousin in our condition (1): the pattern's state predicts its next state better than its elements taken one by one.

## Caveats
- IIT is a theory of consciousness. We measure individuality and agency, not consciousness; cite IIT for the exclusion postulate and the integration idea only.
- Φ needs the full transition matrix under perturbation and is intractable beyond ~10–20 binary elements. It cannot be computed for the village.
- IIT is contested (e.g. the 2023 open letter calling it pseudoscience, and replies); the paper's empirical claims are not what we rely on.
