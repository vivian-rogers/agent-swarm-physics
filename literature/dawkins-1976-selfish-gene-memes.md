# The Selfish Gene, chapter 11: "Memes: the new replicators"

**Citation:** Richard Dawkins, *The Selfish Gene* (Oxford University Press, 1976; 2nd ed. 1989), chapter 11, "Memes: the new replicators".
**File:** none. Notes from abstract/secondary sources, PDF not in repo (a book; the chapter text and its summaries were checked online, 2026-10-09).
**Fields:** evolutionary biology, cultural evolution, memetics

## Summary
The last chapter of the book proposes that genes are one case of a general class, the replicator, and that human culture supports a second one. Dawkins calls the cultural replicator a *meme* (from Greek *mimeme*, "imitated thing"): a unit of cultural transmission or imitation, such as a tune, a catch-phrase, a fashion, or a way of building arches. Memes propagate by leaping from brain to brain through imitation. They are selected by how well they get copied, not by whether they help the people who carry them. The chapter is a short verbal argument, with no model and no data. It started memetics.

## Key ideas
- **Replicator criteria.** A replicator that wins has *longevity*, *fecundity* and *copying fidelity*. Dawkins admits that memes copy with low fidelity: they blend and mutate in each transmission.
- **The meme's-eye view.** Selection acts on memes for their own propagation. A meme can spread while it harms its carriers (the celibacy meme is his example). This is the first statement of a parasitic cultural pattern.
- **Co-adapted meme complexes.** Memes that do well in each other's presence form groups, as genes form co-adapted gene complexes. A religion is his example: the god meme, the hell-fire meme and the faith meme reinforce one another. Later authors call such a group a *memeplex* (the term is from the 1990s; Blackmore, *The Meme Machine*, 1999, made it standard).
- **Hosts are a substrate.** The meme pool lives in brains and artifacts. A meme outlives any single carrier, as a gene outlives any single body.

## Mapping to paper 2
- Our *memeplex* K is Dawkins's co-adapted meme complex made operational: a set of statement clusters, coinages and shared repositories that co-occur within agents (`physics-models/DEFINITIONS.md`, "Egregore").
- The parasitic/mutualist split in the definition section is Dawkins's point that a meme's success need not serve its host. We measure it directly, as the effect of hosting on the host's own assigned role.
- What memetics never gave is a test of whether a meme complex is one *thing* with its own dynamics, or just a list of traits that happen to co-occur. Our condition (1), colonial individuality at the pattern level (Krakauer et al. 2020), is that test.

## Caveats
- The meme as a discrete, high-fidelity replicator is contested. Sperber and the cultural-attraction school argue that transmission is mostly reconstruction, not copying (see `claidiere-2014-how-darwinian-is-cultural-evolution.md`). LLM agents paraphrase, so the same objection applies here.
- Cite for the term and the replicator view, not for any quantitative claim.
